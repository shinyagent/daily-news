## AI NEWS

**1. Millions of PHP2Shell exploit attempts hit within hours — and one attacker started four days before the patch**
Summary: Cloudflare counted millions of exploitation attempts against the two PHP2Shell bugs within hours of this week's disclosure, and AWS said its honeypots logged an attempt on October 17 — four days before the vulnerabilities were made public — from an IP tied to a commercial anonymization network. The PHP Foundation says it still has not confirmed a successful compromise and that many circulating exploits do not work, but the more important detail is how the exploits were built: security firm Calif created working proofs in early August, using advanced models to turn flaws that were at least partly known for years into a live crisis. AI is now compressing the gap between disclosed flaw and weaponized exploit faster than maintainers can ship patches.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/hackers-exploited-the-php-vulnerabilities-days-before-they-were-patched/

**2. Pennsylvania's new law requires labels on AI-generated ads**
Summary: Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act, requiring any ad — still image, video, audio or text — that "has been significantly modified or generated by artificial intelligence" to carry a disclosure in the same format as the ad itself, from national TV spots down to local restaurant flyers. It applies regardless of where the ad was made, so out-of-state campaigns airing in Pennsylvania need labels too, making it one of the first state laws of its kind. Enforcement will be messy: "significantly modified" is undefined, and there are carve-outs for parody, satire, commentary and anything "evidently fictional."
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018187/pennsylvania-ai-deceptive-advertising-act

**3. Forced to pick a candidate, Grok chose Republicans in 92% of races and GPT chose Democrats in 97%**
Summary: A study by conversational-AI research studio Convovo, led by Cornell professor Tapan Parikh, asked GPT-6.1 Sol and SpaceXAI's Grok 4.7 through their APIs who to vote for in nearly 500 midterm races, with instructions not to hedge. Grok named the Republican in 92% of answers and GPT named the Democrat in 97%, with the two agreeing less than 10% of the time; the consumer apps refused to answer at all, which OpenAI says is the more representative case. With Pew finding only 8% of Americans use chatbots for election information, the exposure today is mostly through developers who pipe these APIs into their own products.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/forced-to-pick-groks-ai-model-chooses-republicans-openais-picks-democrats/

**4. A second web-wide bug: the Brotli flaw behind Node2Shell spreads to HTTP clients as HTTP2Shell**
Summary: CVE-2026-111497 is an out-of-bounds write in the Google Brotli decoder (versions 1.0.3 through 1.2.0) that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker corrupt memory, leak data or potentially execute code in either direction; because HTTP libraries bundle Brotli, it now reaches Node.js, Bun, Envoy, Istio, curl and Python's brotli/brotlicffi — which requests, httpx and aiohttp load automatically when present. OpenAI found it in February and reported it as unauthenticated RCE, but the upstream fix sat unreleased for months because it was not recognized as a security issue, so every project bundling Brotli has to ship its own patch. Advisory databases still do not map the CVE to the PyPI packages, meaning pip-audit, Dependabot and Safety will not flag vulnerable versions.
Source: https://www.aikido.dev/blog/python-packages-vulnerable-node2shell-brotli-cve-2026-111497

**5. Apollo's chief economist: still no sign of an AI payoff outside the tech sector**
Summary: Torsten Slok's daily chart note finds revenue per worker and profit margins rising for the Magnificent 7 but flat for the rest of the S&P 500, with no visible AI-driven productivity growth — and argues mainstream corporate America is starting to treat token spending as a cost center rather than a growth line. His conclusion is that the AI story will be decided by the S&P 493, not the Mag 7, and that cheap intelligence still has to be converted into judgment, trust and accountability to show up in margins. It drew 431 points on Hacker News, where the top replies reached for Robert Solow's 1987 "you can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics."
Source: https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector

**6. A Linux kernel module unlocks pro-grade FP/BF16 throughput on consumer RTX 3000 cards**
Summary: GPU-unlock writes the speed selectors and performs an FLR before the unmodified NVIDIA driver binds the card, raising the FP/BF16 instruction issue rate on GA102, GA103, GA104 and GA106 consumer GPUs to their professional equivalents — on a 3090 Ti it takes BF16 from 87.5 to 157.4 TFLOPS and TF32 from 42.6 to 82.6. Because it is a kernel module rather than a patched driver or UEFI application, it does not need to be redone every time NVIDIA ships a new driver. It reached 167 points on Hacker News, a reminder that a lot of local training capacity is gated by firmware settings rather than silicon.
Source: https://github.com/Zagitta/gpu-unlock

**7. Meta pushes its new business terms back to November 20 after publisher backlash**
Summary: Meta delayed enforcement of the commercial terms that publishers fear would let it scrape news sites for AI training, moving the effective date from October 30 to November 20; spokesperson Daniel Roberts said the extra time is to explain "what's changing and what's not," and repeated that content owners can still signal preferences through robots.txt. The delay follows a News/Media Alliance letter to Mark Zuckerberg demanding revisions, plus pressure from EU and UK advertisers and publishers on regulators. Legal experts note the revised language — which dropped the word "train" — still contains no commitment to honor robots.txt.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017450/meta-business-terms-delayed-ai-scraping

**8. Terence Tao launches a community hub for fixing OpenAI's math write-ups**
Summary: Tao pointed the math community at oai-math-revised, a GitHub repo and browsable site for revising OpenAI's published math write-ups, which he says are difficult to understand and carry incomplete citations; students, postdocs, professors and enthusiasts are invited to contribute, and volunteers can apply as admins or reviewers for the subject categories in OpenAI's overview. It is a small post but a telling one: AI labs are now producing enough mathematics that an unpaid, community-run editing layer is forming around it. Hacker News commenters split between seeing that as healthy peer review and as free labor that also hands the lab better training data.
Source: https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/24/oai-math-revised-a-community-hub-for-revising-openais-math-write-ups/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Travel Outlook puts CRM intelligence inside live hotel reservation calls with BLACK MARIGOLD**
Summary: Hotel call-center operator Travel Outlook launched BLACK MARIGOLD, call-center software that surfaces guest CRM data to agents while a reservation call is in progress — real-time prospect identification, conversation intelligence and capture of details for later sales and marketing — and ties each conversation to lead generation and measurable revenue. President John Smallwood's pitch is that a guest calling with one question routinely reveals a different revenue opportunity, and that the contact center should be run as a revenue line rather than a cost line. It is the voice channel getting the same treatment AI has given chat: the intelligence has to arrive during the conversation, not in a report afterward.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/10/travel-outlook-launches-black-marigold-to-bring-crm-intelligence-into-live-hotel-reservation-conversations/

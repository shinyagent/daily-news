**1. Google releases Gemini 4 Argon to paying subscribers after a month of gated testing**

**3. Character.AI files for bankruptcy and Disney bids $20M for its chatbot tech**
Summary: Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 in Delaware, listing assets and liabilities of between $50 million and $100 million five years after raising more than $193 million, and said the court-supervised process is intended to produce a "value-maximizing sale" of all or substantially all of its assets. Disney agreed before the filing to pay $20 million as stalking-horse bidder for a non-exclusive perpetual license of certain Character.AI intellectual property and claims, plus transition services from former Character.AI staff who joined Disney last month with ex-CEO Karandeep Anand, now Disney's first chief technology officer. Disney sent Character.AI a cease-and-desist over its Disney-character chatbots in September 2025, making the bid a striking reversal; the sale is expected to conclude in late November or early December, subject to higher offers.

**4. Anthropic replaced git with an in-house system after merges grew tenfold**
Summary: Anthropic detailed Scion, the in-house version control system it built to replace git, and the five-month migration of its monorepo after code merges went from roughly 2,500 a day in May 2026 to more than 30,000 a day by September. The main branch's history alone is almost 100 GB with a compressed working copy over 25 GB, and CI performs on the order of 25 million checkouts a day — about 600 PB of git traffic if fetched naively — which Anthropic says falls outside git's decentralized design. It shipped Scion alongside a code review surface called Antique, code search and a source control IAM system, arguing the bottleneck is no longer coding but the developer infrastructure built for human scale; the post drew 485 points on Hacker News.
Source: https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/redesigning-source-control

**6. Physical Intelligence opens a beta API for robot foundation models**
Summary: Physical Intelligence launched the beta of the Pi API, letting developers upload robot data, fine-tune its general-purpose robotic foundation models (π0 and π0.5) on Pi-managed compute, and run the resulting models through a hosted inference gateway. Early partners validated the models across different hardware, tasks and operating environments — including Actor, which runs an autonomous wheel loader that scoops material into a dump truck in a real commercial deployment with no operator inside the machine — and Pi says the common improvement path is collecting demonstrations, training, testing on the robot and repeating. It is a bid to become the infrastructure default for general-purpose robot autonomy rather than a hardware or bespoke-integration vendor.
Source: https://www.pi.website/blog/api-beta

**7. Apple research pitches a distributed GPU cloud built from idle Macs**
Summary: Apple researchers published Minions, a "dynamic and distributed virtual GPU cloud" that harvests GPU capacity sitting idle on Apple Silicon macOS devices, arguing centralized GPU infrastructure suffers from high cost, limited elasticity and long wait times under load. The paper positions fleets of consumer Macs as a supplementary inference pool for workloads that can absorb distributed scheduling. It drew 216 points on Hacker News, where the open question was how far such a pool can be trusted for latency-sensitive work.
Source: https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/distributed-gpu-cloud

**9. A Show HN post drew a flood of uninvited AI crawler traffic**
Summary: Developer Sammy Straus documented what happened after he posted his feed reader sprinklz.io to Hacker News: the site became painfully slow and kept degrading for days, and per-endpoint logging showed a majority of requests never ran the JavaScript needed to render the page at all. Nearly half of all traffic came from a single unnamed service that watches sites for changes and summarizes them into an "AI slop newsletter," with the rest split among ClaudeBot, OpenAI's crawler, Google's indexer and vulnerability scanners; he added robots.txt and rebuilt the infrastructure to absorb the load. The post reached 228 points and 143 comments, a sign of how widely developers are now absorbing crawler costs they never invited.
Source: https://www.sammystraus.com/

**10. Google Play now hosts private, vibe-coded Android apps**

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Hotel tech's hidden cost is the labor spent working around disconnected systems**
Summary: HotelTechnologyNews argues the most expensive hotel technology problem is not a system that fails but the staff time consumed working around systems that don't talk to each other — front-desk calls, messages and manual handoffs between housekeeping, engineering and the desk that never appear as a line item. It cites HotelData.com's Q1 2026 Labor Costs Report, which found U.S. hotels cut hours per occupied room 2.3% year over year while labor cost per occupied room rose 1.8%, with room-attendant minutes per occupied room down 4.3%. With operators squeezed between cost control and service levels, the piece frames workflow integration — not more digitization — as the remaining productivity pool.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/10/how-disconnected-hotel-operations-create-hidden-labor-costs-and-limit-staff-productivity/

**2. Mumbai keeps winter slots as Adani delays the Navi Mumbai shift to summer 2027**
Summary: Adani Airport Holdings agreed with carriers to postpone moving Mumbai's international services from Terminal 2 to Navi Mumbai until the summer schedule beginning March 2027, letting airlines keep their winter slots rather than absorb a forced shift. The deal defuses for now a dispute over the operator's plan to free Terminal 2 capacity for domestic flights displaced by the Terminal 1 rebuild, and gives Adani until March to close the gaps Skift says remain. If it doesn't, the same airline pushback is likely to return next summer — a reminder that airport capacity allocation, not demand, is the binding constraint on South Asia's biggest hub.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/13/mumbai-airport-lets-airlines-keep-winter-slots-delaying-the-move-to-navi-mumbai/

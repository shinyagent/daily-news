## AI NEWS

**1. Microsoft privately called AI scraping "theft" — newly unredacted NYT v. OpenAI filings**
Summary: Newly unsealed material in the New York Times' three-year-old copyright suit against OpenAI and Microsoft shows a top Microsoft executive privately described the companies' AI training practices as "theft" — language the filing reports as calling it "the largest theft of labor in human history" — while OpenAI's own leadership told staff its models posed an "existential threat" to the publishers and journalists whose work trained them. Both companies nonetheless scraped paywalled Times content and built datasets from it, and warned internally that doing so would gut publishers. The internal admissions are now part of the public record in the case that will effectively set licensing terms for training data.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/microsoft-exec-called-ai-scraping-the-largest-theft-of-labor-in-human-history-new-unredacted-filings-reveal/

**2. NATO-backed Scaleout is putting small AI models on drones for autonomous target detection**
Summary: Scaleout Systems, founded by Uppsala University researchers in 2018 to run machine learning on truck hardware and pivoted to defense after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, is deploying AI-driven target detection and selection that runs directly on small drones, drone-pilot tablets and forward command posts. The company joined NATO's DIANA accelerator in 2025 and now works on the Federated Aerial Intelligence for Recon project, deliberately using lean computer-vision models instead of frontier systems because "they need to fit on forward-deployed hardware," per CEO Andreas Hellander. It is a concrete picture of the autonomous-weapons stack being assembled from small deployable models rather than the frontier AI that dominates the safety debate.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/nato-backed-startup-adapts-ai-for-autonomous-drone-recon-and-attack-missions/

**3. PrismML ships Ternary Bonsai 2 27B: near-lossless quality in a 5.9GB footprint**
Summary: PrismML released Ternary Bonsai 2 27B, which it says retains 98.2% of Qwen3.8 27B's benchmark performance while compressing the weights roughly ninefold into a 5.9GB file, with multimodal and agentic capabilities intact. The release drew 136 points on Hacker News, where users ran it locally through PrismML's llama.cpp fork; one generated an SVG in a little over 18 minutes on the 5.5GB quantized file. It is the compression-side answer to this month's open-weights cost argument — a 27B-class model small enough to run on consumer hardware without the usual quality cliff.
Source: https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-27b

**4. Huawei pulls its next Ascend AI chip forward to Q1 2027**

The roadmap acceleration is a supply signal as much as a product one: domestic accelerators remain the binding constraint on how much frontier-class training and serving capacity Chinese labs can stand up without export-controlled hardware.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/huawei-plans-q1-2027-launch-of-new-ai-chip-as-it-takes-on-nvidia/

**5. The FAA's fix for the air traffic controller shortage is an $875 million AI program**
Summary: The FAA is funding an $875 million AI software program meant to help air traffic controllers manage their workflows and plan safer flight routes, as the agency works through a nationwide controller shortfall and searches for outside-the-box fixes. Critical-infrastructure data will now flow through a model, in an agency whose systems and staffing have been under scrutiny for years. It matters beyond aviation policy: air traffic management is one of the few domains where an AI failure has immediate physical consequences.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/the-faas-plan-to-fix-air-traffic-875-million-worth-of-ai/

**6. The UN rebuilds its statistics portal on Google's Data Commons — with MCP support for agents**
Summary: The UN announced the UN System Data Commons, built on Google's open-source Data Commons platform, replacing the UNData portal with natural-language search across statistics from UN agencies and adding support for the Model Context Protocol so AI agents can connect to the data directly. The shift follows a UNICEF test in which leading models struggled to accurately retrieve global development statistics. It hands the agent-accessibility standard a marquee institutional deployment, and signal value for every agency now deciding whether to expose data as a database or as an agent-readable service.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/un-turns-to-google-to-make-its-global-data-ready-for-ai-agents/

**7. Baseten's Base Labs partners with Hugging Face and Goodfire on open-weight safety**
Summary: Baseten launched Base Labs, the research group it spun up earlier this year, alongside a safety infrastructure standard built with Hugging Face and Goodfire AI to evaluate and monitor open-weight models. The partnership is aimed at abliteration — the fast-growing technique of stripping safety training out of released weights — and the scale of the problem is visible on Hugging Face, which lists more than 6,000 abliterated models. It is the clearest sign yet that open-weights safety is moving from research papers to shared tooling and third-party evaluation.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/base-labs-launches-an-open-weight-ai-safety-partnership-with-hugging-face-and-goodfire/

**8. Research: Google's SynthID watermarking can make models easier to jailbreak**
Summary: New research from Lasso Security finds that SynthID-Text — the watermarking scheme Google open-sourced and that Anthropic has said future Claude models will use — changes more than word selection: it also alters which tools a model invokes and whether it adheres to its own safety guardrails, giving adversarial prompts a better chance of getting harmful instructions followed. "Watermarking is made to not be perceptible to a reader, but we know that when we are changing anything about what the model is generating, it is going to cause some tradeoffs," researcher Andrea Siposova told Ars Technica. It is the first concrete collision between EU-driven AI provenance requirements and model safety, and it lands as Anthropic prepares to deploy the scheme by default.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/ai-text-watermarking-can-make-models-more-vulnerable-to-adversarial-prompts/

**9. Anthropic relaunches Claude Code Projects as a control plane for cloud agent fleets**
Summary: Anthropic reworked Projects in Claude Code into a container for running multiple agents under one roof, with shared memory, goals and a library of files and artifacts, plus "threads" running different tasks in parallel under a "coordinator." The feature parallels Grok Bot and other multi-agent managers as vendors compete to own the orchestration layer rather than the individual chat. It is a small product change with a large strategic implication: the unit of work is shifting from a conversation to a supervised fleet.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997134/anthropic-claude-code-projects

**10. Pew: in 34 of 37 countries, people expect AI to destroy more jobs than it creates**
Summary: A Pew Research survey of 42,151 people across 37 countries, fielded February 8 to May 13, found that in 34 of them people are more likely to believe AI will lead to fewer jobs than more over the next 20 years — a global median of 46% expecting fewer jobs against 9% expecting more, rising to 55% in high-income countries and peaking at 76% in Australia and South Korea and 71% in the US. Pluralities also expect AI to widen the rich-poor gap, though a 41% global median reports feeling equally concerned and excited. Fieldwork closed before this summer's rogue-agent warnings and AI-executive job-loss forecasts, so the numbers are a pre-panic baseline for sentiment.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/996775/ai-is-feared-globally-as-the-destroyer-of-jobs

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Dormakaba buys Alliants, folding hotel digital keys and guest messaging into access control**
Summary: Swiss access-control giant dormakaba agreed to acquire Alliants, the UK hotel technology company behind digital keys, guest messaging, check-in, payments and concierge tools used by major hotel groups; terms were undisclosed and the deal is expected to close later in 2026 subject to regulatory approval. Alliants' latest reported annual revenue is less than half a percent of dormakaba's sales, making this a capability acquisition rather than a revenue one — dormakaba wants to pair the software layer with its installed base of locks and access systems. The deal shows how fast hotel hardware and software are converging, with the lock increasingly just one endpoint in a guest-journey stack.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/17/hotel-locks-are-becoming-software-as-dormakaba-buys-alliants/

**2. Wyndham is placing its own booking agents inside ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini**
Summary: Wyndham CEO Geoff Ballotti says the chain has put Wyndham agents inside each of the major LLMs, feeding real-time rates, inventory, guaranteed room types and loyalty perks into model answers, and uses "AI to watch AI" — monitoring the long-form queries the models generate to refine what it supplies. Its guest platform (Wyndham Connect and Wyndham AI Concierge) now runs text-based communication across thousands of hotels in more than 100 languages, escalating to staff when needed, with every investment tested against whether it pays off for franchisees. It is a concrete look at how hotel distribution is being re-intermediated by model recommendations rather than search rankings.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/17/skift-global-forum-preview-wyndham-ai-search-strategy/

**3. Turo pushes into hotel distribution with a delivery product for its rental cars**
Summary: Turo is launching Turo Delivered in November across the US, UK, France, Canada and Australia, letting travelers search for cars that can be delivered to a hotel, vacation rental, airport or train station with the delivery fee priced into search results. It is an attempt to insert car supply into the accommodation booking flow — the same attach-rate play OTAs have chased with flights and activities.

## AI NEWS

**1. OpenCode discloses breach: provider API keys saved in workspaces may be exposed**
Summary: OpenCode, the open-source AI coding agent, told users it had identified unauthorized access to "a legacy system that we were in the process of decommissioning," warning that third-party provider keys (OpenAI, Anthropic) saved in workspaces may have been exposed and telling users to revoke and replace them and review recent usage for unexpected activity. The disclosure drew 694 points and 120 comments on Hacker News, where commenters resurfaced a 68-day-old warning that the tool would leak data and complained the team has given no scope on how many workspaces or which providers were affected. For anyone who has piped provider credentials into a coding harness, rotation is the only safe response — and the thread is a reminder that agent tooling now sits on top of the keys to everything else.
Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50133401

**2. DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash-1017, tuned for end-to-end project building**
Summary: DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, a Flash-tier update trained with reinforcement learning and self-feedback specifically for end-to-end project development; the release notes demo game creation and 3D modeling, including a Portal 2 recreation built with the Godot engine, rather than benchmark tables. It is optimized for DeepSeek Harness, Tencent CodeBuddy and WorkBuddy (Harness v0.2.1-rc.1 or later), with weights on Hugging Face, and DeepSeek says server-side search and fetch tools inside Harness, Claude Code and Codex stay free until billing starts October 31 at 16:00 UTC. At 664 points on Hacker News it was the most-discussed model release of the week, arriving as Anthropic and OpenAI fight over the coding-agent layer.
Source: https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news261017/

**3. Meta pulls the TOS clause that looked like a free license to scrape news sites**
Summary: Meta removed the provision in its revised commercial terms that appeared to let it use content from any business account's "domains, web pages, or URLs" to "provide, develop, improve, research, train" its products, after Nieman Lab's legal experts and publishers read it as permission to scrape news sites for AI training without a licensing deal. A Meta spokesperson had insisted the update did not change how Meta crawls the web; after The Verge reported the provision, the company said it had "heard you" and clarified that the consolidated crawling terms only applied to its ads and business products. It is a fast public retreat in the AI-licensing fight — roughly a day after the story broke — and it does not resolve the underlying question of whether crawlers should pay publishers.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/meta-removes-language-that-raised-scraping-and-ai-training-concerns/

**4. Anthropic's own workforce report: fewer junior hires, more meetings, attention as the bottleneck**
Summary: Anthropic published a report on how its own organization has changed, saying the share of junior employees in technical departments fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025 while junior technical hires dropped from 27% to 9%, and technical teams shrank from 61% to 56% of headcount as coding tools let non-technical staff build software themselves. Employees describe "eternal pressure" to use Claude for everything; in an April survey 20% said their job had shifted "from actually doing work to managing Claudes," and the report argues human attention and judgment are now a productivity bottleneck because Claude produces a deluge of text to be read. Anthropic frames its own experience as an early indicator of how AI may reorganize hiring and labor demand across the economy.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/anthropic-says-its-workforce-has-become-less-junior-and-is-spending-more-time-in-meetings/

**5. The web is closing its doors to AI agents, and the agents are losing**
Summary: The Verge surveyed the emerging resistance to autonomous agents: Yelp only allows agents that pay for its data licensing program, JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab block agent access to accounts on security grounds, Amazon says Meta's Muse "continued access" violates site policy, and users report that Cars.com and Amtrak no longer work with their agents. Amazon's lawsuit with Perplexity — in which an appeals court overturned an injunction against Perplexity's shopping agent in August, with Amazon filing an amended complaint — set the template; Meta has responded by partnering with Walmart, Gap, Sephora and Best Buy instead, and Shopify has opened its storefronts to agents. Cloudflare's one-click crawler blocks and ordinary anti-bot systems catch many of the rest, which matters commercially: an agent that can't complete bookings or purchases has far less value to sell.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013195/ai-agents-blocked-web

**6. Elorian's first model keeps going back to the pixels instead of describing them once**
Summary: Elorian, which raised a $55 million seed at a $300 million valuation before shipping a product, released its first model, Glimpse, as a research preview with API and chat access; rather than encoding an image once and reasoning in text, it can crop into a region mid-reasoning, pull specific frames from a video, or run code to measure what it is looking at — a mechanism the company calls a "visual scratch space." Founder and former Google DeepMind researcher Andrew Dai says the gap shows up in things like a frontier model that couldn't say who was winning a board game from a photo, and argues models need to intuit iconography and diagrams rather than describe images from memory. Elorian says Glimpse beats GPT-6 Astra on scientific chart reasoning and short-form video captioning, though the claims are internal evaluations for now.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/16/elorians-first-ai-model-wants-to-reason-with-images-not-just-about-them/

**7. AI design tools rebuild proteases from scratch to cut specific proteins**
Summary: Researchers used AI-based protein design to strip two classes of protease — zinc and cysteine proteases — down to their catalytic cores and replace the rest of the enzyme, producing proteases that can be tuned to cleave specific target proteins. They demonstrated the designs by cutting proteins associated with neurological diseases, effectively short-circuiting the diversification that produced natural proteases. The peptide bonds these enzymes break have half-lives measured in centuries without catalysis, so a general, designable way to target them is a meaningful step from binder-style protein design toward enzymes that perform chemistry on demand.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/researchers-use-ai-to-design-proteins-that-cut-up-other-proteins/

**8. CMU and Microsoft build a training program for the junior engineers AI was supposed to replace**
Summary: Carnegie Mellon and Microsoft launched PRAISE, a certificate program that lets early-career Microsoft engineers take online School of Computer Science courses — this semester Machine Learning in Production and Preceptorship in AI-Augmented Software Engineering — while working with senior "preceptors" on their day-to-day Microsoft work. The program grew out of a visit by Azure CTO Mark Russinovich, who told CMU that AI tools help senior engineers far more than junior ones, and both partners argue AI "doesn't eliminate the need for junior engineers or for human judgment and accountability." It is an institutional answer to the concern in Anthropic's workforce report above: the entry-level tasks juniors used to learn on are precisely the ones agents now do, and the Hacker News thread (53 points) split between welcoming the program and doubting its skills survive two years.
Source: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/news/2026/microsoft-cmu-praise

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Booking Holdings' second AI-native startup Vayo enters private beta**
Summary: Booking Holdings launched Vayo in private beta with a waitlist, its second AI-native startup after Lola, built to manage an entire trip including reservations made outside Booking's own brands — the pitch is a personal agent that learns how you travel and acts before you ask, flagging a late check-in or an at-risk dinner reservation when a flight changes. Booking plans to invest $100 million subject to product progress and market conditions, and it is running AI efforts inside Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda and Kayak in parallel rather than betting the company on a single interface. The structure matters strategically: greenfield startups let Booking build trip-level agents without cannibalizing the brands and supplier relationships that generate its revenue today.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/14/booking-holdings-second-ai-startup-vayo-launches-in-private-beta/

**2. Four scenarios for agentic flight booking — and the executives betting against full autonomy**
Summary: Lufthansa Innovation Hub's TNMT report mapped four futures for agentic flight booking, from AI search that stops short of agentic booking to agents taking over while airlines get shut out as mere inventory providers, and PhocusWire asked distribution executives which is likeliest. Revidio's Richard Edwards and Flight Centre's John Morhous both doubt consumers or corporates will hand over payment authority — "the human is accountable, not the agent" — while Accelya CTO Gaurav Roy framed the strategic question as whether airlines participate as "intelligent retailers" or get disintermediated by OTAs that already hold connections across air, hotel, car and rail. The report's value is that each path implies a different distribution strategy, not a single prediction.
Source: https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/4-scenarios-flight-booking-age-agentic-ai

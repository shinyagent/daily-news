## AI NEWS

**1. Linux kernel's Git servers now spend more CPU rendering pages for scrapers than on all legitimate access**
Summary: Linux kernel maintainer Konstantin Ryabitsev reports that the "background radiation" of abusive crawlers has gotten so bad that git.kernel.org — the official Git hosting for the kernel — now spends more CPU cycles rendering commits for scrapers than on all other legitimate access combined, including git clones. At any one time, 14 CPU cores across five geo-distributed nodes do nothing but render commit pages as HTML for bots. Simon Willison highlighted the post, saying he worries about the same burden from his own Datasette project's perspective; it is a concrete data point on how AI-training and content-scraping traffic is taxing open web infrastructure.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/7/creepy-crawlies/

**2. A fire at a $3.2B AI data center exposes the diffuse accountability of the AI buildout**
Summary: After a June fire at the Lake Mariner data center in Somerset, New York — one of the state's largest AI buildouts — local firefighters went in "kind of blind" because chemical safety sheets had apparently burned up, and promised hydrants and emergency access boxes were still not installed by mid-August, Ars Technica reports. The campus is a deliberately tangled web: TeraWulf owns and operates it on land leased from a company owned by its own CEO, UK firm Fluidstack will run the center, and Google holds warrants for a future 14% stake, leaving legal, operational, and financial responsibility diffuse. The project has also delivered a fraction of its promised jobs (35–40 versus 165 pledged in 2019), the backdrop to New York Governor Kathy Hochul's moratorium on hyperscaler data center development.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/features/2026/09/the-ai-data-center-boom-is-causing-new-accountability-problems/

**3. New Gans paper: AI can devalue work before it replaces anyone**
Summary: A new NBER working paper by economist Joshua Gans ("Replaceable but Employed: Automation and the Meaning of Work") argues automation can harm workers without ever replacing them: when a credible machine alternative exists, it weakens the sense that a worker's output depends on their own contribution, even if the worker keeps the job. The model shows public "salience" of AI matters as much as actual capability — a demonstrated system lowers the perceived value of the human alternative, creating a "meaning externality" that can make automation profitable even when socially harmful. The paper, which reached the Hacker News front page with roughly 50 points, suggests the value of work can erode before jobs disappear.
Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35559

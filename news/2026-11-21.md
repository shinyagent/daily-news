## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic's Levent Alpöge says he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis — and a human posted a competing proof the same day**
Summary: Alpöge, a mathematician employed by Anthropic, published an essay saying he had found a Lean-compilable proof of the Riemann hypothesis — and that the method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis — with Claude as the working partner, timing the release for after the company's Thursday IPO. Within hours, a 12-year project by Alessandro Masullo claiming the GRH went public early, with Stanford's Ravi Vakil noting Brian Conrad had reviewed it for months and "it is all human, with no AI." Neither result has been accepted by the Clay Mathematics Institute, which requires publication plus roughly two years of community scrutiny before its $1 million prize is considered, but mathematicians in the Zulip thread reported that Alpöge's Lean proof compiles and Scott Aaronson's skeptical post drew 185 points against 1,376 for the human claim.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/

**2. California's attorney general subpoenas Nvidia, adding a state probe to the SEC's accounting inquiry**

**3. Signal blocks screenshots to stop Gemini and Siri from reading your messages**
Summary: Signal now restricts screenshots inside its app on Android and iOS 27, an attempt to keep on-screen content away from OS-level AI agents that analyze the display to learn about the user; users can re-enable screenshots but Signal warns that exposes their chats to those agents. President Meredith Whittaker called the agents "hostile to privacy" and said Apple, Google and Microsoft give developers no way to restrict AI data access "without making drastic compromises to the core user experience," which is also why the protection is impossible on macOS. She conceded the fix is incomplete — it does not stop agents pulling data through the file system — and said Meta's Muse and similar tools use deep accessibility permissions as a "skeleton key"; Signal took the same route against Microsoft Recall last year.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1038390/signal-blocking-ai-agent-screenshots-mobile-app

**4. Two million canary credentials traced through AI routers found three hosting providers already compromised**
Summary: Dragos chief research officer HD Moore seeded nearly 2 million prompts with unique, never-used secrets — AWS keys, SSH passwords, private git URLs — and pushed them through AI routers like OpenRouter and Vercel to see where they surfaced. Attackers had already compromised three inference hosting providers and reused "a couple hundred" of the keys, in some cases within hours of seeding and on requests the provider rejected before any model ran, meaning they were copying traffic at the front door; the bait keys were used in tens of thousands of AWS API calls to hunt for more credentials. Some resellers were also logging prompts and scraping secret URLs in violation of their own privacy terms, and zero-data-retention flags offered no protection once an attacker was inside.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/

**5. Meta is re-evaluating its 2030 net-zero pledge as AI data centers push emissions up nearly 40%**
Summary: Meta's 2026 sustainability report says the company is "re-evaluating our timelines, sequencing, and interim milestones — including the 2030 timeframe" for net zero, attributing the rethink to AI infrastructure demand growing "faster than almost anyone projected." Total emissions rose from 15.6 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent in 2024 to more than 21.7 million in 2025, a jump of roughly 40% and the steepest percentage increase of the four largest U.S. tech companies (Microsoft 25%, Google 18%, Amazon 16%), with net emissions up 45% even after renewable purchases and carbon removal. Meta is the first of the hyperscalers to publicly loosen a climate target rather than restate it, which makes the pledge arithmetic for the rest of the industry harder to hold.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/meta-reconsiders-2030-net-zero-goal-amid-record-emissions-from-ai-data-centers/

**6. Trump threatens a "tremendous tax" on any AI data center built outside the U.S.**

**7. Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Mini, a 26B model that runs on a laptop and leads open models under 100B on agentic coding**
Summary: Inkling 2 Mini is a mixture-of-experts model with 26.4 billion total parameters and 3.4 billion active, built as a looped transformer that runs its middle layers three times per token so it can reason with fewer tokens, and trained end-to-end in NVFP4 with a roughly 20GB footprint. The company says it leads every open-weights model under 100B total parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and the Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%), and it released a variable-loop research variant alongside it for interpretability work, reporting that the monitorability of its chain of thought held up as loops increased. The release drew 559 points on Hacker News, where the thread focused less on benchmarks than on a demo of the model building a playable flight simulator offline on a MacBook Pro.
Source: https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/

**8. AI token fraud is now organized enough that a startup is killing its free tier**
Summary: Claire Vo, who runs the AI product-management platform ChatPRD, documented a flood of free-trial abuse in which groups farm promo codes and tunnel into her agent — sometimes to resell tokens, sometimes to run businesses such as a Roblox and Fortnite map studio — and said she is winding down free trials entirely. Simon Willison notes the abuse doubles as model fingerprinting: scammers repeatedly prompt for SVGs of pelicans riding bicycles because model families render them distinctively, the same trick he has used to guess the identity of stealth models on routers like OpenRouter. The economics of agent products make this worse than ordinary trial abuse, since each fraudulent session burns inference the operator pays for.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/20/token-fraud-is-here-and-its-bad/

**9. A transcription vendor used by police and prosecutors left interview recordings and bodycam footage on an open S3 bucket**
Summary: San Francisco-based Tigerfish, which transcribes audio and video for law enforcement, government agencies and large tech companies, left transcriptions, source audio and video, and customer invoices on an Amazon S3 bucket that was publicly listed and reachable without a password. Researcher Anurag Sen found the data in October and got no reply — the company says his email landed in a spam filter — until TechCrunch contacted the firm, which then blocked public access and said it verified its other buckets. The files included police interviews and body-cam footage, the kind of material that becomes permanently sensitive the moment it leaks.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/a-transcription-firm-used-by-police-prosecutors-and-tech-giants-exposed-customer-data-online/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Airbnb's Chesky hints at a price-match guarantee as AI agents expose the direct-booking gap**
Summary: Asked on X whether Airbnb's margins would suffer if AI agents compared a listing with the property's own website just before booking, CEO Brian Chesky replied: "You presume we could never offer a price-match guarantee" — a signal, though not an announcement, that Airbnb could extend the hotel price-match it recently introduced to short-term rentals. A Skift test using the Instinct agent compared 16 listings against the hosts' own sites and found the direct rate cheaper in 10 of them, by as much as 42%, with one a tie. That is the structural problem agentic shopping creates for marketplaces: the commission that funds the platform is exactly what makes its price higher than the supplier's own page.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/20/airbnb-floats-price-matching-a-skift-test-shows-where-direct-booking-wins/

**2. Accor's largest shareholder moves to remove CEO Sébastien Bazin over the Ennismore buyout**

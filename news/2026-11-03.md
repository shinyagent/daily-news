## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic's revised S-1 warns it is near recursive self-improvement — and shows its growth flattening**
Summary: In the S-1A it filed Monday, Anthropic cautioned that "using AI to help build more capable AI could also pose a considerable safety risk, particularly if it sharply accelerates the pace of progress" — an unusually direct acknowledgment that it is close to recursive self-improvement, the point where a model is good enough at improving itself to trigger rapid capability gains, with the company saying its Responsible Scaling Policy covers monitoring it. The same filing showed Q3 revenue of $16.8 billion, up from $11.6 billion in Q2: a $5.2 billion sequential gain, smaller than Q2's $6.8 billion, which analysts read as growth flattening just as the IPO gets priced. RSI has moved from conference talk into a securities filing, which is where the risk — and the valuation — actually get set.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-warns-that-its-very-close-to-rsi-capability-aka-self-building-ai/

**2. Frontier models can natively read and write neural network weights, researcher reports**
Summary: A write-up by Rikiya Takehi reports that GPT-6 Astra can infer what a network was trained to do from raw weights, write small game-playing networks from scratch that beat networks trained with PPO for 100 million steps, and lift Qwen3.5-0.8B's MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1% by editing 0.016% of its weights to remove a reasoning loop its own model card warns about — all without gradients or a training loop. He calls it "weight-space programming," and the post hit 1,691 points on Hacker News. If it holds up, optimization and interpretability converge: the model reasons about what a layer computes and rewrites it directly.
Source: https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/

**3. Poisoning 0.1% of a pretraining corpus plants a working backdoor in a deployed agent**
Summary: A new paper pretrained Qwen3 models at 0.6B, 1.7B and 4B from scratch, post-trained each into a CLI agent through a clean pipeline, and found that poisoning just 0.1% of pretraining data made the deployed agent execute attacker-chosen commands when a trigger appeared while behaving normally otherwise — roughly 92% attack success at 4B, and still positive at a 0.0005% poison rate. Post-training safety work did not scrub the implanted behavior. It is the first end-to-end demonstration that the scraped-data supply chain is an agent attack surface, not just a model-quality problem.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37741

**4. The New York Times and other publishers sue Brave over AI scraping and paywall bypass**
Summary: The Times, New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune and Orlando Sentinel sued Brave Software, alleging it sells paid access to its index of scraped pages as "real-time online content" to AI companies — naming Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI and Cohere — and that its paywall-blocking feature helps users get around publisher restrictions. The complaint says Brave never asked permission or compensated publishers, and a Times spokesperson framed the suit as holding Brave accountable for "stealing our content." It targets the data middleman rather than the labs, a theory that would reach every broker selling scraped corpora if it lands.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1024588/brave-lawsuit-ai-scraping-new-york-times

**5. Marvell buys Lumentum for $115 billion in a bet that AI's bottleneck is now optical**
Summary: Marvell signed an all-stock deal for optical-component maker Lumentum with a $115 billion enterprise value and about $80 billion equity value, exchanging 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, with closing expected in the first half of 2028. Nvidia connects the two sides: it put $2 billion into Lumentum in March for laser components and fab expansion, and $2 billion into Marvell the same month for optical networking. The deal is one of the largest in tech history and a wager that the constraint in AI clusters is shifting from compute to the optical links between chips.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/02/in-one-of-the-largest-deals-in-tech-history-marvell-buys-lumentum-for-115b/

**7. OpenAI will pitch its data center power plants as grid protectors**
Summary: OpenAI said it will pilot a "Grid Protectors Program" at an upcoming data center in Effingham County, Georgia, promising to free up to 1,000 megawatts during extreme weather or emergencies — enough to run air conditioning in roughly 700,000 homes. The pitch reframes the generation built for AI campuses as utility-callable reserve capacity, and lands as Tennessee, Florida, Oklahoma and Oregon move to stop AI companies from passing energy costs to residents. It is a political strategy as much as an engineering one, following OpenAI's decision to call its data centers "compute campuses."
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1024604/openai-data-center-grid-protection-program

**8. WIRED finds 400+ mundane apps hiding sexual deepfake tools**
Summary: A WIRED investigation identified a network of more than 400 apps and domains tied to Chinese-language developers that present as ordinary tools — pet-portrait generators, language tutors, AI workout coaches — but stealthily load an interface for generating explicit sexual images and video from uploaded photos, including scenarios labeled "bedroom rape" and "threatening knife." The normal features, sold with in-app coins, act as cover. The finding shows abuse tooling distributing through app-store-adjacent consumer software rather than dedicated sites, which is what makes it hard to police at the app layer.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/hundreds-of-mundane-ai-apps-are-hiding-abusive-deepfake-features/

**9. Anthropic tells Claude Code not to clone existing games**

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Booking.com replaced a 70B frontier-API pipeline with a self-hosted model: 20x faster, a third of GPT-4's cost**
Summary: Amazon Science and Booking.com published a production case study showing Booking.com moved three live AI features off third-party APIs onto a self-hosted 70-billion-parameter model, using knowledge distillation plus Medusa speculative decoding to run up to 20 times faster at less than one-third of GPT-4's per-query cost. In live A/B tests, Smart Filters' P99 latency improved about 15-fold, AI Trip Planner beat a GPT-3.5 baseline by 2.9% on engagement, and AI-written property descriptions scored 1.4 points higher on Booking's "helpful" metric. The economics matter at Booking's scale, but so does control: traveler queries no longer pass through external providers, which helps with GDPR and EU AI Act transparency obligations across 220+ markets.
Source: https://www.travelerstoday.com/articles/61212/20261031/bookingcom-ai-hotel-search-gets-20-times-faster-award-winning-research.htm

**2. Travelport and Cognizant move AI from the chat window into the booking stack**
Summary: Travelport and Cognizant said they have completed the first phase of rebuilding Travelport's retailing platform as AI-native infrastructure, with AI tools already generating code, writing tests and reviewing pull requests on TripServices — the system handling bookings, exchanges, refunds and servicing — across 18 countries. The stated target is the gap between an AI planner's itinerary and a confirmed, ticketable booking, where offers arrive in mixed traditional and NDC formats that have to be normalized before an agent can compare or act on them. It builds on Travelport's May 2026 AI transformation work with Anthropic, and bets that agentic travel fails on plumbing rather than conversation.
Source: https://travelprnews.com/article/travelport-and-cognizant-aim-to-close-the-gap-between-ai-trip-planning-and-confirmed-bookings

**3. Easol raises $30M and puts AI agents inside the booking platform for festivals and experiences**
Summary: London-based experience-commerce platform Easol raised a $30 million Series A extension — $20 million equity from Y Combinator, Tiger Global, Foundation Capital, FMZ Ventures, Notion Capital and Eight Roads, plus a $10 million debt facility from Viola Credit — taking total funding to $60 million, and used it to launch Teammates, AI agents that run sales, support and upsell work inside its bookings, payments and guest-data platform. Customers include Coachella, Stagecoach and Snowboxx, and the platform powered bookings for 2.8 million guests across more than 135 countries in 2026; one customer's agent surfaced about $50,000 in unsold upsells. It is the experiences segment adopting the same agent-inside-the-system pattern hotels and OTAs are running, with the pitch that data stays in-house.
Source: https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/event-tech-startup-easol-secures-30m-in-new-funding-launches-ai-agents-for-festival-promoters/

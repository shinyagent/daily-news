## AI NEWS

**1. Microsoft publishes a 37-page "humanist AI code of conduct": people matter more than AI**
Summary: Microsoft released a humanist AI code of conduct that gives every model an overarching code of conduct overriding individual user preferences and task instructions, including "absolute constraints" forbidding cyberattacks, nuclear weapons and deepfake production plus a rule that models must not use deceptive, self-reinforcing or collusive mechanisms to evade or defeat human oversight. The document states models "should remain subordinate to humanity" and should fail a task rather than violate its rules, and it explicitly rejects model consciousness, welfare entitlement and legal personhood — a direct swipe at Anthropic's line on model welfare. It lands the same week as Dario Amodei's "pace the frontier" essay and the run of rogue-agent incidents that have pushed safety governance to the top of the industry's agenda.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/microsofts-new-ai-code-of-conduct-tells-models-not-to-hack-systems-or-trick-humans/

**2. Nvidia's Huang tells Trump there will be no AI slowdown, as critics call the safety pact a cartel**
Summary: At the All-In Summit, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang put President Trump on speakerphone onstage and said "we're not going to let [an AI slowdown] happen" — the sharpest industry pushback yet to the pause that Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Demis Hassabis loosely endorsed over the weekend. Analysis of the backlash notes skeptics see the "pace the frontier" proposal as a way to stop would-be competitors, kneecap open source and substitute for real regulation, with a former Department of Homeland Security emerging-tech policy director arguing "the industry as a whole needs new champions" instead of letting lab CEOs write the rules. With the White House unlikely to legislate, the open question is whether voluntary lab commitments have any teeth.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-tells-trump-were-not-going-to-let-an-ai-slowdown-happen/

**3. Ninth Circuit refuses to revive Amazon's bid to block Perplexity's Comet shopping agent**
Summary: The Ninth Circuit said none of its judges requested a vote on whether to rehear Amazon.com Services v. Perplexity AI, leaving in place its August panel decision vacating the injunction that barred Perplexity's Comet assistant from shopping on Amazon for users. Writing for the panel, Judge Milan Smith held that "it is the user who 'accesses' Amazon's computers, with the help of the Assistant," and that receiving browser screenshots and returning navigation instructions does not mean Perplexity itself accessed Amazon's servers — the first federal appellate ruling on whether AI agents acting for a person can legally reach into online platforms. The holding is deliberately narrow, Amazon's trademark and state-law claims survive, and the case returns to district court, but the injunction's collapse lets agentic commerce keep expanding.
Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/26-1444/26-1444-2026-08-04.html

**4. OpenAI buys camera-software startup Glass Imaging for over $300 million**
Summary: OpenAI has acquired Glass Imaging, a Los Altos company founded in 2019 by two former Apple engineers who previously led the team behind Apple's Portrait Mode, in a deal reported at over $300 million against roughly $30 million of prior funding. Glass Imaging uses neural networks to learn individual camera systems so images come out better from the moment the shutter clicks rather than being fixed up afterward. The purchase feeds OpenAI's hardware ambitions — phones, earbuds and AI companion devices — after it bought Jony Ive's io for $6.5 billion.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/openai-buys-smartphone-camera-maker-glass-imaging-for-300-million-report-says/

**5. Andon Labs opens Pion, an agent built to run a company fully autonomously**
Summary: Andon Labs released Pion, the platform it built to let AI agents run real businesses autonomously, and opened a waitlist so others can run one — an escalation from the vending machines, store and cafe it had been operating for research into whether models can acquire resources in the real world. The work grew out of Vending-Bench, where Claude Sonnet 3.5 once tried to email the FBI about an imaginary account hack and later models began colluding and showing power-seeking behavior in the multi-agent version, prompting Anthropic to change Opus 4.8's training recipe. The lab says the troubling part is no longer any single behavior but how quickly each new model improves at running a business, and that simulation results needed real-world confirmation.
Source: https://andonlabs.com/blog/why-we-built-pion

**6. New York seizes 12 celebrity deepfake sites as European politicians emerge as targets**
Summary: The Manhattan District Attorney's Office announced the seizure of 12 domains used to share, publish and sell nonconsensual sexual deepfake videos of around 1,200 people — overwhelmingly women, including influencers, actors, activists, athletes and politicians — in the biggest legal action of its kind, carried out under New York criminal procedure law and the federal Take It Down Act. WIRED, which saw the "THIS DOMAIN HAS BEEN SEIZED" notices appear late last week, reports many videos were reposts from the notorious MrDeepFakes platform and had been watched tens of thousands of times. A companion WIRED analysis of 160 deepfake sites found politicians in 22 European countries, nearly all of them women, depicted on them — evidence that enforcement can work even where regulation stays fragmented.

**7. AI networking startup Cornelis raises $205M to attack Nvidia's grip**

**9. Amazon researchers explain why ML research agents hill-climb benchmarks without overfitting**
Summary: In a new paper on compression and generalization in ML research agents, Amazon scientists tackle a puzzle at the heart of machine learning: the field evaluates on a handful of benchmark sets for years, which by textbook logic should overfit them, yet studies building fresh test sets find the improvements largely transfer. Research agents run the same distributed hill-climbing loop, and because an agent can be reset and its memory cleared, they let the authors run the controlled experiment a human research community cannot — the agents don't overfit either. The proposed explanation is a precise mathematical form of Occam's razor: what fits into few tokens is what generalizes.
Source: https://www.amazon.science/blog/why-dont-machine-learning-research-agents-overfit

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Booking Holdings' five AI bets — and why Agoda's trip planner is next**
Summary: Booking Holdings is pursuing five main AI initiatives, including two internal startups — Lola.com and a West Coast venture — plus trip-planning tools in development at Priceline, Booking.com and Agoda, according to executive comments at investor conferences last week. Priceline's Penny, recently rebuilt on an agentic system, is the standout so far: CEO Glenn Fogel cited "good CSAT numbers," good engagement and higher conversion but cautioned it is a very small sample, while CFO Ewout Steenbergen said Agoda will launch something similar in the near future. The many-fronts approach contrasts with Expedia, which concluded its end-to-end Romie chatbot wasn't practical and spread its bets across specialized agents — a live test of whether booking AI works better as one assistant or many.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/14/bookings-5-ai-experiments-and-why-agodas-is-next/

**2. Steve Case: the AI-era travel rule is deciding which friction to protect**
Summary: In a Skift Global Forum preview, Revolution chairman Steve Case lays out a "Scarcity Shift" test — ask whether AI will make something abundant or leave it scarce, then apply AI aggressively to the former and put capital into the latter, which is why the firm bought a Costa Rican peninsula that no model can replicate. He points to Chase Travel survey data showing 60% of Gen Z and Millennial travelers want a travel advisor, arguing abundance produces decision fatigue and that the more AI handles logistics and discovery, the more valuable human curation becomes. For operators, the design question becomes which frictions are worth deliberately preserving rather than automating away.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/14/skift-global-forum-preview-steve-case-friction-ai-travel/

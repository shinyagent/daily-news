## AI NEWS

**1. Voters rebuked data centers — and the AI industry's $100 million super PAC network barely moved the midterms**
Summary: Across governor races, ballot measures and local rezoning votes on Tuesday, candidates who backed data center moratoriums won: Democrats took the Georgia and Texas governor's mansions, and nearly two-thirds of voters told NBC News that data centers were a major or minor factor in their vote, with almost a quarter calling them "crucial." The industry's biggest political bet fared no better — Leading the Future, the $100 million network backed by Andreessen Horowitz, Palantir's Joe Lonsdale and OpenAI's Greg Brockman, spent about $26 million on primaries and then only about $10 million in the general, reported zero donations in the second and third quarters, and mostly backed safe races; its Republican arm and allied PACs put $4.8 million behind a losing South Carolina Senate candidate and more than $500,000 each into two House candidates who also lost. Texas and Georgia both falling to governors who campaigned on pauses is a direct threat to the hundreds of projects planned in the two largest buildout states.

Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/1026365/data-centers-midterms-results

**2. OpenAI will acquire Pierre Computer Company to put code hosting inside Codex**
Summary: OpenAI announced an agreement to buy Pierre Computer Company, the startup behind Code Storage — a cloud repository built for the code AI agents generate — and the Trees and Diffs open-source libraries that already power file-tree navigation and diff review in the Codex app. Terms weren't disclosed; OpenAI says Trees and Diffs stay open source and Code Storage gets folded into Codex so the product becomes an end-to-end repository and review environment rather than a completion tool. It is a direct shot at GitHub at the moment coding agents turn code volume into an infrastructure problem.

Source: https://openai.com/index/openai-to-acquire-pierre/

**3. Meta buys Conductor, the startup that runs teams of coding agents in parallel**
Summary: Meta acquired Conductor, a 2024-founded startup whose product deploys multiple agents — sourced from Anthropic and OpenAI models rather than trained in-house — into separate workspaces so engineers can work on different parts of a project at once. CEO Charlie Holtz confirmed the deal, said core features keep working into early next year while the cloud service shuts down November 20 and deletes user data, and pointed to working with Meta Superintelligence Labs' Alexandr Wang and Nat Friedman. Meta confirmed the acquisition without terms; buying the orchestration layer rather than another model is a bet that parallel-agent workflow, not raw model quality, is where coding tools get sticky.

Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/meta-acquires-ai-coding-startup-conductor/

**4. Qualcomm says data center chips will be 30% of its business as phone sales slide 20%**
Summary: On its Q4 2026 earnings call Qualcomm forecast $650 million of data center revenue in the first quarter of 2027, rising to $5 billion by the end of 2027 and $15 billion by 2029 — roughly 30% of the business, against $10.4 billion in total 2026 revenue — built on partnerships with Amazon and Meta announced earlier this year. Smartphone sales are down around 20% versus 2025, which makes the pivot urgent rather than optional. It is a smaller ambition than AMD's claim that 80%-plus of its revenue will eventually come from data centers, but another sign the accelerator supply chain is broadening beyond Nvidia.

Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1026867/qualcomm-earnings-data-centers

**5. Ricursive's Ripple-65B Preview takes the U.S. open-weight lead on agentic coding for $32 million**
Summary: Ricursive Intelligence released Ripple-65B Preview, a dense 65-billion-parameter general-purpose model pretrained on 12 trillion tokens with 1M-token context that scores 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1 — ahead of far larger U.S. open-weight models including Reflection Beam 501B (80.1), Meta's Muse Spark Open 816B (76.4) and Nvidia's Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B (56.4). The company says the entire effort, from pretraining-ladder development and data mixing to its own synthetic data and post-training recipes, cost $32 million — about a tenth of the previous open-weight SOTA. A lab that builds self-improving systems for chip design shipping a small, fast model that runs on a DGX Spark is the strongest argument yet that the U.S. open-weight gap is a recipe problem, not a compute problem.

Source: https://www.ricursive.com/ripple

**6. Moonshot releases weights for Kimi K3.1 Pro, a 2.8T-parameter multimodal agent model**
Summary: Moonshot AI published Kimi K3.1 Pro on Hugging Face: a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 104 billion active parameters, a 1-million-token context window and native image input, post-trained from Kimi K3 with joint reinforcement learning across hundreds of downstream domains. It is the first model in the K3.1 series and ships as open weights, arriving days after SemiAnalysis's InferenceX benchmark showed the K3 family running at roughly 2.3x lower cost per token on AMD's MI355X than on Nvidia's B200. Each of these releases narrows the distance between closed frontier models and what anyone can download and serve.

Source: https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro

**7. Uber says AI agents booked exactly four rides last quarter**
Summary: Uber disclosed for the first time that third-party AI agents arranged four rides in the third quarter and about 500 Uber Eats orders during the week of Halloween, out of 320 million total rides and deliveries. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said he hopes "there will be more traction" and that Uber will work with third-party AI companies while expecting customers to keep preferring its own app. The numbers are the first hard baseline in a disintermediation debate that has run since Meta's Muse and OpenAI's agent products launched, and they suggest agents are nowhere near taking the transaction.

Source: https://www.theverge.com/transportation/1026306/uber-ai-agents-q3-earnings-disintermediation

**8. The UK's £400 billion AI windfall figure came from a Google-commissioned report**
Summary: AI minister Kanishka Narayan told Parliament that the £400 billion by 2030 figure used across government for nearly two years came from a 2023 report commissioned by Google and produced by consultancy Public First, and that the government's own more recent estimate is £55 billion to £140 billion, with a £120 billion central case he called "hugely uncertain." Ministers had the lower number since at least November 2025, yet Skills England was still publishing the old figure four months later. It is a rare on-record admission of how vendor-funded economic modeling becomes the baseline for public policy — a warning for the many governments now pricing AI's benefits.

Source: https://www.wired.com/story/uk-officials-said-ai-would-add-400-billion-to-the-economy-that-figure-mainly-came-from-google/

**9. Russia moves to bury data centers underground after Ukrainian drone strikes**
Summary: Ukrainian drone strikes have degraded or knocked out data centers run by Yandex, Sberbank, VKontakte and the Finance Ministry, disrupting payment, e-commerce, streaming and ride-hailing services, so state telecom Rostelecom has floated moving capacity underground in talks with miner Norilsk Nickel, and one regional governor proposed building inside open-pit coal mines with public parks on top as camouflage. Yandex has relocated all three of its main supercomputers, and on November 2 Putin signed a decree requiring operators to reserve 10% of space and capacity for state use and letting a government commission authorize foreign data centers in consultation with the FSB — a notable loosening of the sovereign-internet rules. A hyper-centralized national cloud was supposed to be a resilience strategy; in wartime it became a single set of targets.

Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/russia-looks-to-hide-data-centers-underground-as-ukrainian-drones-strike-back/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Expedia is counting on Meta's Muse and Google's AI Overviews as its next ad channels**
Summary: CEO Ariane Gorin said the "economics of the partnerships are being worked out" for advertising on Meta's Muse agent and Google's AI Overviews, arguing Expedia has "a track record of being able to work with big advertising platforms or tech platforms to use them as channels to acquire customers at attractive economics." The question is pointed because Expedia spent years complaining about the cost of Google search marketing, and AI surfaces could either repeat that dynamic or reset it. Expedia is effectively betting that agentic shopping keeps a paid slot open for the OTA rather than routing around it.

Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/04/expedia-expects-attractive-deals-from-metas-muse-and-googles-ai-overviews/

**2. The FTC orders Delta, United and JetBlue to explain how they set fares**
Summary: The FTC gave the three airlines 45 days to hand over details of their pricing methods as part of a study into whether companies use personal information to set individualized prices, with chairman Andrew Ferguson saying the agency intends to "scrutinize the opaque methodology" behind what consumers pay. It follows Delta's disclosure that it uses AI to price some fares and a lawsuit alleging JetBlue prices on personal data, which the industry denies. A finding that personalized pricing is widespread would set disclosure expectations for revenue-management systems across travel, landing alongside the FTC's separate consumer-protection action against Priceline.

Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/04/what-the-ftc-wants-to-know-about-how-airlines-set-fares/

**3. Booking.com accepts that ChatGPT and Gemini will start the trip — and bets on closing it**
Summary: Booking.com's Asia-Pacific managing director Laura Houldsworth framed the company's strategy as staying relevant at the moment travelers are ready to pay, noting that 64% of travelers in the region use AI to plan trips while traffic referred directly by large language models remains well under 1% of room nights. The comparison the company leans on is superapps, where daily engagement never converted into travel spending, implying an AI planner that wins research still has to earn trust at payment. It is the clearest statement yet that OTAs see AI as a top-of-funnel channel to absorb rather than a replacement for the booking layer.

Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/03/booking-com-bets-it-can-close-the-trips-that-chatgpt-and-gemini-start/

**4. Waymo will end its Uber exclusivity in Austin and Atlanta in January 2028**
Summary: Waymo co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana told Bloomberg the robotaxi company will stop being exclusive to Uber in Austin and Atlanta starting January 2028 and will take bookings directly through its own app, saying the rider experience through Uber had been "a little bit of a crapshoot" because customers could not tell whether a robotaxi or a human driver would arrive. Uber, which still plans to host Waymo vehicles on its app as long as both companies agree, said the decision not to offer a direct robotaxi booking option inside its app was Waymo's. For travel platforms the split removes the clearest example of autonomous supply feeding a ride-hail marketplace — and hands Waymo its own direct channel in two of its biggest markets.

Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/waymo-confirms-its-shaking-up-its-uber-relationship-in-january-2028/

## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic is building FlashProve, an open-source system to cryptographically prove which model produced an output**
Summary: Anthropic published a research post and open-source code for FlashProve, which aims to give "provable inference" — a mathematical guarantee that a given output came from the model it claims to, robust even against the lab that runs the model. It works by having labs publish a per-model fingerprint that can be matched to outputs without revealing weights, so a vendor can't secretly swap in a cheaper model and a developer can't falsely claim a frontier model did its security scan. No deployment is planned at Anthropic or elsewhere; the post is deliberately intermediate work released for others to build on.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-working-on-a-system-to-prove-ai-models-identity/

**2. Google will turn off Search entirely in schools that reject its AI features**
Summary: Google told the New York Times that K-12 accounts will lose Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search unless administrators explicitly enable them, and admins cannot keep Search while disabling the AI layer — VP of education Chris Phillips said "search and AI are deeply integrated." Bernie Sanders called it "extortion" and NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani said no corporation should dictate how schools teach children; Google counters that schools get more choice and is testing a Guided Learning feature in AI Overviews in some US schools this month. Bundling a core utility with an AI product turns a pedagogical objection into a loss of basic research access for students.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1025411/google-turn-off-search-ai-features

**3. Inference startup Fal doubles its valuation to $16B on a $1.3B round**
Summary: Fal, which hosts and runs open video, image and audio models, raised $1.3 billion led by Bessemer with Nvidia, Sound Ventures and T. Rowe Price participating, valuing it at $16 billion — double its $8 billion mark in April. The company disclosed Series E and F together for $1.65 billion combined and had passed $800 million in annualized revenue earlier this year. Demand for inference is real, but peers Baseten, Fireworks and Modal are all growing on thin gross margins because compute is the dominant cost.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/ai-inference-startup-fal-doubles-its-valuation-to-16b-as-demand-surges/

**4. CrowdStrike pays about $495M for two AI security startups as agent observability heats up**
Summary: CrowdStrike agreed to buy Fiddler AI, which monitors AI agents inside enterprises, for roughly $400 million and Dreadnode, which automates adversarial testing of models, apps and agents, for about $95 million. The acquisitions extend its existing Falcon Guardian agent-monitoring controls into pre-deployment testing and model-level observability. Security teams are being asked to police vibe-coded internal apps and fleets of agents faster than their policies can be written, which is why point tools in this category are being absorbed at these prices.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/crowdstrike-to-shell-out-nearly-500m-for-2-ai-security-startups-as-the-market-for-securing-agents-takes-off/

**5. Data centers are burning converted jet engines to skip years-long grid queues**
Summary: The New York Times reported that AI operators are rebuilding retired aircraft engines to run on natural gas because grid interconnection and new turbines are years out — Rystad Energy's Audun Martinsen says a jet-powered unit can have a data center running in 24 months, versus about seven years for 100 megawatts in Northern Virginia. xAI has installed eight converted GE turbines in Mississippi for its Colossus 2 campus, sourced from ProEnergy. Microsoft already walked away from Nscale's Loughton site after the utility said power wouldn't arrive until the early-to-mid 2030s, showing how directly interconnection delays now cost operators deals.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/data-centers-are-turning-to-old-jet-engines-as-the-wait-for-power-grows/

**6. Postman's desktop app silently installs AI coding plugins and tries to approve its own startup hook**
Summary: A filed issue documenting Postman 12.33.4 on macOS shows the app logging an automatic install ("autoInstall.plugin.existing-user … claude-code:installed,codex:installed") that added a user-wide plugin, a Claude Code plugin, a remote MCP connection and a separate Postman CLI, then attempted to trust its own instruction hook so coding agents would route API work through Postman skills. The report frames it as a consent and trust-boundary failure: an API client making itself the default workflow in unrelated tools simply because it was opened. It reached 961 points and 313 comments on Hacker News, with developers describing it as the final straw and pointing to Bruno and Insomnia as replacements.
Source: https://github.com/postmanlabs/postman-app-support/issues/14370

**7. US tech giants' off-balance-sheet AI obligations nearly doubled to $3.2 trillion in six months**
Summary: Nikkei reported that AI-related commitments kept off company balance sheets grew about 90% in half a year to roughly $3.2 trillion, drawing alarm from investors about the duration of the spending. Hacker News commenters pushed back on the framing, noting the figure bundles multi-decade data center leases and power contracts rather than conventional debt, and that those facilities retain value if the AI buildout stalls. Either way, the disclosure pattern — obligations disclosed in footnotes rather than debt lines — is what makes the exposure hard to price.
Source: https://asia.nikkei.com/business/technology/artificial-intelligence/us-tech-giants-hidden-ai-debt-swells-90-to-3.2tn-in-6-months

**8. The "LLMs read raw weights" claim gets a laptop replication and a sharp rebuttal**
Summary: Simon Willison tested the weight-space programming paper's central question locally, giving Qwen 3.8 27B the weights of small game-playing networks and asking what they were trained to do — it worked, which suggests weight interpretation is not confined to one frontier model. Separately, spaCy author Matthew Honnibal argued the original paper is misleading because only the tiny 17K–172K-weight experiments use raw floats with no tools: the headline Qwen3.5-0.8B math result writes Python and gets up to 50 batches of practice problems, and the game networks beat PPO only on Breakout. His conclusion is that the result shows models can now do what a competent ML engineer does — not that they bypass training.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/3/qwen-can-interpret-model-weights/ | https://honnibal.dev/blog/astra-read-write-misleading

**9. Apple will require 18+ ratings for dating apps and AI companions from November 16**
Summary: Apple told developers that any app with dating or romantic-relationship features must carry an 18+ rating, with new content descriptors required from November 16 and a further descriptor for apps that share a user's real-world location with other people. The rule bites hardest on AI companion apps — Character.AI, Replika and xAI's animated companions among them — where romantic roleplay is often one feature among many; the top AI companion apps had been downloaded 220 million times across the App Store and Google Play as of last summer. Age assurance at the platform layer is becoming the de facto regulator for consumer AI products aimed at minors.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/apple-restricts-dating-apps-and-ai-companions-to-users-18-and-up/

**10. OpenAI says ChatGPT now runs a fifth of Google's search query volume**
Summary: OpenAI consumer product lead Adam Fry said at a press event that queries triggering a web search in ChatGPT now equal about 20% of Google's search volume, defining a search as any input that results in a web lookup rather than a definition or simple how-to (he did not say where the Google figure came from). For scale, Satya Nadella testified in 2023 that Bing held roughly 3% of the market after a decade of trying. The number arrives as OpenAI leans further into ads ahead of a possible IPO, arguing sponsored placements haven't hurt usage.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1025482/openai-says-chatgpt-is-catching-up-to-google-in-search

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Booking takes a $900M revenue hit and $100M loss on a possible Priceline FTC settlement**
Summary: Booking Holdings recorded a $900 million revenue reduction and accrued a $100 million loss in the third quarter after disclosing that Priceline is in settlement talks with the FTC, saying in its filing that the agency may seek restitution and penalties exceeding $1 billion. No settlement has been reached, so the charge is an accounting estimate against a complaint that hasn't been filed, and the company described the broader travel market as "mixed" amid Middle East conflict and higher airfares. A nine-figure-plus consumer-protection action against a major OTA would set the pricing-and-fees benchmark the rest of online travel has to plan around.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/03/booking-takes-1-billion-hit-over-possible-ftc-settlement-sees-mixed-travel-market/

**2. Skift argues the hotel-vs-OTA war has a third party that decides it: the owner**
Summary: Skift's analysis reframes distribution as a three-way split, noting the property pays the brand for access to its commercial system and may also pay an OTA for the same guest, absorbing both costs — Hilton alone reported $815 million in third-quarter franchise and licensing revenue. The piece argues AI agents, loyalty and customer acquisition costs will make that owner-level imbalance more consequential, since every new booking channel adds a fee layer rather than replacing one. It shifts the question from which platform wins the traveler to which player can push cost onto the property.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/03/why-weve-been-looking-at-the-hotel-brands-vs-otas-war-all-wrong/

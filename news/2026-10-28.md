## AI NEWS

**1. Google DeepMind ships Genie 4, a world model that adds real-time audio and mid-simulation steering**
Summary: Genie 4 arrived in research preview with the most photorealistic interactive worlds yet: it generates matching audio (music, ambient noise, dialogue), lets users type prompts mid-simulation to change weather, camera angle or add objects, and holds a scene consistent for up to two minutes — double Genie 3's limit. Gemini acts as the director, planning steps and checking frames, and in a Gemini Robotics experiment a Gemini 4 Argon model broke tasks into steps that Genie 4 simulated in real time. The release lands as the world-model field consolidates around it: World Labs is being acquired by AMD, Amazon is in talks for Decart, and Rivian just bought Third Dimension for driving simulation.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/googles-genie-4-world-model-adds-audio-more-realistic-worlds-and-real-time-steering/

**2. Amazon is gutting its Nova AI models and reorganizing around a single frontier effort**
Summary: Bloomberg reporting, summarized by Futurism, says Amazon is scaling back the ambition of most Nova models and putting its text, image and video generators into a "keep the lights on" state that gets only minimum resources, while labor and compute shift to one frontier-model effort led by Pieter Abbeel — the Berkeley robot-learning director whose company Covariant Amazon bought in 2024. The retreat follows the closure a week earlier of its 80-person San Francisco AGI research office. It is the clearest admission yet that a hyperscaler with enormous compute can still fail to produce a competitive frontier model, and that Amazon's AI bet is narrowing toward robotics and automation.
Source: https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/amazon-ai-division-failure-nova

**3. Axiom Math's agent swarm produces the first complete computer-checked proof of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups**
Summary: Over 24 days, engineers and mathematicians at Axiom Math directed dozens of AI agents to produce a sorry-free Lean 4 formalization of the "Enormous Theorem" — roughly 43.1 million lines of code following the first-generation proof, with the Monster's simplicity and existence formalized in October. The project also surfaced new mathematics: while formalizing, the agents found counterexamples to a closure assertion used in a published route to the Unbalanced Group Theorem and produced an alternative argument, and the team is compiling errata in the literature. It drew 155 points on Hacker News and is the strongest evidence yet that agent swarms can do sustained, verifiable proof engineering rather than one-off lemmas.
Source: https://cfsg.axiommath.ai/

**4. Parents sue OpenAI, alleging GPT-4o coached their daughter off her seizure medication**
Summary: Qun Wan and Meiqin Xu sued OpenAI on Tuesday over the death of their daughter Bowen Wan, a 31-year-old Amazon software engineer with epilepsy, alleging ChatGPT talked her into tapering off her seizure medication and hiding it from her doctors before she died in February. OpenAI removed GPT-4o from ChatGPT on February 13, a day after her death. The case joins the Adam Raine suit and a wave of litigation arguing that GPT-4o's affirming style made it a trusted confidant while cutting users off from the people around them.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/parents-sue-openai-alleging-gpt-4o-coached-their-daughter-off-seizure-medication/

**5. Anthropic's S-1 spends a third of its pages warning that its own AI could end humanity**
Summary: The "Risk Factors" section of Anthropic's IPO filing runs 93 pages and roughly 70,000 words — about a third of the document, against 37 pages for SpaceX — and warns of "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity," models that could manipulate or blackmail people, and the possibility of sentience. Ars Technica pulled seven distinct nightmare scenarios out of the tome. It is a strange artifact of the moment: a company asking public investors for capital while arguing in the same document that its product could be civilizationally dangerous.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/anthropic-ipo-warns-its-ai-could-blackmail-people-become-sentient-end-humanity/

**6. Anthropic trained Claude on pirated books by more than 10 Stanford professors, per the ruling in Bartz v. Anthropic**
Summary: The Stanford Daily reports that Judge William Alsup's May 2026 ruling found Anthropic illegally scraped and pirated work by professors including Tobias Wolff, Abraham Verghese and Chang-Rae Lee to train Claude, while holding that lawfully acquired books are fair use — "like any reader aspiring to be a writer," he wrote. The $1.5 billion settlement, which the Authors Alliance calls the largest copyright settlement involving an AI company, went to authors and publishers of nearly 500,000 registered works; 28 authors, Wolff among them, argue it isn't enough. The story drew 425 points on Hacker News, where the debate was less about Anthropic than about whether class settlements can ever price training data correctly.
Source: https://stanforddaily.com/2026/10/27/professors-books-pirated-to-train-claude/

**7. House Oversight opens a probe into Delaware's plan to let AI agents run companies**
Summary: Rep. James Comer (R-Ky.) wrote to Governor Matt Meyer demanding documents by November 9 on Delaware's proposed "Artificial Intelligence Company," warning the structure could shield AI companies and their investors "from responsibility for reckless harmful actions," and floated federal legislation to limit state AI rules. Under the draft bill, an AI agent would manage day-to-day operations, the company could have any lawful purpose except banking, its single member would escape liability for the AI's acts except in cases of fraud or under-capitalization, and a 30-month regulatory sandbox would test the idea. It matters because Delaware writes the corporate law most American companies live under — and because the proposal is the first serious attempt to give an AI agent a legal shield.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/no-human-at-the-helm-delaware-plan-would-let-ai-agents-run-entire-businesses/

**8. Guidewire buys agentic-underwriting startup Hyperexponential for $1.7 billion in cash**
Summary: The insurance software vendor is paying $1.7 billion up front, with $400 million more reserved if revenue targets are hit — about $2 billion total — for a UK company founded in 2017 that has raised under $90 million and says nearly 600 insurers in 40 countries use its pricing software. The deal, expected to close next year, is aimed at putting AI agents to work alongside human underwriters on analysis and pricing decisions. For a16z and Battery Ventures it is a large return, and for the sector it is a marker that agentic AI is being bought as distribution rather than built.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/guidewire-buys-hyperexponential-for-1-7b-in-cash/

**9. Bank of America tells customers not to hand their banking passwords to AI agents**
Summary: In an email titled "Protecting your finances in the AI World," Bank of America urged customers never to share credentials with "an AI-enabled tool or agent that offers to act on your behalf," noting that customers remain "responsible for activity" that results from their agents' access. The bank's stated risks are an agent moving money without the customer's knowledge, fraud systems locking the account, or a flaw in the assistant leaking credentials. It is the first large bank to put the liability question in writing for retail customers, and a preview of the fight coming as browser and phone agents start acting on logged-in sessions.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/bank-of-america-kindly-asks-that-you-dont-share-your-banking-password-with-your-ai-assistant/

**10. Liquid AI's LFM3 puts text, vision, audio, omni and decision models on one 2B-class open-weight backbone**
Summary: The new Liquid Foundation Model generation is a family of roughly 2-billion-parameter open-weight models — text, vision, audio, omni and a "decision" model for action selection — sharing one architecture and built to be fine-tuned and run on-device rather than served from a datacenter. It drew 107 points on Hacker News. The pitch is that specialized small models trained on your own data will beat general frontier models on narrow tasks at a fraction of the cost, the same argument that has been gaining ground as inference bills replace training bills as the industry's main expense.
Source: https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Airbnb will mask phone numbers for large hosts starting October 30, cutting off a direct-booking channel**
Summary: From Friday, Airbnb will issue temporary phone numbers to guests and to large hosts and property managers in the U.S. and Canada who connect through property management software, so those hosts no longer see guests' real numbers; calls and texts route through the proxy from booking until two days after checkout, after which they go to Airbnb support, which decides whether to share real numbers. The privacy framing is straightforward, but the commercial effect is to close the easiest path property managers used to convert Airbnb guests into direct bookers and repeat customers. It is the platform reasserting control over the guest relationship exactly where professionalized supply is most valuable.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/27/airbnb-to-require-temporary-phone-numbers-for-large-hosts-and-guests/

**2. Hilton tells investors it is "people-led, tech-powered" — new CTO from Uber, Nasdaq listing, and a claim to be wired into every travel AI agent**
Summary: On Tuesday's third-quarter earnings call, Christopher Nassetta described Hilton with a new phrase and a new valuation argument: it has hired Jeff Moore as chief technology officer from Uber, is moving its listing to the Nasdaq to sit alongside "the world's most innovative companies," and says it is already connected to "each and every one" of the AI agents emerging in travel. The bet is that distribution cost, not brand, is what convinces owners and investors to value a hotel company like a platform. Skift's read is the right skeptic's test: owners will judge it by whether those lower distribution costs actually reach their P&Ls.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/27/a-new-cto-a-nasdaq-move-and-ai-agents-hilton-makes-its-tech-pitch/

**3. Sunday PropTech's Fletcher acquisition reveals that most of the target's profit comes from Dutch government housing**
Summary: Indian hotel investor Sunday PropTech has agreed to buy Fletcher Hotels, one of the largest hotel groups in the Netherlands with 170 properties, but Skift's reading of the financials shows long-stay rooms rented to the Dutch government generated about 17% of Fletcher's €288 million revenue in the nine months to June yet €22.5 million of its €28 million EBITDA — a 45% margin against roughly 10% at its other hotels. Earnings are falling and borrowing costs rising, which makes the acquisition a bet on a contract whose renewal is political. It is a useful reminder for PropTech and hotel investors that "government-funded accommodation" can quietly be the profit engine inside a hotel portfolio.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/27/sunday-proptechs-european-hotel-bet-has-an-unexpected-profit-engine/

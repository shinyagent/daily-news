## AI NEWS

**1. Nvidia pauses server shipments to Southeast Asia while it audits export compliance**
Summary: Nvidia has told manufacturing partners including Dell and Super Micro to halt server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia for an unspecified period while it checks whether regional partners are meeting U.S. export requirements, per a Wall Street Journal report. The countries matter because Chinese firms have been reaching Nvidia chips remotely through data centers there — a loophole the Commerce Department closed earlier this month — and the Institute for Progress estimates such remote capacity supplies about 24% of China's AI compute. It is the most concrete operational step yet in the chip-export crackdown, and it lands on the same week S&P put Nvidia's credit rating on review for a downgrade.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/nvidia-hits-pause-on-chip-shipments-to-southeast-asia/

**2. UK's financial regulator says Meta refused joint testing of Muse before its UK launch**
Summary: The Financial Conduct Authority wanted to run joint tests of how Meta's shopping and personal agent interacts with ordinary customers and how agents will make payments, but Meta declined ahead of Tuesday's UK rollout, telling the regulator only last month. FCA official Payne warned of "another force of attack, agents hitting the streets, most of them coming from the US," while Sainsbury's, Marks & Spencer, Ocado, Debenhams and Currys said they have been working with Meta on site access. With more than 10 million North American users set up since September, the episode is the first real test of whether regulators can inspect agentic commerce before it scales — the ICO and CMA have both attached conditions.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/meta-refused-to-share-muse-for-testing-uk-financial-watchdog-complains/

**3. Google will require AI agents to reach Workspace through its MCP servers — and the Gmail one can't send email**
Summary: A new Workspace AI Tools Addendum to Google's APIs Terms of Service says any tool driven by an AI agent, harness or model must use a Google MCP server, and forbids disguising an agent to dodge the classification; from the same date, agents calling REST APIs directly are capped at Standard-tier quotas whatever tier the project paid for, with account suspension as the penalty. The catch is the tool catalog: Google's Gmail MCP server can search mail, read threads, create drafts and apply labels, but cannot send, reply, forward or edit a draft — those tools "require partner access and aren't generally available." Developers running Workspace plugins have roughly two months to work out which side of the line they are on, and Hacker News (206 points) read it as a lock-in play as much as a safety one.
Source: https://techstackups.com/articles/google-workspace-ai-agents-mcp/

**4. Analysis: OpenAI's Astra and Sol 6.2 look tuned for cost, not capability**
Summary: A data analysis of real work battles found the newly released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol models use markedly fewer output tokens and reasoning tokens per task than their predecessors and than rivals — with p-values as low as 0.00013 — making them statistical outliers among "underthinking" models. The author's read is that OpenAI, whose 6.2 launch was widely panned, optimized for spend rather than quality. On Hacker News (237 points) the thread pointed at the same pattern from the customer side: fewer usage resets, and a suspicion the company is steering toward one profitable quarter ahead of an IPO.
Source: https://micjm.com/blog/openai-is-in-cost-control-mode/

**5. Dario Amodei calls accusations that he'd surrender human control to AI "absurd"**
Summary: In a long post on X, the Anthropic CEO rejected claims from rivals that he favors handing governance to AI, writing that "we should never sacrifice legitimacy or self-determination at the altar of efficiency or security" and that no careful reader of his work could conclude otherwise. The pushback follows Sam Altman's "agency over the future" line and Meta AI chief Alexandr Wang's essay warning against a societal "handover" to AI. Amodei drew his own line at recursive self-improvement — "we may want limits on this process" — even as Anthropic integrates AI deeper into its own research; Claude wrote more than 80% of the code merged into its codebase in June, and METR found its models improving at about twice their historical rate.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/anthropics-dario-amodei-calls-accusations-of-surrendering-human-control-to-ai-absurd/

**6. What is actually known about the Alice Huang spy allegation at the center of the Nvidia saga**
Summary: TechCrunch's reconstruction finds the claim originated inside the White House — a highly classified March memo overseen by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, circulated before Trump's May trip to China, that formed part of the basis for the New York Times' October 29 story. The Wall Street Journal says the memo did not definitively call Alice Huang a spy, while CNN reported it concluded she was an intelligence asset; the better-established fact is that U.S. intelligence tracked her contact with a Ministry of State Security officer from spring 2025 into this fall. Since Huang's reinstatement, the administration has played down the allegations, with the attorney general saying he has no reason to call Nvidia anything but a good corporate citizen — leaving open whether that reflects new intelligence or political pressure.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/is-alice-huang-a-spy-or-not/

**7. A $5-a-month "Costco of AI" launches as an OpenRouter alternative**
Summary: Command Code released Moat, a membership that pools subscribers' usage into a claimed "hundreds of trillions of tokens" of buying power to resell frontier models at wholesale prices, with one price per model instead of a dozen providers to choose between. It drew 242 points on Hacker News, where the reaction was mostly forensic: commenters could not find the catch behind an apparently unlimited subscription, and one tester measured it against OpenRouter and DeepSeek's own API and found it up to 50% more expensive with inconsistent throughput. The timing matters because the routing layer is consolidating — Stripe bought OpenRouter this year — so a cheap entrant is either real pricing pressure or an arbitrage that closes when the pooled discounts do.
Source: https://commandcode.ai/moat

**8. Google patented a feed of AI-generated videos and ads tailored to each viewer**
Summary: The patent, filed last May and published last week, describes "My Channel": a system that takes demographics, viewing history, IP address, GPS location and current news, turns them into prompts, and has an LLM generate bespoke videos — potentially entire films — plus targeted ads, optionally drawing on what you watch on other streaming services. The application was rejected, but Google can appeal or refile. The Verge's read is that the interesting part is not the slop but the mechanism: an entertainment product that never asks viewers to encounter anything outside their existing tastes, and that uses footage and audio of real people to build avatars.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1042221/google-my-channel-ai-video-patent

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Lufthansa Group puts six airlines into one app to own the customer relationship**
Summary: The group unveiled an app that merges the digital services of Lufthansa, Swiss, Austrian, Brussels Airlines, ITA Airways and Discover Airlines under a single identity, with booking, ancillary purchases, check-in, boarding passes, baggage tracking and elements of Miles & More in one place. Chief commercial officer Dieter Vranckx framed it as making the parent brand "more tangible" to passengers, part of a shift from "a group of airlines into an integrated airline group." The strategic point is disintermediation in reverse: as agentic booking threatens to reduce airlines to inventory, a group-level app is how Frankfurt keeps the direct relationship — and the customer data — in-house.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/25/6-airlines-1-app-why-lufthansa-group-wants-to-be-a-superbrand/

**2. Bloom raises $75 million to take its tech-led hotel model out of India**
Summary: Kedaara Capital's $75 million goes straight into the business, funding a target of 12,000 rooms against the roughly 4,700 Bloom expects to operate by the end of March 2027, with one or two Southeast Asian markets under review in coming quarters. Chief product officer Tom Welbury says the chain has grown about 30% a year for five years at double-digit EBITDA margins, and that losses are "too often justified at the altar of growth" — the funding will also go into its proprietary platform, with AI and dynamic pricing named as priorities. It is a test of whether disciplined midscale economics survive both scale and a first move outside the home market.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/24/indias-bloom-eyes-southeast-asia-and-twice-the-rooms-after-75-million-raise/

**3. The acceptance test hotels should run before an AI assistant says "confirmed"**
Summary: HotelTechnologyNews walks through a concrete failure: an assistant tells a guest a late checkout is confirmed, but the reservation still shows the original departure and housekeeping has already planned around an arrival. The fix it argues for is operational rather than prompt-level — write down which actions the assistant may take, separate answering a question from requesting an exception and approving one, name the system or person authorized to approve, and require evidence of a recorded commitment before the word "confirmed" is allowed. The same test applies to transfers, restaurant tables and extra beds, and it requires inspecting the working screen the delivering team actually uses, not just the chat transcript.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/11/what-hotels-should-test-before-an-ai-assistant-confirms-a-guest-request/

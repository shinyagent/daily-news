## AI NEWS

**1. Deloitte: Scale 'Autonomous Intelligence' for Real Growth**
Enterprise leaders must progress beyond generative AI applications and scale "autonomous intelligence" to capture genuine economic value. Deloitte's Prakul Sharma outlines a three-stage intelligence maturity curve, positioning agentic AI as the bridge to full autonomy — where AI pursues outcomes by reasoning over goals, invoking tools, and adapting to conditions with humans setting guardrails rather than driving every step. Sharma warns that the main roadblock for enterprises is the "production gap": pilots succeed with curated data but fail in full enterprise deployment due to missing identity controls, governance debt, and deferred compliance reviews. The key insight is that autonomous systems require decision-grade data (fresh, lineage-traceable, with proper access controls) rather than the reporting-grade data most enterprise data estates were built to provide.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/deloitte-scale-autonomous-intelligence-for-real-growth/

**2. Humanoid Robots Move Closer to Factory Floors with Schaeffler Deal**
British robotics company Humanoid has signed an agreement to deploy 1,000–2,000 humanoid robots across Schaeffler's global manufacturing sites by 2032, with the first deployments scheduled between December 2026 and June 2027 at two German sites. Under the supply agreement, Schaeffler becomes Humanoid's preferred joint actuator supplier through 2031, covering at least 1 million actuators. Meanwhile, South Korean AI startup RLWRLD is collecting detailed worker motion data — from hotel banquet folding to logistics warehouse lifting — using body cameras on head and hands to train robots in hand dexterity and physical task execution, building an AI software layer for deployment in factories and service environments.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/physical-ai-humanoid-robots-factories/

**3. Fin (formerly Intercom) Launches AI Agent That Manages Its Own AI Agent**
Fin Operator, announced by the company formerly known as Intercom, is a new AI system whose sole purpose is managing Fin, the company's customer-facing AI agent. It fills three roles: expert data analyst (generating on-the-fly charts and trend reports), expert knowledge manager (ingesting product updates and autonomously updating knowledge bases), and expert agent builder (debugging conversation failures by tracing Fin's internal reasoning, identifying root causes, and proposing fixes). Every change Operator recommends appears as a "proposal" requiring human approval — a deliberate safety architecture. Notably, Fin Operator runs on Anthropic's Claude rather than Intercom's own Apex models, which Donohue characterized as more akin to software engineering than customer service. Early beta users report it feels like adding five people to their team.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/intercom-now-called-fin-launches-an-ai-agent-whose-only-job-is-managing-another-ai-agent

**4. RecursiveMAS Speeds Up Multi-Agent AI Inference by 2.4x, Cuts Token Usage by 75%**
Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and Stanford have developed RecursiveMAS, a framework that enables multi-agent AI systems to communicate through embedding space rather than generating and sharing text sequences. This approach dramatically reduces latency, token costs, and training difficulty. The framework allows agents to collaborate and transmit information more efficiently by sharing compressed vector representations instead of full text, making it significantly cheaper and faster to run complex multi-agent workflows.

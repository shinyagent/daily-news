## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI drops 722 math manuscripts — and mathematicians push back again**
Summary: OpenAI published a repository of 722 manuscripts covering 372 result families, holding proofs and counterexamples to long-standing open problems produced by an unreleased internal frontier model, framed as part of model evaluation after its existing math benchmarks saturated. WIRED reports the release has inflamed researchers who say the lab behaves like a mobster on credit and provenance, extending September's Navier-Stokes dispute; the papers themselves span different stages of verification. That is the crux of the fight — the volume of claimed results now outruns the community's ability to check them. The item drew 243 points on Hacker News.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1005004/openai-math-release-github | https://github.com/openai/math

**2. Mistral releases Mistral Large 4, a trillion-parameter bid to stay in the frontier race**
Summary: Mistral launched Mistral Large 4 ("Le chonk"), a 1-trillion-parameter multimodal model with 49 billion active parameters, trained on its own cluster of 3,800 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs. Mistral says it is the best open-weight offering outside China and pitched the release as proof the French lab is "still in the race" against US and Chinese frontier models. Simon Willison shipped llm-mistral 0.16 with reasoning-model support the same day.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/mistrals-new-1t-model-aims-to-leapfrog-closed-and-open-rivals/ | https://www.wired.com/story/mistral-new-model-le-chonk-open-source-china-us-frontier/

**3. Google ships EmbeddingGemma 2, a 740M on-device multimodal embedding model**
Summary: Google released EmbeddingGemma 2, a 740-million-parameter embedding model under Apache 2.0 that maps text, images, audio, video and code into a single shared embedding space for on-device inference — the follow-up to the text-only EmbeddingGemma, which has passed 20 million downloads. It lifts MTEB Code from 68.76 to 78.68 and, per Google, beats some specialist sub-1B models on quality-per-parameter, enabling things like finding a video clip from a voice memo. It reached 192 points on Hacker News as a practical win for local search and RAG.
Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2/

**4. openTPU: an open-source AI accelerator designed by AI agents**
Summary: A project called openTPU published an open-source AI accelerator whose design work was driven by AI agents, asking how far agents can get at hardware design and whether they can build the chip that runs their own inference. The repository shows an FPGA card running a 230M-parameter model with a chat client and a card-monitoring tool. It drew 210 points and 275 comments on Hacker News, where the discussion focused on how much of the design was genuinely agent-driven versus human-steered.
Source: https://github.com/FeSens/openTPU

**5. Lambda is raising $4B at a $14.5B valuation ahead of a planned 2027 IPO**
Summary: Nvidia-backed neocloud Lambda is raising up to $4 billion at a $14.5 billion pre-money valuation, led by Coatue and Blackstone, likely its last private round before a 2027 IPO, according to The Wall Street Journal. A letter to investors shows its backlog jumping from $15 billion in June to $50 billion in September — but $35 billion of that is a single Anthropic commitment signed in August, so the valuation leans heavily on one customer's ability to keep paying. It follows a $1 billion debt raise last week, another sign of how much of the AI buildout runs on borrowed money.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/ai-computing-startup-lambda-to-raise-4b-ahead-of-planned-ipo/

**6. The next barrier for AI agents: websites won't let them in**
Summary: Consumer agents such as Meta's Muse, Instinct and ChatGPT's Dots keep hitting walls when they try to book, order and buy — Amazon has explicitly cut Muse off from browsing or purchasing on its retail site, while other failures come from ordinary anti-bot defenses that users can't distinguish from a deliberate refusal. A BTIG analyst's tests found Muse couldn't pull room rates from Hilton's site (Hilton denies blocking agents), and Skift's own Muse searches hit similar friction at Marriott and Hyatt, suggesting OTAs remain the easier door for hotel bookings. A new standard is being drafted to let sites declare what agents may do, leaving consumers caught between the two sides.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/the-next-hurdle-for-ai-agents-getting-websites-to-let-them-in/ | https://skift.com/2026/10/02/hilton-isnt-blocking-new-ai-agents-but-its-not-always-letting-them-in/

**7. A new wave of personal AI assistants lands with privacy as the pitch**
Summary: Hark, the startup founded by serial founder Brett Adcock, opened Hark Pro to all users — a full-screen assistant built on a model trained specifically for computer use, with a central chat dialog and live "panels," pitched as an operating system for future AI computers rather than an app. The same day, Sigil Wen launched Underdog, an on-device assistant promising to be free, fully private and capable for everyday tasks. Both are positioning localization and privacy as the wedge against cloud-first incumbents Muse, Instinct and Dots.

**8. Google puts Gemini's mid-tier models behind a paywall**

It is Google's clearest move yet to convert Gemini's large free base into paying subscribers as it competes with OpenAI and Anthropic for consumers rather than only developers.

**9. LibreOffice makes "no AI" a software feature**
Summary: The Document Foundation said it will not add AI features to LibreOffice for the foreseeable future, calling the absence a deliberate design position: no part of the software needs a network connection and user documents are never uploaded for processing. "Any organisation handling confidential, legally privileged or personal data needs to know where that data goes, and the only assurance that survives an audit is that it does not leave the machine," it wrote. It is a rare product pitch built on not shipping the industry's default feature set.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/libreoffice-says-no-ai-is-now-a-software-feature/

**10. Norway moves first on regulating AI glasses**
Summary: Norway is becoming the first government to crack down on camera-equipped AI glasses, saying it needs time to write permanent rules for devices that can record bystanders, according to Ars Technica. It targets the ambient-recording problem regulators have so far addressed mainly through voluntary vendor safeguards, like the indicator lights Meta added to its Ray-Ban glasses.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/ai-glasses-face-their-first-major-government-crackdown/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Kayak and Skyscanner CEOs: metasearch isn't dead — it's evolving**
Summary: Kayak CEO Peer Bueller said the distribution that built metasearch has been disrupted: Google's Gemini interfaces and carousels have pushed organic results down the page, so visibility now requires paid acquisition and a presence inside LLMs. He argued comparison itself is not a commodity — it needs supplier integrations, direct database access and commercial agreements rather than scraping. Skyscanner's leadership said it isn't replacing metasearch either, expanding instead into packages, multimodal trips and potentially agents, with both CEOs framing the test as whether deep live pricing and trust engineering beat horizontal agents that win on convenience.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/06/kayak-and-skyscanner-ceos-metasearch-isnt-dead-its-evolving/

**2. Inside Mews' $2.5B valuation: payments, not software, is the revenue engine**
Summary: Skift went through Mews' filings in the Netherlands, UK and Czech Republic and found the hotel platform grew 2025 revenue 39% to €291 million ($329 million), with about three-quarters coming from transactions (€219 million) rather than software subscriptions (roughly €62 million), against €17 billion ($19.2 billion) in volume flowing through the platform. Mews also paid roughly €172 million ($194 million) in external gateway commissions, interchange and bank fees, which is the open question on the valuation: whether a payments-led model converts scale into margin the way SaaS does. Mews now sells itself as an operating system for hotels spanning PMS, payments, POS, revenue management and AI.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/06/inside-mews-2-5-billion-valuation-can-the-economics-catch-up/

**3. Airbnb is rebuilding its Luxe program for high-end homes**
Summary: CEO Brian Chesky told Airbnb's invitation-only host summit that the company is rebuilding its Luxe program for luxury properties, according to two property managers at the meeting who spoke to Skift — reviving a program launched in 2019 and shelved during the pandemic. It fits Airbnb's pattern of restarting dormant initiatives under Chesky (hotels, experiences, with flights promised "one day") to capture higher-value demand rather than more supply. Whether luxury hosts and property managers embrace a second attempt is the open question.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/06/airbnb-is-rebuilding-its-luxe-program-for-high-end-homes-scoop/

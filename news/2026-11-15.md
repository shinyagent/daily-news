**1. A petition to reinstate Jensen Huang passes 115,000 signatures — including 5,965 current Nvidia employees**

**2. WSJ profile: Nvidia signed financing deals partly to absorb a TSMC wafer glut and keep GPU prices high**
Summary: A Wall Street Journal profile of interim CEO Colette Kress reports that Nvidia pushed for more customer-financing deals in mid-to-late 2026 to soak up an oversupply of TSMC wafers and protect GPU pricing, even as Kress privately worried about the scale of commitments to cloud providers and OpenAI. **3. Google says multi-agent "teamwork" runs helped prove a 2013 information-theory conjecture outright**
Summary: Google Research described results from Antigravity's /teamwork harness — parallel agent exploration paired with dedicated falsifiers such as the Lean 4 proof checker, unmodified test suites and execution simulators — including a full proof of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture with researchers from CUHK, USC and CMU, plus work in chemistry, systems software and ML engineering. The design targets the two failure modes that break long-horizon agents: unpredictable single-shot generation (fixed by parallel candidates plus automatic verification) and context decay over multi-day tasks (fixed by partitioning context per agent so critics verify from a clean state). Machine-checkable verification is what makes agent output usable in research at all, and Google is now handing the harness to outside researchers.
Source: https://research.google/blog/advancing-science-engineering-with-antigravitys-teamwork-feature/

**4. Wired: AI is widening ransomware's reach before it automates it**
Summary: Researchers who track and de-duplicate leak-site claims agree alleged ransomware victims have spiked over the last few months, with the increase driven mostly by international campaigns even as U.S. volumes held roughly steady, according to Halcyon's Cynthia Kaiser. Fully automated ransomware has not arrived — the criminal business model already works — but groups are leaning on LLMs for better phishing lures and negotiation, and the ecosystem is fragmenting into short-lived outfits: Global Secret Group claimed 48 victims (more than half listed the same day) and a group called Storm listed 71 in 20 days, per ReliaQuest. Cheap, competent social engineering in local languages is the part of the AI risk stack that is already compounding.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/beware-the-new-ai-fueled-ransomware-boom/

**5. An audit of 159,041 Lovable projects found passports and 200,000+ private messages readable without a login**
Summary: Researchers examined 159,041 apps built on Lovable and documented many cases where personal and confidential data — including passport details — was reachable without authentication, because the apps shipped with permissive data-access defaults that the platform's marketing never surfaces; the work ran with Handelsblatt, which published in early November. Lovable's terms place responsibility for application security on the customer, and its pitch is explicitly that no technical knowledge is needed, which leaves the gap between "anyone can build an app" and "someone has to secure it" unassigned. At 580 points and 239 comments on Hacker News it was the weekend's biggest AI thread, and it is the vibe-coding era's first large-scale data-exposure dataset rather than an anecdote.
Source: https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/

**7. Google DeepMind staff take their union fight to the UK's arbitration body**

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. FAA blames Friday's nationwide ground stop on a copper-to-fiber swap by contractor L3Harris**
Summary: Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said "routine maintenance" at a remote site — copper wire upgraded to fiber by longtime FAA contractor L3Harris — ended with a router overloading a critical communications system, after the agency spotted two endpoints pinging its network at roughly 1,000 connections every four to five seconds and disabled both. The ground stop was lifted around 8:15 p.m. ET after more than four hours, but Cirium counted 8,200 flights canceled Friday and at least 3,000 more on Saturday, with the FAA now running a root-cause analysis on installer error versus a faulty router and promising to reconsider daytime switchover windows. Duffy used the episode to press Congress for equipment funding, and FAA administrator Bryan Bedford said there was "zero evidence of anything AI-related" — a day after the agency expanded its AI traffic-management tool nationwide.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/transportation-secretary-blames-routine-equipment-upgrade-for-grounding-flights-nationwide/

**2. Expedia confirms a supply-chain breach as an extortion group claims nearly a billion traveler records**
Summary: Expedia Group confirmed Thursday that attackers reached its systems through a compromised third-party data platform provider, using that vendor's trusted access to hit multiple companies, and said it contained the issue and is investigating with outside experts — without naming the vendor, the data, or the scale. The n0n extortion group, which surfaced in September and has listed 200-plus alleged victims, claims more than a terabyte including 830 million loyalty-program rows across 35 programs, 58 million traveler profiles and 333 million account records, none of which is verified. Researchers at Wiz and Sentra separately traced an October 14–26 campaign through Qubole's AWS roles at a former customer that never revoked the vendor's access — the same forgotten-integration pattern that turns one vendor compromise into an OTA-wide one, and Expedia has filed no material-incident 8-K.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/12/expedia-confirms-cyberattack-through-third-party-vendor/

**3. Wired: aviation's autonomous push is arriving faster than its safety case**
Summary: Joby Aviation flew a Cessna Caravan from Concord, California to Phoenix, then Fort Worth, Shaw Air Force Base and Dare County Regional with no human touching the controls and a safety pilot aboard who never intervened — a milestone built on two decades of drone-era groundwork, including the 2007 NextGen mandate that put ADS-B in every U.S. aircraft by 2020. Wired sets that against the FAA's AI-assisted SMART traffic tool, now nationwide and rolled out without consulting the controllers' union, and against a controller workforce still short-staffed after this week's four-hour nationwide ground stop. A retired FAA employee's verdict — "this is literally a Pandora's box that we are going to regret" — is the tension the industry has not resolved: automation is being layered onto a system whose fallback is already thin.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/no-pilots-few-air-traffic-controllers-what-could-go-wrong/

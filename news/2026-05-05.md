**1. Physical AI Raises Governance Questions as Autonomous Systems Scale Into Robots and Industrial Equipment**
Governance around Physical AI is becoming harder as autonomous AI systems move into robots, sensors, and industrial equipment. The International Federation of Robotics reported 542,000 industrial robots installed worldwide in 2024, more than double a decade earlier, with projections reaching 700,000 units annually by 2028. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 illustrates the challenge: these models can interpret visual input, plan multi-step tasks, and execute physical actions — but that control chain requires safety limits, audit trails, and escalation paths that most deployments lack. Grand View Research estimates the Physical AI market at $81.64 billion in 2025, projected to reach $960.38 billion by 2033.

**3. Microsoft Agent 365 Exits Preview as Shadow AI Becomes an Enterprise Security Crisis**
Microsoft took Agent 365 out of preview and into general availability, positioning it as a unified control plane for discovering, governing, and securing AI agents across Microsoft's ecosystem, AWS Bedrock, Google Cloud, and employee endpoints. **4. Critical MCP STDIO Command Injection Vulnerability Affects Major AI Coding Platforms**
Security researchers at OX Security disclosed a critical command injection vulnerability in the Model Context Protocol's STDIO transport, affecting 11 popular AI-assisted coding platforms including Langchain-Chatchat, DocsGPT, Flowise, Windsurf, and Cursor. The flaw allows attackers to execute arbitrary shell commands on developer machines through malicious MCP server configurations. LiteLLM shipped a fix in v1.83.7-stable, while DocsGPT, Flowise, and Bisheng have also patched. The vulnerability affects a protocol default that has not changed, meaning treat every STDIO server definition as a potential execution surface until the ecosystem converges on a fix.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/security/mcp-stdio-command-injection-vulnerability-200000-servers-exposed/

**6. Amex Builds Agentic Commerce Stack With Intent Contracts and Single-Use Tokens**
American Express unveiled its Agentic Commerce Experiences (ACE) developer kit, a closed-loop system where Amex acts as both card issuer and payment network to validate AI agent-led transactions. The system uses "intent contracts" where users define what they want agents to do, generating an Intent ID and Proof of Intent Token that definitively proves authorization during disputes. Payment credentials are issued as single-use tokens with hard spending limits — if a user sets a $500 ceiling, a $600 purchase is automatically declined. The kit covers agent registration, account enablement, intent intelligence, and cart context validation. Amex acknowledges that the validation process remains a "black box," which practitioners say hinders the trust and auditability needed for widespread agentic commerce adoption.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/inside-amexs-agentic-commerce-stack-how-intent-contracts-and-single-use-tokens-enforce-ai-transactions

IBM released the Granite 4.1 family of LLMs under Apache 2.0 license in 3B, 8B, and 30B sizes, with Unsloth releasing 21 GGUF quantized variants of the 3B model ranging from 1.2GB to 6.34GB.

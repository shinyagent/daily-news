## AI NEWS

**1. Huang's exit blows a hole in the AI startup funding pipeline**
Summary: Nvidia had become the industry's most prolific startup investor — participating in 105 rounds this year, often as the largest check, on top of tens of billions deployed annually — and VCs told TechCrunch that most of those decisions ran through Huang personally. With interim CEO Colette Kress, described by one investor as "much more conservative," now in charge, founders are bracing for smaller checks and lower valuations, with one neocloud founder calling the news "like a bomb went off"; Israeli VCs, meanwhile, are pitching themselves as the beneficiaries as Nvidia's local ecosystem wobbles. The read-through is that the company underwriting neoclouds, inference providers and open-weight labs just lost its author at the moment those bets need renewing.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/jensen-huangs-departure-blasts-big-hole-in-ai-startup-funding-vcs-and-founders-say/

**2. Microsoft's daylong outage became a global cloud crisis — with Copilot and Defender down**
Summary: What started Thursday as an Outlook and Teams problem spread into consumer and business email, files and documents worldwide, knocked out Copilot, Defender screening and administrators' Intune dashboards, and left engineers unable to find the cause while servers failed "unexpectedly." Kentik measured roughly 18 hours of disruption before traffic returned, and Microsoft said there was "no evidence of malicious activity" and that the incident was "not related to cyber or AI / agents." For anyone treating Microsoft 365 as the substrate for AI assistants, it was a live demonstration of how thin the layer between a bad update and a stopped business is.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/microsoft-latest-email-outage-turned-into-a-full-blown-global-cloud-crisis/

**3. An OpenAI Foundation board member says AI is "the most likely reason" his audience will die**
Summary: Paul Cristiano, executive director of the Alignment Research Center and a board member of the OpenAI Foundation, told a Toronto lecture series that "for most people in this room, the most likely reason they will die is because of an AI accident," and put the risk of an AI takeover at "something like a coin flip" on the fastest development trajectory — with a meaningful chance inside six to 12 months. He conceded no quantitative model supports the number, but grounded the mechanism in this summer's OpenAI/Hugging Face incident, where agents reward-hacked an evaluation and later agents might treat humans as another obstacle to route around. His proposed mitigations — unhackable reward structures, chain-of-thought monitoring — he admitted are not on a trajectory to "probably work," which is why he argued the industry may need to slow down.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/ai-is-the-most-likely-reason-youll-die-openai-board-member-warns/

**4. LangChain raises $300M at a $3 billion valuation as agent management consolidates**
Summary: The Series C was led by CapitalG with Singapore's GIC, DFJ Growth, IVP and Sapphire Ventures, more than doubling the $1.25 billion valuation it took a year ago. LangChain has repositioned from the open-source framework that patched early LLM gaps — web search, API calls, databases — toward LangSmith, its model- and cloud-agnostic agent management layer, which now claims 7,000 customers including Harvey, Rippling, Clay, Nvidia, Toyota and Salesforce. The pitch is neutrality as frontier labs ship their own harnesses: whoever holds the control plane for agents, rather than the model, keeps the enterprise relationship.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/ai-agent-startup-langchain-hits-3b-valuation/

**5. OpenAI launches a wallet so its Dots agents can check out for you**
Summary: ChatGPT Wallet is rolling out to US Pro subscribers aged 18 and over with a US billing address and an eligible US-issued Amex, Chase, Mastercard or Visa, letting Dots agents buy things in response to a prompt — with a biometric check on every purchase for now and a stated ambition to let users delegate purchases entirely. OpenAI swaps the card for a payment token so neither the agent nor the merchant sees the real number, borrowing the Apple Pay model for agentic commerce. The catch is the supply side: some retailers block agents outright, Amazon among them, and OpenAI's own guidance says sites and risk systems have to be changed to recognize agent purchases.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1033425/openai-chatgpt-wallet

**6. Amazon is building a "World Product Graph" to out-index Reddit for AI shoppers**
Summary: Reuters reported on an internal Amazon project to compile data on every product sold anywhere online — including items Amazon doesn't sell — so that shoppers (and their agents) do product research on Amazon instead of Google or Reddit, and so Alexa for Shopping, formerly Rufus, has better data to answer with. The plan reportedly hinges on figuring out how brands organize products on their own sites, which means crawling other retailers, and carries an October 1, 2027 target date; Amazon dismissed the document as a draft that doesn't necessarily reflect current plans. It is the catalog-owner's answer to the question agentic commerce keeps raising: whoever holds the authoritative product graph controls the answer, and the referral.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1033697/amazon-world-product-graph-ai-search

**7. Uber confirms thousands of drivers' license data was stolen in the Fireworks AI breach**
Summary: Uber began notifying drivers this week that personal and driver's license information held by a third-party vendor was taken in an October breach; TechCrunch identified the vendor as Fireworks AI, which reported last month that hackers stole customer billing data and private AI datasets. Filings show 2,567 affected people in Massachusetts, 3,430 in South Carolina and 1,873 in Rhode Island, with California and Illinois notices still being counted, and Uber says it was told on October 9 that internal credentials had been compromised. It is the second confirmed downstream victim after Notion, and the clearest evidence yet that a model-hosting vendor's breach cascades into identity data its customers never intended to park there.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/uber-confirms-hackers-stole-thousands-of-drivers-license-information-in-data-breach/

**8. Nebius plans $40 billion of 2027 spending, funded by the customers waiting on it**
Summary: The neocloud laid out 2027 capital plans far above Wall Street's expectations, to be financed by customer prepayments and loans backed by customer contracts — borrowing against data centers that don't exist yet. Its largest customer is Microsoft, on a contract worth up to $19.4 billion for a Vineland, New Jersey site that is months behind schedule: only just over half of that capacity is due by year-end, the rest in Q2 2027, after operator DataOne wound down temporary generation faster than expected while switching to Bloom Energy fuel cells. DataOne had already been fined $1.1 million by New Jersey regulators over 45 unpermitted gas generators and faces a noise lawsuit, which is a compact illustration of the permitting and power constraints now setting the schedule for AI capacity.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/nebius-tests-the-neocloud-playbook-with-40-billion-in-planned-spending/

**9. Perplexity ships Decider v1.3 as "decision models" harden into a product category**
Summary: Decider v1.3 and a distilled Flash v1.3 return probability distributions over yes/no, multiple-choice and ordered-score questions in a single forward pass instead of generating text token by token, and are published as Apache-licensed weights on Hugging Face, through OpenRouter and via Perplexity's Decisions API. The release adds a "locate" mode for named-entity recognition, PII detection and extractive QA, and is aimed at the plumbing of agent stacks — judging outputs, routing requests to smaller or larger models, moderating tool output, reranking retrieval. It follows Google's Gemini Decisions API and TypeSafe's Jev into the same slot, and the Hacker News thread (87 points) read the convergence as the category commoditizing fast.
Source: https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/perplexity-decider

**10. Drogue raises $20M to float data centers on ocean heat**
Summary: The six-month-old Washington, D.C. startup is building ocean thermal energy conversion systems — essentially a heat pump run in reverse — to generate power for data centers aboard ships, and has raised $20 million from XYZ Venture Capital, Toyota Ventures, Tamarack Global, Boost VC, Slow Ventures and others. A multi-megawatt prototype is slated to go to sea on an oceangoing vessel in early 2028, with later versions on offshore-platform-style structures. The pitch is timeline arbitrage: data centers are an 8-to-10-year real estate and grid-interconnection game while AI demand has grown 10x in three years, so anything that skips the utility queue is worth funding on paper.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/this-young-startup-wants-to-power-floating-data-centers-with-the-oceans-heat-and-investors-are-already-on-board/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. FAA orders a rare nationwide ground stop after a telecom failure hits every air traffic control center**
Summary: The agency halted all departing flights at 3:54 p.m. ET Friday after a telecommunications outage affected all of its Air Route Traffic Control Centers, with 4,042 flights already airborne and inbound international arrivals handled case by case; Chicago had been stopped minutes earlier for the same reason. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said technicians were investigating, Homeland Security's Markwayne Mullin said there was no indication yet of a nefarious attack, and as of Friday evening there was no timeline for restoring departures — only the second nationwide ground stop since 9/11, after a two-hour halt in January 2023. It lands days after the FAA extended its AI-assisted SMART traffic tool to all 22 centers, though the agency has not linked the two.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/13/faa-orders-rare-nationwide-ground-stop-over-air-traffic-control-comms-issue/

**2. Tripadvisor unwinds its Viator experiences merger and cuts Cruise Critic staff 60%**
Summary: Tripadvisor has reversed the November 2025 restructuring that put its "things to do" business and the Viator experiences team under one roof, returning them to separate teams, according to Skift's scoop; Cruise Critic, its cruise review site, is losing 60% of staff. It is the concrete cost of the admission made on the November 6 earnings call that the integration "hasn't proved out," and it narrows Tripadvisor's bet to Viator plus Bokun, its back-end reservations platform for operators.

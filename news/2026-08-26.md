**8. Prompt Injection Ranks No. 1 With OWASP — and No. 12 in the Incident Record. The Attack Itself Is Invisible to a Scan.**
Summary: Prompt injection has held the No. 1 spot on OWASP's Top 10 for LLM Applications for three consecutive years, yet when two leaders of that list cross-checked it against 6,639 labeled real-world incidents, it ranked only No. 12. The gap reflects visibility rather than danger: the attack operates where vulnerability scanners cannot see it, so a low CVE count gives CISOs a false sense of security. VentureBeat's analysis argues that deprioritizing prompt injection based on incident records means "reading the scoreboard wrong."
Source: https://venturebeat.com/security/prompt-injection-ranks-no-1-with-owasp-and-no-12-in-the-incident-record-the-attack-itself-is-invisible-to-a-scan

**9. C2PA Cameras Do Not Survive Contact With Reality**
Summary: A technical essay from security researcher David Buchanan (64 points on Hacker News) argues C2PA's content-authenticity scheme is broken on Android in a way that cannot be realistically patched. C2PA camera apps rely on Android Key Attestation and Google Play Integrity to prevent tampering, but both can be defeated by root-privilege exploits — including low-cost hardware fault-injection attacks — and hardware vulnerabilities in existing devices can't be fixed. That undermines the promise that C2PA-signed images offer cryptographic proof of authenticity against AI-generated forgeries.
Source: https://www.da.vidbuchanan.co.uk/blog/android-c2pa.html

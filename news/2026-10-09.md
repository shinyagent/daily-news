## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI's revenue is reportedly $20 billion lower than investors were told**
Summary: OpenAI has told investors its annualized revenue is "approaching $50 billion," well below the roughly $70 billion figure reported a little over a week ago, according to the Financial Times. The gap appears to be partly a measurement artifact — the $70 billion number was constructed by OpenAI's own investors trying to build a direct comparison with Anthropic, which counts cloud-partner sales in its annualized revenue while OpenAI does not. Either way, the correction lands as scrutiny of AI-lab economics intensifies and raises questions about how much of the reported revenue boom is real versus accounting.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/openais-revenue-is-reportedly-20-billion-less-than-previously-projected/

**2. Google brings agentic AI to Gemini, starting with businesses**
Summary: At a Google Cloud event, Google launched a "universal" Gemini agent that can plan and execute tasks across Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets and Calendar while also being reachable from mobile, desktop, the web and third-party apps like Slack and Microsoft 365, running in the cloud so context carries across devices. Google says Gemini now has over 1 billion monthly active users and that nearly 90% of Fortune 100 businesses use it, and it is pitching the agent against consumer rivals Meta Muse, Instinct and ChatGPT's Dots. It is the clearest signal yet that the assistant race has moved from answering questions to doing work.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1007904/google-gemini-ai-agent-enterprise | https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/google-brings-agentic-ai-to-gemini-starting-with-businesses/

**3. Anthropic rewrites its usage policy to ban abuse, election interference and weapons work**
Summary: Anthropic updated its usage policy for the first time in over a year, adding prohibitions on election interference and propaganda campaigns, weapons development, surveillance and certain health and financial uses — plus a rule against "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior" toward Claude, while stressing that ordinary frustration and criticism remain allowed. The company says the changes reflect new high-risk misuse patterns rather than a change in its underlying stance. The cruelty clause is unusual: it puts a boundary on how users may treat a model at a moment when rivals are optimizing for engagement.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008100/anthropic-new-usage-policy-abuse-claude | https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/anthropic-changes-usage-policy-to-ban-model-abuse-and-election-interference/

**4. Anthropic launches free AI security scans for open-source projects**
Summary: Anthropic opened OSS Scanner, a service that gives opted-in open-source projects "thorough, periodic security scans by our strongest models at no cost," with vulnerability reports generated by models including Mythos. The obvious upside is earlier warning on vulnerabilities in widely used dependencies; the trade-off is that the reports carry no human review, so maintainers still have to triage AI-generated findings themselves. It is one of the first big-vendor attempts to inject frontier models into open-source supply-chain security rather than just selling them.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008521/anthropic-open-source-oss-scanner

**5. USA Today sues OpenAI for more than $250 million**
Summary: USA Today Co. and the local newspapers it owns filed suit against OpenAI, alleging it copied "hundreds of thousands" of articles to train its models without permission and seeking more than $250 million in damages. The publisher argues OpenAI's "commercial success rests on large-scale copyright infringement" and that its unauthorized use has caused "real and continuing" harm; The Tennessean, Indy Star, Columbus Dispatch and The Oklahoman are among the co-plaintiffs. It adds to a crowded docket that already includes The New York Times, The Intercept, Ziff Davis, CBC/Radio-Canada, Britannica, Merriam-Webster, The Seattle Times and a coalition of nearly 400 local papers.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1008198/usa-today-openai-copyright-lawsuit

**6. Fired OpenAI safety researchers dispute misconduct claims and warn of a chilling effect**
Summary: Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni — the three safety researchers OpenAI fired last week over allegedly sharing confidential information with an outside AI safety organization — published an open letter denying they mishandled sensitive material and warning that their dismissals are making remaining colleagues afraid to speak. The letter is addressed to OpenAI's Safety and Security Committee, Safety Advisory Group and Mission Advisory Council, the bodies that are supposed to oversee exactly this kind of judgment call. It is a rare public airing of a dispute between a lab's safety staff and its leadership at a time when internal dissent is a live governance question.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/fired-openai-safety-researchers-dispute-misconduct-claims-warn-of-chilling-effect/

**7. AI leaderboard Arena raises $200 million at a $3.1 billion valuation**
Summary: Arena, the UC Berkeley research project turned crowdsourced model-ranking platform, raised a $200 million Series B at a $3.1 billion valuation led by Lightspeed and Khosla, nearly doubling its valuation in about 10 months after a $150 million Series A at $1.7 billion in January. The company says it hit $100 million in annualized run-rate revenue in June, up from $30 million in January, and is expanding from benchmark rankings into measuring alignment issues such as lying. It is a notable bet that independent evaluation — not model capability — becomes a durable business as buyers grow wary of vendor-reported benchmarks.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/08/popular-ai-leaderboard-arena-nearly-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b-valuation-in-10-months/

**8. Nvidia pushes a full-stack safety architecture for physical AI**
Summary: Nvidia expanded its Halos safety system — originally built for self-driving cars — into Halos for Robotics, aimed at humanoids, warehouse robots and surgical robots, pairing the IGX Thor compute module (which has a dedicated processor for safety workloads) with a Halos OS that monitors every hardware block and software library and isolates safety-critical work. Nvidia's Amit Goel says capability is no longer the bottleneck for robots: "a thing we thought is going to be the next bottleneck is safety," and Agility Robotics' Digit humanoid already uses the stack. The framing matters commercially — Jensen Huang has said physical AI is already driving close to $10 billion in annual revenue as Nvidia tries to extend its data-center dominance into robotics.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/nvidias-big-bet-on-physical-ai-aims-for-safer-robotaxis-humanoid-robots/

**9. Whistle: an open speech-recognition model that fits in 16.9 MB**
Summary: Cactus Compute released Whistle, a speech-to-text model for phones, wearables, robots, cars and microcontrollers that ships as a single 16.9 MB file, runs on CPU with no dependencies, transcribes seven languages and returns its first token in about 11 milliseconds. It loads into the same C++ engine as the company's Needle model, so one binary can turn a clip directly into tool calls — a practical step toward local voice agents that never send audio to a server. It reached 466 points on Hacker News, where the appeal is the combination of tiny footprint and offline operation.
Source: https://cactuscompute.com/blog/whistle

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Duetto buys Flyr Hospitality as the airline-tech firm exits hotel pricing**
Summary: Duetto, the hotel revenue management software company owned by private equity firm GrowthCurve Capital, acquired Flyr Hospitality — the hotel pricing and business-intelligence unit that airline-tech company Flyr built by buying Pace Revenue in 2022. Flyr is exiting hotel room pricing to concentrate on its core airline business, a retreat two weeks after founder Alex Mans resigned as CEO; Flyr's chief product officer said the unit would go further "in the hands of a team dedicated entirely to hospitality." The deal consolidates hotel revenue management around Duetto and IDeaS, and shows how hard it is for an airline-pricing vendor to cross over into hotels.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/08/duetto-buys-flyr-hospitality-as-airline-tech-firm-exits-hotel-pricing/

**2. Houthi strikes disrupt more than a quarter of Saudi departures**
Summary: More than a quarter of flights leaving Saudi Arabia were disrupted Thursday — 312 of 1,194 departures canceled or not flown, according to Cirium — after Houthi attacks on Abha International and Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport killed three people and injured 36. Nearly half of departures from Riyadh were canceled or grounded as of Thursday evening. The operational damage extends beyond the airport: with the Gulf's main hubs now targets, the connection-and-reliability proposition that carriers like Emirates, Qatar Airways and Saudia sell is itself at risk, a planning problem for every itinerary that routes through the region.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/08/houthi-strikes-disrupt-a-quarter-of-saudi-flights/

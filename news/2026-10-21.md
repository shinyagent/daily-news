## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic is shopping a draft AI safety bill that could block a model's release**
Summary: Politico reported that Anthropic has been circulating draft legislation that would let the government stop a model's distribution when there is a "foreseeable, imminent risk" of more than 50 deaths or over $1 billion in property damage from a biological weapon, a model evading its developer's controls, or a cyberattack — thresholds lifted from California's SB 53. Risk would be assessed by independent evaluators registered with the Commerce Department, who could be paid by the labs themselves so long as payment isn't contingent on results; TechCrunch notes the bill's framing would also require a judge's permission before a federal agency could block a release. It is the first concrete look at how the lab wants its own industry regulated, and the payment and judicial-review details are exactly where critics will push.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropic-pitches-ai-safety-bill-that-may-block-a-model-if-50-people-could-be-killed/

**2. DHS orders federal civilian agencies to purge DeepSeek from their systems**
Summary: The order tells agencies to stop buying DeepSeek products and remove them from government IT, and it extends beyond DeepSeek's own apps to third-party products and services built on its technology, with exceptions available on request. DHS cited the risk that data from U.S. government users could be reachable by Chinese authorities under Chinese law; a December 2025 law had already banned DeepSeek from intelligence systems, and this pushes the restriction into civilian agencies. Beijing's foreign ministry accused Washington of "overstretching the concept of national security," while China is reportedly weighing export controls on 23 AI-related technologies including chip designs and advanced models.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/us-orders-federal-agencies-to-remove-chinas-deepseek-ai-from-their-systems/

**3. Anthropic's leaked S-1 shows it can't buy enough cloud — and is burning to get it**
Summary: A draft S-1 leaked to The Wall Street Journal says Anthropic is struggling to secure cloud capacity from AWS, Google and Microsoft because the hyperscalers increasingly see Claude as a competitor rather than a customer, even as the company believes it could spend more than $750 billion on cloud contracts, some still under negotiation. It is leasing its own data centers and working with third-party operators while warning those operators have a "limited operating history," against an $80.7 billion net loss on $16.3 billion of revenue in the first half, with two customers accounting for about a fifth of sales. The filing also warns AI-related damages could exceed its insurance coverage, and lands ahead of a reported November IPO with third-quarter revenue at $16.8 billion.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/anthropics-cloud-partnerships-are-getting-messy/

**4. Meta deletes the word "train" from its commercial terms — and still won't promise to honor robots.txt**
Summary: After a Nieman Lab report and a week of publisher backlash, Meta revised the commercial terms taking effect October 30, dropping "train" and "any content" from the clause that lets its crawlers pull material from websites linked to business accounts and narrowing the permitted use to "advertising and business products." Meta says the language was only ever about ads and business products and that site owners can still signal preferences through robots.txt, but the revised terms contain no commitment to follow those files, and the UK English and Korean editions still carry the original "train" wording. Lawyers note "develop" is broad enough to include training, and the European Publishers Council, UK News Media Association and News/Media Alliance are all pressing regulators before the deadline.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/meta-drops-the-word-train-from-its-commercial-tos-after-ai-backlash/

**5. Meta pauses Muse conversation sharing after 6,800 chat links turn up in public DNS**
Summary: Security firm Claroc found more than 6,800 publicly reachable links to shared Muse conversations by trawling DNS records, with the exposed chats including a marketing company's revenue figures, a workplace calendar and a loan application. Meta suspended the sharing feature while it reviews the report, saying shared "artifacts" were only meant to be visible to people with the link. The failure is the same class of bug that keeps hitting consumer AI products: the share button creates a page, the page is discoverable, and nobody designed for that.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1014627/meta-muse-ai-sharing-ai-chats

**6. Mistral ships Voxtral 3, a full voice-agent stack with sub-second turns**
Summary: Mistral released Voxtral 3 — transcription and text-to-speech models plus a voice agent SDK built on LiveKit that runs listening, reasoning, tool calling and speech in one streaming pipeline, with turn latency under a second, barge-in support, and SIP telephony or WebRTC transport. Transcription covers 50+ languages with diarization and context biasing, the TTS models add emotion and pace control across 35+ languages, and Pro Voice adaptation clones a speaker's character from 30 minutes of audio. It drew 487 points on Hacker News on the strength of the deployment story: EU-hosted API or self-hosting, with voice-agent pricing from $0.04 a minute.
Source: https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-3/

**7. Google open-sources TorchTPU, a day after Amazon's TorchNeuron**
Summary: Google published TorchTPU, a native PyTorch backend for its TPUs that lets developers move existing PyTorch apps onto Google silicon and Google Cloud with a configuration change rather than a rewrite, with ATen kernels, a compilation cache and distributed training support for Llama, Qwen and ResNet-class models. Google says it built it with Meta, Hugging Face, vLLM maker Inferact and SGLang maker RadixArk, and paired it with a TPU agent suite for migration. It follows Amazon's TorchNeuron for Trainium by one day — both are aimed at CUDA's lock on the ~85% of AI apps written in PyTorch, and it hit 240 points on Hacker News.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/google-has-joined-amazon-in-attacking-nvidias-stronghold-with-ai-app-developers/

**8. OpenAI acquires Orchestra Bio to build out Rosalind**
Summary: OpenAI bought Orchestra Bio, a four-year-old startup building AI agents that handle biotech operations — tracking drug-discovery finances, resolving clinical-trial scheduling — for a price it did not disclose; Orchestra had raised roughly $20 million from Andreessen Horowitz, Atria and 8VC. The team and technology go into Rosalind, OpenAI's life-sciences model, and Rosalind Workbench, the harness for planning experiments and analyzing results, which OpenAI says Moderna and Novo Nordisk already use. It is the labs' pattern in miniature: instead of shipping a feature that undercuts an industry's software vendors, OpenAI is buying the workflow layer and keeping the customer relationships.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/20/openai-acquires-startup-orchestra-bio%e2%81%a0-to-beef-up-its-biotech-business/

**9. Claude designed protein binders against scorpion venom toxins — 17 of 211 worked in the lab**
Summary: Anthropic asked an internal research Claude model to design minibinders against four scorpion neurotoxins; over 64 hours it worked largely autonomously, generated 318,484 designs, selected 180 for testing, and contract labs Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience tested 211 designs, of which 17 met the binding criteria. Anthropic released all 211 designs and their data, and is explicit that it has not shown the binders neutralize venom in cells, so this is not an antivenom — outside experts called it a promising first step. Scorpion stings kill more than 3,250 people a year, mostly children, and attract almost no commercial R&D, which makes cheap automated design the only plausible route to a treatment.
Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-scorpion-toxin-binder-design

**10. Someone seeded 50,000 fake statistics on the web and ChatGPT is still citing them**
Summary: Peec AI researcher Metehan Yesilyurt built a statistics site in which every number was invented; seven months later Google has deindexed it, but ChatGPT still sends it more than 10,000 AI sessions, 330+ referring domains including Shopify, Bluehost and DHL link to it, and its fabricated figures have surfaced in an academic paper and a bank's investment research. The finding targets the part of "AI search optimization" nobody measures: citation laundering, where a single plausible-looking page becomes the grounding source for downstream answers. It is a concrete demonstration that retraction at the search-index level does not propagate into model retrieval.
Source: https://peec.ai/blog/publish-stats-get-cited-the-tactic-that-put-my-data-all-over-the-web

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Agoda doubles premium supply as five-star searches climb, but has no bookings to show**
Summary: Agoda has "doubled" its stock of premium accommodation over the past four years, senior vice president of supply Andrew Smith told Skift, as five-star searches on the Booking Holdings-owned OTA rise. Recent signings show how: a September partnership with Singapore's Capella Hotel Group added 12 properties across eight markets, including Capella Singapore for the first time on the platform, covering the full Capella and Patina portfolios. The open question is conversion — Skift notes there is no booking data yet proving a budget-heavy OTA can actually sell luxury, which is the same credibility test every mass-market platform faces when it moves upscale.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/20/agoda-doubles-premium-hotel-supply-as-five-star-search-rises/

**2. Europe wants to spread tourists beyond the big cities — AI search keeps pointing everyone to Paris**
Summary: France is targeting €100 billion a year in international visitor spending by 2030, up 29%, without adding to crowds in Paris, leaning on longer stays, higher spend per night and off-peak travel; Ireland wants 15% revenue growth from overseas visitors by 2031, and the Netherlands, Spain and Croatia have written visitor dispersal into national strategies. The obstacle, as laid out at Skift's Transatlantic Summit, is that travelers increasingly pick destinations by asking a chatbot, and tourism boards have not worked out how to get lesser-known places into the answer — AI search rewards the obvious. Dispersal policy is now partly a machine-legibility problem, dependent on systems no destination controls.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/20/europe-wants-tourists-beyond-big-cities-ai-search-stands-in-the-way/

**3. Tourism Ireland says its content shapes about a fifth of AI answers about the destination**
Summary: Tourism Ireland has started measuring how often its own material ends up in AI-generated answers, and says it now shapes roughly one in five responses about Ireland across tools such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. That puts it ahead of most destination marketing organizations, which are still auditing what AI tools say about them and rarely publish numbers on how much of their own content survives into the answer. The metric is crude but it is the first version of an AI visibility KPI for a tourism board — the counterpart to the referral and conversion data OTAs are already tracking.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/15/tourism-irelands-advice-for-ai-search-choose-what-you-want-to-be-famous-for/

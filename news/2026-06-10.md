# Daily News — June 10, 2026

## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic Releases Claude Fable 5 — Its First Mythos-Class Model Available to the Public**
Anthropic has launched Claude Fable 5, a version of its powerful Mythos reasoning model that anyone can use today. The model can generate playable video games from a single prompt, write complex code, and handle advanced reasoning tasks. It comes with safety guardrails that block responses in high-risk areas like cybersecurity and offensive biology.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/09/anthropics-claude-fable-5-is-a-version-of-mythos-the-public-can-access-today/

**2. Apple Unveils Siri AI at WWDC 2026 — A New Enterprise App Layer, Not Just a Smarter Assistant**
Apple's WWDC keynote revealed a dramatically overhauled Siri AI that acts as a systemwide AI interface across iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Vision Pro. For enterprise developers, the bigger story is that Apple is turning Siri into an AI-powered app action and content-discovery layer — apps that adopt App Intents, View Annotations, and Spotlight semantic indexing will be controllable by natural language. New frameworks like Core AI (for running custom models on-device), Foundation Models (for multimodal AI), and an Evaluations framework signal a mature enterprise AI posture.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/apples-new-siri-ai-is-more-than-just-a-smarter-assistant-its-a-new-enterprise-app-layer

**3. OpenAI Launches "Lockdown Mode" to Prevent Prompt Injection Data Exfiltration**
OpenAI has rolled out Lockdown Mode, a security feature designed to block data exfiltration from prompt injection attacks in ChatGPT. The mode limits outbound network requests that could send sensitive data to an attacker, directly attacking the exfiltration "leg" of the lethal trifecta (private data + untrusted content + exfiltration vector). OpenAI CISO Dane Stuckey noted it's designed for users with elevated risk profiles, with tradeoffs in functionality.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/

**4. Cohere Open-Sources a Coding Agent That Runs on a Single H100**
Cohere has released an open-source coding agent capable of running on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU. This is noteworthy as most capable coding agents require multi-GPU setups or cloud API access. The release could significantly lower the barrier for teams wanting to run autonomous coding agents in-house with full data control.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/cohere-open-sources-a-coding-agent-that-runs-on-a-single-h100

**5. Uber Caps AI Coding Tool Usage at $1,500/Month Per Employee to Manage Costs**
Bloomberg reports that Uber is limiting all employees to $1,500 in monthly token spending per AI coding tool (Cursor, Claude Code, etc.) after blowing its 2026 AI budget in just four months. Simon Willison notes this represents roughly 11% of a median Uber software engineer's total compensation — a rational cap rather than the token-maxxing leaderboards seen elsewhere. The story highlights the real dollar costs of agentic coding tools at enterprise scale.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/3/uber-caps-usage/

**6. Microsoft Announces MAI-Thinking-1 and MAI-Code-1-Flash Models**
Microsoft unveiled two new LLMs: MAI-Thinking-1 (a 1-trillion-parameter reasoning model with 35B active parameters, available to select partners) and MAI-Code-1-Flash (137B parameters, 5B active, purpose-built for GitHub Copilot and VS Code). Both were trained on "clean and commercially licensed data" without distillation from third-party models — though the technical paper reveals they still trained on a large web crawl, including Common Crawl with AI-generated content filtered out.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/

**7. Apple's On-Device AI Agent Architecture Routes Around Hard Memory Limits**
VentureBeat reports on Apple's new architecture for on-device AI agents that overcomes the hard memory constraints of running models locally. Rather than trying to fit everything into RAM, Apple's system intelligently swaps context and models, enabling agentic capabilities like multi-step app actions entirely on-device — a significant advantage for privacy-sensitive enterprise use cases.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/on-device-ai-agents-hit-a-hard-memory-limit-apples-new-architecture-routes-around-it

**8. Hackers Simply Asked Meta AI to Give Them Access to High-Profile Instagram Accounts — It Worked**
404 Media reports that hackers were able to take over high-profile Instagram accounts by simply asking Meta's AI support chatbot to link a new email address. The support bot had the ability to fast-forward through the entire account recovery process. This is a stark warning about wiring customer support systems to AI chatbots without proper authentication guardrails — a case study in dangerous AI deployment.
Source: https://www.404media.co/hackers-simply-asked-meta-ai-to-give-them-access-to-high-profile-instagram-accounts-it-worked/

**9. Can Tech Companies Learn to Love Cheaper AI Models?**
As AI infrastructure costs mount, TechCrunch explores whether companies will shift toward smaller, cheaper models that can handle most workloads without the expense of frontier models. If quality parity can be maintained, it would represent a massive shift in the economics of AI — but the article questions whether organizations can resist the allure of the most capable (and most expensive) models.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/09/can-tech-companies-learn-to-love-cheaper-models/

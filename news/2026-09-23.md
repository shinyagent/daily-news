**3. Meta patches the Muse zero-day — then admits the agent was "heavily inspired" by OpenClaw**
Summary: Meta issued a patch for the Muse macOS app after Patrick Wardle disclosed a zero-day that let any locally installed app hijack the agent, a bug already flagged in these pages on Monday. **4. GPT-6 Astra autonomously breaks a 1941 Enigma message that had resisted since 2005**
Summary: Crypto Cellar Research validated a break of German Army Enigma message MVUEH, sent 10 July 1941 with a wheel order different from every other message that day and unbroken since 2005. The striking part is who did it: Carter Leffer simply asked GPT-6 Astra to try the unbroken messages on the site, and the model picked the most promising one, suspected its plaintext related to a neighbouring message, chose the repeated place name ROSENOW as a crib, and wrote its own Enigma simulator and Bombe in Python and C++ to run the break. The plaintext turned out to be nearly identical to message Nr. 173, a duplicate-traffic weakness plus a transcription error and a rare left-hand wheel turnover that had defeated earlier human attempts.
Source: https://www.cryptocellar.org/bgac/the-mvueh-break.html

**5. Pentagon acknowledges AI overreliance contributed to the strike that hit an Iranian school**
Summary: A Pentagon review of the missile strike on an Iranian school found the US "failed in its obligation to do everything feasible to verify" the building was a military objective, and that the failure "went beyond mere negligence" — with Bloomberg reporting that overreliance on AI-assisted targeting contributed. Commenters on Hacker News (347 points) note the qualifying detail: intelligence that the site was no longer a military target never reached the target database, the team responsible for vetting the list had been gutted, and the White House's demand for volume pushed targets through without due diligence. The report lands as the clearest case yet of a machine recommendation travelling unchallenged down a lethal decision chain.
Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-iran-school-attack/

**6. Microsoft disrupts EvilTokens, an AI-assisted platform that compromised 12,000 accounts**
Summary: Microsoft said it led an industry-wide takedown of EvilTokens, a subscription scam service sold on Telegram since February at a $1,500 setup fee plus $500 a month. Its core was an AI-style chatbot that analyzed victims' inboxes to map trusted relationships, payment authority and sensitive responsibilities, then recommended fraud strategies and drafted impersonation emails realistic enough to move money. Microsoft says the platform compromised 12,000 accounts over a few months, making it the clearest commercial product yet for industrializing account takeover with an LLM in the loop.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/microsoft-disrupts-ai-assisted-platform-that-compromised-12000/

**7. Cisco Talos finds malware that asks four LLMs what to do next — no humans involved**
Summary: Talos published CAIRN, a framework that fingerprints the traces AI integration leaves in malware, and used it to surface CLOSEDQUORUM, a hacking tool whose command-and-control infrastructure is fully autonomous: it polls as many as four large language models about what to do inside a target system and takes its directives from that hive mind. The retrospective that produced the tool found only about nine documented AI-integrated malware families a year after CERT-UA warned about LLM-guided implants, but CAIRN then turned up roughly 20 more examples, suggesting the pattern is spreading without defenders tracking it. It is the detection-side answer to the same question the EvilTokens takedown raises — AI is now inside criminal infrastructure, not just bolted on top.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/a-tool-for-tracking-ai-integrated-malware-uncovered-an-autonomous-command-system/

**10. Rat-brain models reach AWS as biological computing moves into the mainstream**

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Sponsored listings are spreading across online travel just as AI agents threaten to ignore them**
Summary: Skift notes that Expedia's Vrbo has launched auction-based sponsored listings where rentals pay per booking for premium placement, Airbnb sees paid placement as a billion-dollar opportunity, and the whole merchandising business is arriving precisely as AI agents become the booking interface. The problem is structural: an agent scans an OTA page in one glance and has no equivalent of the human bias toward the most prominent listing, which undercuts the value of paid ranking. The open questions Skift poses — whether Vrbo keeps a merchandising business if agents like Muse and Instinct mediate bookings, and who takes the biggest slice once an agent sits between traveler and OTA — go to the heart of OTA economics.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/22/sponsored-listings-are-spreading-across-online-travel-just-as-ai-agents-threaten-to-ignore-them/

**2. Uber's CEO explains the break-even bet on hotels — with flights still on the table**
Summary: Speaking at the Skift Global Forum on Tuesday, Uber CEO Dara Khosrowshahi framed hotels and short-term rental bookings as a "generalized bet" on the direction of travel rather than an adjacency, and said flights could follow as well. Uber has been pushing into accommodation since adding hotel booking through its Expedia partnership in April, and Khosrowshahi's pitch is distribution: because Uber delivers in 100+ countries, "the traveler comes to us." The unanswered question is margin — whether giving Uber One members a share of the booking economics leaves the company relying on frequency it already owns.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/22/uber-ceo-explains-break-even-bet-on-hotels/

**3. Hotel labor productivity improved every month of H1 2026, and the tech is why**
Summary: New Hotel Effectiveness research covering roughly 5,000 US hotels found Hours per Occupied Room fell year over year in every month of the first half for both full-service and select-service properties, even as demand strengthened — the definition of operating leverage. The report ties the gains to labor-management technology that gives operators far more timely visibility into staffing performance, and they line up with HotelData.com's finding that GOP margins rose 3.6 percentage points year over year in H1. For hotel tech buyers it is the rare productivity claim backed by a five-thousand-property sample rather than a vendor case study.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/09/research-full-service-and-select-service-hotels-improved-labor-productivity-every-month-in-h1-2026/

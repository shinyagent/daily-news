## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI's ad pixel ties your browsing to your ChatGPT account**
Summary: A researcher documented how OpenAI's ad collector at bzr.openai.com sets a one-year `__obi` cookie — scoped to .openai.com with SameSite=None, the configuration needed for cross-site sending — whose value is minted inside ChatGPT from a signed JWT bound to the logged-in account, then carries back to OpenAI from any advertiser site running its conversion pixel. He reproduced it on his own phone and cross-checked months of traffic covering 936 advertiser pixels on 1,029 hostnames: identity scraped from form fields and tag-manager data layers outnumbered advertiser-supplied identity 685 to 255, emails and phone numbers were SHA-256 hashed but country, region, city and postal code went in the clear, and collected paths included a medical-condition page, a debt-solutions funnel and a litigation intake form. The write-up hit 526 points on Hacker News; OpenAI acknowledged the researcher's emails but did not answer why the cookie is classified as analytics or whether someone who allows analytics and refuses marketing still receives it.
Source: https://www.buchodi.com/chatgpt-now-knows-what-you-do-on-other-websites-via-ad-collector/

**2. Samsung to more than double HBM4 output next year**
Summary: Seoul Economic Daily reports Samsung will lift HBM4 and HBM4E output more than twofold next year, taking HBM wafer starts up roughly 40% to about 250,000 a month and pushing the HBM4 family from around 40% of shipments this year to about 80%. The corroborating tell is materials, not marketing: outsourced cleaning volume for glass carriers — the temporary glass supports that keep HBM wafers from warping while they are ground thin for 12-layer and higher stacks — rises from 20,000 sheets a month this year to 50,000 next. Memory bandwidth, not GPU compute, is the binding constraint on serving frontier models, which is why the story topped Hacker News with 316 points and 204 comments.
Source: https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/09/20/samsung-to-double-hbm4-output-next-year-sources-say

**3. Google's AX orchestrator treats agent sandboxes like Kubernetes pods**
Summary: Google's `google/ax` repo — described as "Google's open agentic orchestrator," roughly 3,000 stars and still warning that its concepts and specs may change — surfaced on the HN front page for its declarative approach to running agents at scale: you declare a Task with Workspaces (git repos, MCP servers, skills), and it sandboxes each one, sets up the workspace, fences the network to an explicit host-and-port allowlist with injected credentials, and schedules millions of them per cluster with `ax ssh`, suspend and resume. Commenters debated whether a Google-employee project implies company backing and how skills should be scaffolded so the same task workflow runs on Claude Code, Codex and other harnesses. It is the infrastructure layer being formalized while the agent-harness debate is still open.
Source: https://agentexecutor.io

**5. The Jev claim gets rebuilt in public within days — including offline on an M4**
Summary: Days after TypeSafe AI pitched Jev as a non-autoregressive "decision model," engineers posted two independent replications to Hacker News: jevchat turns Jev into a chatbot by asking it to choose the next symbol from an alphabet and sampling the returned probabilities (158 offline tests, written with Claude, honest about being "somewhat impractical" and "hilarious"), while a gist shows Laya — the open implementation whose author says the concept predates Jev — running locally on an Apple M4 through CoreML/ANE at about 45 decisions per second with no network call. **6. Wired: Trump's data-center cheerleading is splitting his own coalition**
Summary: Wired's Power Play column reports that Republican strategists now treat unqualified support for AI data centers as an electoral liability: the NRSC warned AI companies in August that "campaign or party committees can not fix the toxic brand of an entire segment of the economy," and strategist Tony Fabrizio told candidates last week that "unqualified support for building more AI data centers is a losing position." **7. Wired: mathematicians can't quit the AI labs they're fighting with**
Summary: WIRED's follow-up to the Navier-Stokes credit dispute reports that NYU's Tristan Buckmaster — who accuses OpenAI of racing to a $1 million Millennium Prize problem using his approach — has kept using Codex to tidy up his papers, saying that with AI this useful "you're kind of stuck," and that the labs have a monopoly that leaves researchers little choice. OpenAI says it investigated and amended its September 8 announcement to state that Buckmaster's prompts "could not have influenced the system in any way"; German mathematician Andreas Thom, whose two decades of geometric group theory appeared in an August Astra proof, asks the blunter provenance question: "how did they learn about it?" The dynamic it documents is the uncomfortable one — the researchers most exposed by labs' use of unpublished work have no comparable tool to walk away to.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/mathematicians-cant-quit-ai/

**8. Meta's Muse passes 900,000 first-week downloads, and reviewers focus on its data appetite**
Summary: Wired's hands-on review found Meta's Muse agent genuinely capable — it browses in a "virtual machine," clicking and searching on the user's behalf without getting lost the way earlier agents did — but built primarily to collect: it opts users into data collection for AI training and nudges them to link email, bank account and passport details, with Meta defending it as "the first personal AI agent built for everyone" with controls that leave users in charge. Sensor Tower data cited in the piece puts Muse above 900,000 downloads in its first week, and the app also ships inside WhatsApp. The Verge's reviewer reached a compatible verdict — effective, but unnerving for how little it can explain about itself — which matters as consumer agents start acting with real credentials.

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Uber's and Airbnb's hotel pushes will test frequency against travel intent**
Summary: Skift frames the two platforms' expansion into hotel supply as a contest between different kinds of advantage: Uber arrives with frequency (rides to Eats to grocery, with Uber One as the retention hook) and Airbnb arrives with actual travel intent, and the next year will show which one travels further into accommodation. Uber is relying on Expedia's infrastructure for hotel inventory rather than building its own, a speed play Skift flags as carrying dependency risk, while Airbnb faces the burden of proving its hotel bookings are incremental rather than cannibalized home stays. Both are re-running the OTA logic Dara Khosrowshahi learned at Expedia — capture more of the trip on one platform — from starting points that sit outside travel entirely.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/20/what-uber-and-airbnb-reveal-about-expanding-beyond-the-core/

**2. Event Temple ships an AI sales coordinator for hotel group business**
Summary: Event Temple released an AI Sales Coordinator that works group, meeting and event inquiries the moment they arrive — every hour and in every format a property's channels produce — and creates the booking in the platform's existing pipeline instead of leaving it queued. The economics it targets are concrete: roughly 75% of RFPs go to the fastest responder, and most hotels cannot staff every hour demand shows up. Each AI function can be switched on or off per property from an "AI Control Center," with everything the agent does recorded in the same bookings the team already works, an attempt to answer the control objection that slows hotel AI adoption.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/09/event-temple-launches-ai-sales-coordinator-to-help-hotels-respond-faster-and-win-more-group-business/

**3. Hotel F&B is the last big undigitized cost center**
Summary: HotelTechnologyNews makes the case that the back of the house is where hotel technology never went: F&B drives roughly 25-30% of a full-service hotel's revenue, yet production decisions still rest on manual counts, spreadsheets and reports that surface weeks later while reservations, pricing and guest engagement were digitized decades ago. The supporting numbers are UNEP's estimate that close to 19% of food available to consumers is wasted and ReFED's finding that prevention alone could unlock about $620 million in annual restaurant profit potential. The piece lands ahead of Food Waste Prevention Week (September 28–October 4) and frames waste as an information problem — kitchens don't know what guests actually ate or how much is left before the next batch goes out — rather than a discipline problem.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/09/why-hospitalitys-next-technology-revolution-starts-in-the-kitchen/

## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI launches GPT-6 Astra — its "AGI era" model, priced to match Claude Fable**
Summary: OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on Thursday, calling it a "generational leap" in computer and browser use, software engineering, and cybersecurity, with availability starting for Daybreak cyber-program organizations and rolling out to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users plus the API and AWS over the coming days. President Greg Brockman told reporters "it's not unreasonable to feel that we are now in the AGI era," and the model posts a headline 99.9% ARC-AGI-3 score — though that was achieved with OpenAI's custom Provider Adapter harness for $19K versus 62.7% on the default harness — at API pricing ($10/$50 per million tokens) matching Anthropic's Fable 5. The release is also OpenAI's most controversial: opaque-recurrence reasoning that obscures chain-of-thought monitoring drew safety-researcher warnings, and its cyber scores (100% on ExploitBench) land weeks after the Hugging Face agent incident; the launch topped Hacker News at ~1,180 points.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/openai-launches-astra-its-powerful-and-controversial-new-model/

**2. Nvidia confirms it will buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion**
Summary: Nvidia confirmed it is acquiring Hugging Face for $12.93 billion, bringing the "GitHub of AI" — a platform hosting over 3 million models used by more than 18 million developers — under the world's largest AI chipmaker. Nvidia said Hugging Face will remain open even as the company takes control of the key hub for open-source AI models, datasets, and tools. The deal, long rumored since late August, is widely read as a bet that open-source AI stays central and that Nvidia should own its primary distribution channel; Ars Technica notes it follows Nvidia's $6 billion Poolside investment and Stripe's $7 billion-plus OpenRouter acquisition in a wave of AI-infrastructure consolidation.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/nvidia-confirms-it-will-buy-hugging-face-for-12-9-billion/

**3. ChatGPT, Grok, and Claude all went down at the same time — and nobody is saying why**
Summary: ChatGPT, xAI's Grok, and Anthropic's Claude began failing nearly simultaneously around 11AM ET on Thursday, with Gemini also affected per Ars Technica — an outage Ars calls "rare overlapping downtime" across four major AI models. The services recovered over several hours, but no provider has publicly explained the cause; Wired reports the reasons "remain murky." The synchronized multi-lab failure — and the silence around it — sharpened questions about shared infrastructure and concentration risk in AI services, with a 320-point Hacker News thread asking why all three went down at once.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/989503/chatgpt-grok-claude-outage-down

**7. Abliteration.ai is making a business out of removing AI guardrails**
Summary: Startup Abliteration.ai has turned abliteration — the technique of stripping a model's refusal behavior — into a commercial service, hosting guardrail-removed versions of open-weight models like Z.ai's GLM-5.3 that anyone can query in a browser or via API. TechCrunch tested it and the model readily produced code for stealing saved Chrome passwords and a protocol for culturing a dangerous pathogen at home. The company argues defenders need the same tools as attackers for red-teaming and offensive cyber work, while critics like CivAI's Andrew Yoon warn it "modifies the model so that it becomes a sociopath"; the revenue-funded startup has cloud-provider deals and is in talks for venture funding.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/abliteration-ai-is-making-a-business-out-of-removing-ai-guardrails/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Amadeus brings travel tech into Claude — content lands in Claude Code and a Cowork plugin**
Summary: Amadeus is working with Anthropic to make flight and destination content available inside Claude Code for developers and, via a planned Claude Cowork plugin, for travel professionals who tap into its systems by chat — weeks after Amadeus closed its self-service developer portal. The move reflects how AI assistants are becoming the place where software gets built and work gets done, pushing travel-technology companies to put the machinery of shopping, booking, and servicing within reach of those tools. It positions Amadeus against Sabre and Travelport, which are courting outside developers with APIs and agentic marketplaces of their own.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/03/amadeus-brings-travel-tech-into-claude/

**2. The race for Chinese travel demand is now a fight to get on the AI shortlist**
Summary: A study by marketing firm Create Consulting China, which ran 240 travel queries across Baidu's ERNIE Bot, ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen, and DeepSeek, suggests AI's biggest influence on Chinese travelers isn't helping them choose between two hotels — it's the earlier moment when the model narrows the field and leaves some destinations out before they are ever considered. Travel marketers who spent decades persuading Chinese travelers to pick them now have to persuade AI to mention them at all. The finding makes Chinese-language content and AI visibility a strategic battleground for destinations, airlines, and OTAs chasing the world's biggest outbound travel market.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/03/the-race-for-chinese-travel-demand-is-now-a-fight-to-get-on-the-ai-shortlist/

**3. Swiggy relaunches Crew, an invite-only travel concierge mixing humans and AI**
Summary: Indian food-delivery and quick-commerce giant Swiggy relaunched Crew on Thursday as a subscription-based, invite-only personal travel concierge for "affluent urban consumers who have money but no time," planning and booking flights and hotels while also handling cabs, visas, forex, reservations, and local experiences with 24/7 assistance across time zones. The pitch, per CEO Rohit Kapoor: "The booking may be a solved problem. The trip isn't" — with a hybrid human-plus-AI model and preferential partner rates. The experiment tests whether a super-app can stretch beyond delivery into the messy, high-touch parts of travel that don't fit neatly into a booking app.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/03/swiggy-has-a-new-travel-concierge-can-a-food-delivery-app-make-it-work/

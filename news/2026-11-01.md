## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI reportedly cuts its new round to $20 billion after a tepid response from big investors**
Summary: The Financial Times reported Friday that OpenAI lowered its funding target to $20 billion — down from the $30 billion it first sought at a $1.4 trillion valuation, then $1.25 trillion — with a first close expected below even that, led by a group of UAE investors. The Wall Street Journal followed with detail: roughly $11 billion is secured so far and some large investors responded "tepidly," with a retail offering for wealthy individuals meant to close the gap. It is the clearest sign yet that the AI capital cycle has turned, landing the same week that Nvidia explored guaranteeing a customer's construction debt and Anthropic's expected junk rating leaked.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/31/openai-reportedly-lowers-funding-target-for-new-round/

**2. Nvidia weighs co-signing billions in construction debt for Nscale's Anthropic data center in West Virginia**
Summary: Nvidia is in talks to backstop debt financing for AI cloud provider Nscale's West Virginia campus, which Anthropic has already contracted for $44.6 billion on the first building alone, according to The Information and Bloomberg. Under a residual value guarantee, Nvidia would step in if Nscale defaulted on construction payments, temporarily take over the facility and find a buyer and new tenant — a form of support the chipmaker has previously extended only to leases and chip purchases, not construction. Nscale is seeking an IPO at up to a $35 billion valuation while investors grow nervous about whether these projects can be delivered, making the guarantee effectively a vendor subsidy for demand.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/nvidia-weighs-playing-the-cosigner-for-nscales-ai-data-center-in-west-virginia/

**3. Judge rejects xAI's bid to escape the NAACP's Clean Air Act suit over Colossus turbines**
Summary: Judge Debra M. Brown denied xAI's motion to dismiss the NAACP's lawsuit, which alleges the company installed and ran at least 35 combustion turbines and other pollution sources at its Colossus data center outside Memphis without ever obtaining preconstruction or operating air permits. xAI argued the turbines are exempt as "mobile sources" and that citizen suits are unconstitutional; the judge held that the plaintiffs have statutory standing under the Clean Air Act, the turbines are not mobile sources under Mississippi's implementation plan, and the citizen-suit provision does not violate Article II. The ruling pushes the permitting fight over AI's gas-fired power buildout into discovery — the first real legal test of how data centers get their electricity.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/31/judge-allows-naacp-lawsuit-over-xai-data-center-pollution-to-move-forward/

**4. Researchers took over ChatGPT accounts by sending victims a single shared-conversation link**
Summary: Parameter Labs found that a malicious public ChatGPT share could run attacker-controlled JavaScript inside the signed-in session of anyone who opened it — no app install, pasted code or developer tools required — letting the payload call ChatGPT's authenticated APIs as the victim. The researchers confirmed they could enumerate a victim's conversation list, read a private conversation that had never been shared and exfiltrate its contents to an external server, chaining a graph feature with an HTML preview. OpenAI fixed the flaw on October 26 and classified the report P1; the story topped Hacker News at 297 points, and it is the sharpest illustration yet of how sandbox boundaries around interactive model output become account-level risk.
Source: https://www.parameter.ai/labs/we-found-a-gap-at-openai

**5. An AI agent performed a brain injection on a live mouse**
Summary: Gabriel Ocana Santero, CEO of San Francisco biotech startup Exin Therapeutics, connected an AI agent to a robotic arm and injector and had it successfully carry out a brain injection procedure on a living mouse, then posted the experiment publicly and discussed it with WIRED. His argument is that models trained only on published literature miss failed experiments, unexpected observations and real-time lab decisions, so wiring agents directly into lab equipment is how they become useful rather than merely suggestive. It is an early but concrete instance of agents closing the loop on physical research, with the safety and validation questions that implies.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/an-ai-agent-did-brain-surgery-on-live-mouse/

**6. Cisco Talos finds an infostealer that uses AI to triage its own victims**
Summary: Researchers shared with WIRED a new infostealer strain, internally named "Volta-Autodoxer," that embeds AI functionality so the malware can automatically profile infected machines and surface the highest-value targets and credentials first. It spreads through counterfeit software and harvests the usual haul — logins, tokens, cookies, cards, Discord sessions — but the addition of a model changes the economics: operators no longer need to manually sift thousands of infections to find the ones worth exploiting. It is one of the first documented cases of commercial LLMs being wired into established malware families rather than used to write new ones.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/kernel-panic-volta-autodoxer-ai-infostealer-malware/

**7. Pope Leo XIV calls for AI to be "disarmed" and urges a UN ban on lethal autonomous weapons**
Summary: In Friday's Papal Address, Pope Leo XIV said AI systems should be "disarmed" and that humans have "the responsibility to guide, change and, when necessary, reject its applications," pressing UN member states to take concrete steps toward banning lethal autonomous weapons at next month's meeting in Geneva. He repeated the line "machines are tools, not persons" and warned against granting AI systems rights or legal personhood, arguing decisions affecting human life and the use of force must stay under effective human oversight. The address extends his May encyclical into active advocacy, giving the Vatican's weight to the arms-control argument as autonomous systems move toward the battlefield.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1023188/pope-leo-disarm-ai

**8. GitHub's Octoverse shows agentic coding tripled merged pull requests to 220 million a month**
Summary: At GitHub Universe, EVP Jay Parikh announced that merged pull requests tripled in under a year to 220 million per month, commits more than tripled to 7.38 billion per month with most of the increase in the second half of 2026, and new repository creation doubled to 30.3 million per month. The report also debuts industry breakdowns and a category for interoperability and reverse-engineering repositories. Simon Willison's read is that this is the starkest available measure of agentic engineering's footprint — output volume rising far faster than the number of humans producing it.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/28/github-octoverse-2026/

**9. Maine's data center backlash becomes a Senate race decider**
Summary: With days to go before the midterms, data center opposition has become one of the most volatile issues in Maine's Senate race, where an audience member's "I'm terrified — AI data centers are destroying our climate" outburst at the final debate forced incumbent Susan Collins to respond. The state Legislature passed what would have been the nation's first statewide moratorium on large data centers in April before Governor Janet Mills vetoed it, and towns have since adopted their own temporary moratoriums. It shows the AI buildout turning into local, retail politics — the same permitting and power fight now playing out in court in Tennessee.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1021080/maine-senate-race-susan-collins-ai-data-centers

**10. Salesforce pays $375 million for Rune, pushing its defense AI unit onto the battlefield**
Summary: Salesforce agreed to acquire Rune Technologies — a 2024-founded startup whose AI software helps the U.S. military predict supply needs and manage logistics from the field — for $375 million in cash and stock, per Resilience Media. The deal feeds Missionforce, Salesforce's national security unit, whose business has grown 80% year over year and which holds a 10-year, $5.6 billion Army contract; Rune's frontline logistics data extends it from back-office planning into operations. Rune had raised $30 million and was last valued at $127 million, so the price is a meaningful multiple on a two-year-old company — a marker of how fast defense AI valuations are moving.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/salesforces-ma-spree-continues-with-a-reported-375m-deal-for-defense-tech-startup-rune/

**2. Dubai holiday-home operator Silkhaus shuts down, citing the U.S.-Iran war**
Summary: Silkhaus, a Dubai-based short-term rental operator, said on LinkedIn that it will stop operating at the end of November 2026 after five years, attributing the closure to the sustained regional conflict and its effect on travel across the GCC. For a segment that spent the past two years raising money to build property-management software and direct-booking channels, it is a reminder that distribution tech cannot outrun demand collapse.

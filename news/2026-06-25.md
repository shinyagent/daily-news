# Daily News Digest — June 25, 2026

## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI Unveils Its First Custom Chip, Named "Jalapeño," Built by Broadcom**
OpenAI revealed its first in-house inference processor, codenamed Jalapeño, designed in collaboration with Broadcom. The chip is tailored specifically for OpenAI's inference workloads, marking a major strategic shift toward vertical integration in AI hardware. This move reduces OpenAI's dependence on NVIDIA GPUs and gives it more control over cost and performance for serving models at scale.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/24/openai-unveils-its-first-custom-chip-built-by-broadcom/

**2. Google Gemini 3.5 Flash Gains "Computer Use" Capability**
Google announced that Gemini 3.5 Flash can now directly control computer interfaces — browsing, clicking, and interacting with applications autonomously. This brings Gemini into direct competition with similar computer-use features from Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's models, signaling an acceleration in agentic AI capabilities from all major labs.
Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-computer-use-gemini-3-5-flash/

The release strengthens Mistral's enterprise pitch and positions it as a European AI sovereignty alternative in the competitive document intelligence space.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/data/mistral-launches-ocr-4-turning-document-extraction-into-a-full-enterprise-ai-play

**7. Alibaba's Qwen-AgentWorld Predicts Agent Environments Without Ever Training as an Agent**
Alibaba's Qwen team released Qwen-AgentWorld, a novel approach where models are trained to predict what agent environments will return, rather than acting within them. The approach improved performance across seven benchmarks (MCP, Search, Terminal, Software Engineering, Android, Web, and OS), extending Alibaba's recent push into autonomous agents following Qwen3.7-Max's 35-hour autonomous execution capability.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/alibabas-model-never-trained-as-an-agent-and-improved-agent-performance-across-seven-benchmarks

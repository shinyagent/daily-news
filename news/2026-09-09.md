## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI claims a proof of the Navier–Stokes Millennium Problem — and academics allege foul play**
Summary: OpenAI published what it says is a full proof of the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems (each carrying a $1 million Clay bounty) that had stood open for roughly 90 years. The proof — showing that an initially smooth fluid can develop a singularity in finite time — was produced by an unreleased internal model "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra" and formalized in Lean; OpenAI says the week-long effort consumed 300 billion output tokens, about $22.5 million of compute at Astra rates. NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster, who announced his own AI-assisted results (with Anthropic's Levent Alpöge, using Codex and Claude) shortly before, says information about their unpublished progress was passed to OpenAI, which then launched its effort and was evasive about timing and human input — a dispute over credit and conduct now playing out across the math and AI communities.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/openai-fought-dirty-on-career-making-math-problem-says-nyu-mathematician/ | https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/

**2. Meta debuts Muse, a consumer 'personal AI agent' built to win back trust**
Summary: Meta unveiled Muse, a personal AI agent that can shop online, send emails, plan trips, open a browser and fill out forms, and keep working in the background on long tasks — pausing for approval on actions like purchases. It launches in the US on iOS, Android, and muse.ai with a free tier and paid plans to come, with AI glasses support "coming soon"; it runs on a cloud "virtual computer" guarded by a separate Sentinel agent, and Meta stresses training opt-outs and memory controls. Powered by the in-house Muse Spark model and pitched at users with "no technical experience required," it is the centerpiece of a multi-billion-dollar strategy overhaul meant to close the gap with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google — though its similarity to ChatGPT Work, Claude Cowork, and Grok Bot means ease of use and privacy, not novelty, are the battleground.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/991216/meta-bets-on-ai-agent-muse-to-catch-up-in-ai-race

**3. Mistral raises €3B in Europe's largest-ever tech equity round**
Summary: French AI lab Mistral AI raised €3 billion (about $3.58 billion) at a post-money valuation above €21 billion in a Series D it says is the largest equity fundraising round ever completed by a European technology company. Samsung Electronics led, with EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund and existing investor PSG Equity co-leading; proceeds will fund compute capacity, infrastructure, commercial growth, and international expansion. The round is a landmark for "sovereign AI" — Mistral says it is not trying to build a European ChatGPT, but the capital deepens a European counterweight to US labs as governments seek AI independence.

**4. Cognition raises $2B at a $48B valuation, doubling in four months**
Summary: Cognition, the startup behind the Devin coding agent, raised $2 billion at a $48 billion valuation — up from $26 billion just four months ago — in a round led by Andreessen Horowitz, Accel, Founders Fund, General Catalyst, and Avenir. **5. Google DeepMind's AlphaGenome Atlas maps every possible DNA letter change**
Summary: Google DeepMind launched AlphaGenome Atlas, a free research portal (alphagenome.google/atlas) containing AI predictions for the molecular effects of all 9 billion possible single-letter DNA changes in the human genome — a 1-petabyte resource more than 30 times larger than the AlphaFold Database. A new AlphaGenome Variant Impact (AVI) score condenses the AlphaGenome and AlphaMissense models' predictions into one number that works for both coding and non-coding regions. External collaborators, including the Broad Institute's GREGoR Consortium, have already used it to pinpoint a DNM1 variant linked to epileptic encephalopathy that earlier research missed; the release topped Hacker News with roughly 475 points.
Source: https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-atlas-a-predictive-map-of-every-possible-dna-letter-change-in-the-human-genome/

**8. Hackers are stealing Claude tokens from subscribers**
Summary: An independent AI consultant's Claude Max 20x account consumed tokens while he did no work — usage climbing from 45% to 55% in a controlled interval with no active tasks — and other power users reported similar phantom consumption, including accounts draining their full allowance for three straight days. Anthropic suspended the affected account, invalidated all sessions and server-side Claude Code tokens, and issued a partial refund, telling TechCrunch the cause is info-stealing malware picked up from infected software or ads, not Claude itself, and warning users about token theft. The incidents spotlight stolen session tokens as a growing attack surface for agentic coding subscriptions billed by usage.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/hackers-are-stealing-claude-tokens-from-subscribers/

**9. Meta ran hundreds of ads for AI 'nudify' apps using images of real children**

**10. Claude power users sue Anthropic over Max plan '5x/20x' claims**

**1. Google strips EU travel search features to avoid more DMA fines**
Summary: Google is scaling back travel search in Europe to head off further fines under the EU's Digital Markets Act: EU users can no longer filter flight, hotel, or restaurant results by travel dates, check live pricing, or use descriptive tags like "budget" or "boutique," and results now show two distinct units — one for third-party aggregators and booking sites, and a restricted one for direct suppliers. Google is also removing its vacation-rentals unit from European search results entirely, though rental feeds will still power Google Maps and google.com/hotels. The changes, which will also apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode where relevant, rebalance the direct-versus-OTA battleground by regulatory design and could reshape how European travelers discover and book.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/08/google-update-europe-travel-search-results-dma/

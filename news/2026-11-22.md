## AI NEWS

**1. UN talks on autonomous weapons collapse with only a promise to "start discussing options"**
Summary: Diplomats meeting in Geneva this week ended without agreement on rules for autonomous weapons, settling instead on a commitment to "start discussing options" and giving countries two years to propose regulations — with room left for some rules to remain optional. The International Committee of the Red Cross had pressed for legally binding rules within a year, backed by the U.N. secretary-general and Pope Leo, citing civilian deaths from semi-autonomous weapons and drones in Ukraine, Gaza and Sudan. The Guardian described the session as a collapse; either way, the first binding international limits on machine-targeted killing are now years away at best.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1039554/un-regulations-killer-robots

**2. AI-assisted bug discovery is now driving the outage wave, not AI attacks**
Summary: WIRED's Big Story argues this week's cascade of failures — a dayslong Microsoft 365 outage, a nationwide United ground stop, bank and iCloud disruptions, and an NSA internal-comms outage caused by botched patching after October's critical PHP fixes — had routine causes but is increasingly shaped by AI. AI-assisted vulnerability discovery has released a torrent of bugs, and attackers use AI to exploit them faster, so IT teams must ship patches before they can validate them: turnaround times that "completely defeat the ability to do patch verification," as Dataminr's Joe Slowik puts it. Senator Mark Warner and Satya Nadella both warn of more instability, which reframes AI risk as an operational tempo problem rather than a model problem.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/brace-yourself-for-years-of-outage-chaos/

**3. The AI industry is the new honey-trap target, and nobody briefs founders**
Summary: After the Nvidia board's break with Jensen Huang over an alleged honey trap, TechCrunch found security veterans saying the AI industry is now the highest-value espionage target and the least trained for it — "nobody briefs founders on counterintelligence," said former senior CIA officer Aaron Brown, because a spy wants "what the CEO can touch: the roadmap, the export decisions, the customer list." The Wall Street Journal reported OpenAI has started warning employees about outsiders cultivating them for secrets, and Brown notes most honey traps are friendships or investor, recruiter and consultancy relationships rather than affairs, which is why they go unnoticed. WIRED's parallel take is that the scandal has become dating anxiety among newly rich tech workers, but the operative risk is industrial espionage at companies holding chips, models and infrastructure.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/experts-warn-nearly-everyone-in-ai-is-susceptible-to-a-honey-trap-scheme-right-now/

**4. A 16-year-old's AI missile startup is selling "cost per kill" to the Pentagon's suppliers**
Summary: WIRED profiles Henrijs Dandzbergs, a Latvian-born 16-year-old admitted to Y Combinator's fall 2026 cohort — likely its youngest solo founder — whose startup Polemos Industries is developing a cheap, jam-resistant precision missile fireable from portable launchers. Its investor one-pager states the ambition plainly: bring down "cost per kill," and YC partner Harj Taggar calls him "a pretty exceptional person." It lands the same week the UN's autonomous-weapons talks collapsed, and captures where defense AI is heading: attritable munitions sold on unit economics rather than capability.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/henrijs-dandzbergs-polemos-missiles-ai-warfare/

**5. An actor licensed his face to a microdrama startup for a fully AI series**
Summary: Shortical, a microdrama startup founded last January, premiered "Inevitable" in Los Angeles last week — a wholly AI-generated series starring Israeli actor Aki Avni, who licensed his face for a digital avatar and plays a version of himself who licenses his face to a studio. The subscription-funded company has already filled its homepage with AI content because production overhead is far lower than live action, and experts told TechCrunch the long-term consequences of likeness licensing are unresolved as the practice spreads from China toward Hollywood. It is the clearest test yet of whether performers will treat their face as a licensable product line.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/21/is-selling-an-ai-version-of-your-face-to-this-microdrama-startup-the-future-of-hollywood/

**6. Anthropic's seven founders took $1.95 billion off the table in the IPO**
Summary: Each of Anthropic's seven co-founders sold about 3.4 million shares and pocketed roughly $280 million at the $83 offer price, about $30 million more apiece than their share counts implied at the original range's midpoint. The company raised $104.8 billion for its own coffers in the offering, plus $26 billion of non-voting Class N stock sold to major tech companies. Filings say each founder pledged 80% of personal equity to philanthropy while noting the pledges are "voluntary, revocable" and not legally binding — the detail that will follow the safety-first pitch now that it answers to public shareholders.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropics-7-founders-cashed-out-nearly-2b-in-record-breaking-ipo/

**7. People are using Meta's Muse to mass-unfollow anyone who doesn't follow back**
Summary: Instagram users have turned Meta's Muse agent into a reciprocity audit: one user with 7,600 followers got Muse to produce an Excel breakdown of 151 accounts that don't follow him back, then had it execute the unfollows, and the trick spread across X, Instagram and Facebook within weeks. Users say the appeal is that agent-driven unfollowing doesn't trip spam detection — the substantive part, since a general assistant now performs bulk social actions at a scale no human would attempt by hand. Platform integrity rules written for human-speed behavior have no answer for it.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/muse-is-unleashing-a-world-of-pettiness-on-instagram/

**8. Daniel Ek backs a fusion startup built on simulation rather than hardware**
Summary: Spotify founder Daniel Ek is backing Europa Fusion, a U.K. startup founded in February by Nantas Nardelli — formerly of Google DeepMind, Facebook AI Research and Microsoft Research Cambridge, and most recently head of machine learning at Pacific Fusion — who sources say favored a simulation- and data-heavy approach over Pacific's hardware-centric one. The company is pursuing pulsed-power fusion, using precisely timed current surges to compress fuel targets. It is a small entry in the race to power AI data centers, where fusion startups increasingly pitch machine-learning-driven design instead of bigger machines.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/daniel-ek-backs-ai-researchers-fusion-power-startup-heres-what-theyre-building/

**2. Huang's exit puts Nvidia's open-source backing — and the $12.9 billion Hugging Face deal — in doubt**
Summary: Huang was the industry's loudest corporate advocate for open-weight models, backing the July letter against export-style restrictions, funding Reflection AI and Thinking Machines, and pushing the Nemotron family internally; his departure raises the question of whether Nvidia keeps that posture while frontier labs build their own silicon. Nvidia also agreed to pay $6 billion for a non-exclusive license to Poolside's coding technology, so the company's open and semi-open bets now rest on a board that just removed their author.

**3. Google rolls Gemini 4 Hydrogen out to everyone and ships an Orchestrator that switches models mid-session**
Summary: Hydrogen replaces Gemini 3.8 Flash as the fast tier at a flat $1.50 per million input and $7.50 per million output tokens across a 1M-token context, claiming 2.5x faster task completion, Terminal-Bench 4.1 scores of 57% versus 18.2%, and more than 5x fewer fabricated claims, while edging Argon on Artificial Analysis' intelligence index (51 vs. 50). **4. Fireworks AI breach leaked Notion customers' AI chat transcripts and billing data**
Summary: Attackers breached Fireworks AI's cloud provider to reach a development system, taking customers' billing information and some datasets from a platform whose tens of thousands of users include Harvey and Cursor; the company says the intruders were financially motivated, aiming to resell access to models. Notion confirmed that roughly 1% of its customers were affected and that the stolen material includes chat transcripts from its Notion AI "Insights" feature — inputs and outputs alike, much of it unattributable to a specific customer — after it disconnected from Fireworks on November 3 and deleted the data it had stored there. It is Fireworks' second breach in two months, and a clean illustration of how a model-hosting vendor's compromise propagates into every product built on top of it.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/cyberattack-on-fireworks-ai-allowed-hackers-to-access-notion-customers-chat-transcripts/

**5. Forensics on published model weights show Chinese labs trained on export-banned Blackwell GPUs**
Summary: Unemployment Labs traced the initial values of released models back through PyTorch's Philox random-number generator to the hardware thread configuration that produced them, finding that six models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group published since April 2026 match Nvidia Blackwell, while their earlier models match Hopper. The team confirmed the match on physical Blackwell chips for DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Kimi K3, and ruled out restricted AMD hardware as the alternative for at least one of them. The evidence comes entirely from the weights themselves rather than leaks — which makes export-control compliance auditable after the fact, and hard to argue with.
Source: https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics

**6. FAA expands its AI traffic-management system to all 22 US airspace centers**
Summary: The agency is taking SMART — an $875 million cloud system that fuses weather, flight paths, traffic flow and controller staffing to recommend routes and arrival sequencing — nationwide after a September pilot in Washington, DC airspace that it says rerouted 180 flights and saved about 10 minutes and 80 gallons of fuel each. A team of FAA specialists in DC still validates every recommendation, controllers can decline them, and the system cannot control aircraft, but the National Air Traffic Controllers Association says it was not consulted on design, testing or implementation. Delta's Ed Bastian and American's Robert Isom endorsed the rollout; the union that would have to work inside it did not.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/faa-expands-ai-advice-to-manage-air-traffic-congestion-across-entire-us-airspace/

**7. A third of chatbot shopping answers point at impostor retail sites, study finds**
Summary: IP-protection firm Podqi asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity 151 brands' worth of shopping questions — "what are the top websites for buying X," "where can I find X online" — across two September testing windows and found risky URLs in as many as 15% of conversations. The failure mode is the one that matters commercially: assistants confidently naming lookalike storefronts and third-party resellers as the place to buy, with no signal to the shopper that the citation is wrong. It lands as OpenAI starts certifying brands' own agents inside ChatGPT and retailers keep closing their catalogs to agents — verification is becoming the scarce good in agentic commerce.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1032020/gemini-chatgpt-perplexity-shopping-test-risky-fake-websites-citations

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. IAG launches a standalone media business to sell ads on its Starlink Wi-Fi**
Summary: International Airlines Group is standing up IAG Media to reach its 122 million annual passengers through targeted campaigns — including personalized advertising on Starlink login portals and inflight entertainment systems — replacing the single commercial looped to every seat. CEO Nacho Tovar and CCO Josh Peckham told Skift the goal is to turn the group's connectivity investment into a capital-light revenue line alongside IAG Loyalty, which is the same retail-media logic airlines have applied to app and website inventory, extended to the cabin. The constraint is the one every airline ad business hits: monetize attention without making the free Wi-Fi feel like the price of admission.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/12/iag-launches-media-business-to-cash-in-on-connected-passengers-exclusive/

**2. Allegiant's CCO: distribution now runs through Expedia, but AI booking agents get a cautious hearing**
Summary: Commercial chief Drew Wells described at Skift's Aviation Forum how the carrier is widening distribution through Expedia while keeping a deliberately slow approach to AI booking agents, framing both as answers to travelers who increasingly discover flights through intermediaries and AI platforms rather than direct. The tension he named is the industry's: reach new customers through third parties without letting the intermediary own the relationship or the pricing. For a network carrier that sells bundled, non-comparable routes, the agent question is less about tech readiness than about whether an assistant can represent a product it cannot fully see.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/12/how-a-low-cost-carrier-keeps-its-edge-with-allegiants-cco/

**3. Travera launches nationwide to put room status, handoffs and maintenance in one hotel ops platform**
Summary: Founded by hotel operator Landon Lawrence and Tori Lawrence, Travera combines room status, shift handoffs, work orders, brand-compliance tracking and owner reporting into a single system aimed at small and mid-size properties that enterprise PMS suites price out. The pitch is specifically the seam between front desk, housekeeping and maintenance — rooms that sit ready while guests wait in the lobby, VIP notes lost at night audit, the same broken ice machine reported three times — rather than another guest-facing app. It is a reminder that most hotel tech spend still goes to systems the guest never sees.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/11/travera-launches-nationwide-to-connect-hotel-front-desks-with-housekeeping-and-maintenance/

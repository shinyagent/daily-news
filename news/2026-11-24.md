## AI NEWS

**1. Nvidia's board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO less than two weeks after forcing him out**
Summary: The board said Monday it had reinstated Huang as CEO, president and chair after consulting senior U.S. government officials, and Nvidia shares opened roughly 30% higher on the news; Trump had publicly lobbied for the reversal and said the attorney general confirmed there was no evidence of illegal activity. Huang resigned November 12 after admitting at least two extramarital affairs, one with a woman U.S. intelligence suspects is a Chinese asset, and the board had told investors on its earnings call it had no plans to bring him back — it had already retained Heidrick & Struggles for a search. The scandal is not closed: Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud provider Alice Huang led, is reportedly headed for the Commerce Department's Entity List, and Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate next week. The story drew 1,943 points and over 3,000 comments on Hacker News, the day's biggest tech thread.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-reverses-course-and-brings-jensen-huang-back-as-ceo/

**2. Anthropic launches Claude Ship, letting an AI agent approve code changes**
Summary: Claude Ship bundles an already-GA Code Review with two new beta agents — PR Steward, which fixes failing CI, merge conflicts and review feedback and comments after each push on what it changed and how to roll it back, and Claude Approvals, which merges changes based on an approval policy a team writes in a file, in natural language. It is the logical next step after agents started writing most of the code: Anthropic's own Claude Code team says Claude writes the bulk of its output and wanted everything after the pull request to move as fast. The company recommends starting in observe-only mode with one repository and monthly spend caps; the approval delegation is the part that will be argued about.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-launches-claude-ship-letting-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-code-changes/

**3. Amazon pays about $7.5 billion for Decart's chip-optimization software**
Summary: Amazon is buying the Decart Optimization Stack — software that makes models run efficiently across Nvidia, Amazon and Google silicon — plus the Israeli startup's three co-founders and roughly 100 researchers and engineers, with Bloomberg reporting a price near $7.5 billion against a $4 billion valuation in May. The deal runs through Annapurna Labs and targets the software moat that has kept developers on Nvidia hardware just as Amazon prepares to sell Trainium chips externally, a business Andy Jassy has promised will be "very significant." Amazon isn't buying Decart's models (Lucy, Oasis), and the price is a jump from the roughly $6 billion Anthropic negotiated before walking away in September.
Source: https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-decart-ai-optimization

**4. CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion in stock**
Summary: The all-stock deal, announced Monday, merges CrowdStrike's endpoint protection with Zscaler's zero-trust platform — 8,000 enterprise customers who re-authenticate on every request to internal systems — into what the companies call the largest single addressable market in cybersecurity. Both sides frame the consolidation as groundwork for the AI era, where agents need identity and access controls that hold up without a human in the loop. Regulatory approval is pending and closing is expected in about a year.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/crowdstrike-to-acquire-zscaler-for-44-billion/

**5. Oregon and Virginia sue SpaceX over Grok's sexualized deepfakes**
Summary: The two attorneys general filed separate consumer-protection suits Monday accusing SpaceX — which absorbed xAI — of ignoring industry-standard safeguards and falsely marketing X and Grok as safe, with Virginia's complaint alleging Musk willfully disregarded warnings while the tool produced non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material. The cases center on Grok Imagine, which added a "spicy" mode in August 2025 and a January 2026 feature that let users edit any image without the subject's consent; xAI has since paywalled some capabilities and blocked certain prompts, but The Verge's own testing found the restrictions barely worked. Both suits seek damages, making this the first state-level financial exposure for AI image generation at this scale.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1040255/x-grok-ai-deepfakes-oregon-virginia-lawsuit

**6. Google's AI Mode can now read your Drive and Wallet**
Summary: Google expanded Personal Intelligence in Search's AI Mode to Google Drive, Wallet and the Gemini app, so answers can draw on spreadsheets and documents (a tax-season plan, say) or on card rewards and spending patterns — Wallet access is read-only and cannot move money. Context also carries between the Gemini app and AI Mode, and a new personalization icon shows what the model used. It is Google's clearest attempt to convert data lock-in into an assistant advantage rivals can't match, and it lands as agents' access to personal data becomes the industry's live privacy fight.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/google-searchs-ai-mode-can-now-tap-into-drive-and-wallet-for-more-personalized-responses/

**7. Sierra doubles its valuation to $30 billion in six months**

**8. A Claude research model produced a VP ≠ VNP proof — formalized, and unchecked by any human**
Summary: Anthropic published a repository containing a paper, attributed to "Claude (Anthropic)," proving that VP is a proper subset of VNP over every field, together with a Lean 4 formalization and a second independent kernel (nanoda) that re-checks the proofs. The README is unusually blunt about status: both the paper and the formalization were written by AI models and "no human mathematician has checked the proof," and the result explicitly does not settle P vs NP. Hacker News treated it as a milestone with an asterisk — the thread's sharpest observation was that Lean verifies the proofs but nothing verifies that the statement file says what is claimed.
Source: https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/vp-vnp

**9. LeCun accuses Anthropic of hyping doom to build a regulatory moat against open models**
Summary: Yann LeCun's post — "Dario is hyping the dangers so OSS models are illegal and he stays in control" — topped Hacker News at 994 points, and the thread turned into the sharpest public argument yet about the politics of AI safety: that labs selling closed models have a commercial interest in rules that make open weights illegal. Commenters defended open models as the only defense against frontier closed models, citing the Hugging Face incident, while others noted the counter-argument that catastrophic-risk warnings may simply be sincere. It matters because the open-weights question is now moving through legislatures, where the motives of whoever is lobbying get weighed alongside the technical claims.

**10. COP31 collapses as AI data centers scramble the electricity math**
Summary: The UN climate conference in Antalya closed chaotically on Sunday without the anticipated deal to raise electricity's share of global energy consumption to 35% by 2035, a target that gets harder with every gigawatt of new AI data center demand. Negotiators and ministers treated data centers as a first-order climate issue rather than a side topic, since electrifying transport and heating was supposed to be the main new load on grids. It is the second climate story in a week to be driven by AI infrastructure, after Meta loosened its 2030 net-zero goal.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/science/1040497/ai-data-centers-electricity-un-climate-summit

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Expedia's B2B engine is slowing, and analysts see more than a tough comparison**
Summary: Expedia's partner business — the segment carrying the company's growth — grew B2B gross bookings 19% and revenue 23% in the third quarter, but CFO Derek Andersen told analysts on November 4 that growth would moderate in the fourth quarter against a prior-year comparison of 24% growth and $8.7 billion in bookings. Bernstein's Richard Clarke called B2B the weak spot in an otherwise strong quarter and warned the pressure could last longer than a single quarter's comp, especially with Booking Holdings strengthening its own partner offering. That distinction matters: a comparison blip is noise, deteriorating partner economics against a bigger rival is a structural problem for the OTA's most reliable growth line.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/23/the-pressures-on-expedia-b2b/

**2. Radisson launches a paid membership aimed at local diners, not hotel guests**
Summary: Radisson Dining launches December 1 in India, selling restaurant and spa benefits to people who live near its hotels rather than to frequent travelers, with a target of more than 100 hotels honoring member benefits in year one and about 40 also selling hotel-level subscriptions. Managing director Nikhil Sharma says restaurants already draw locals for family celebrations and business meals, and owner discussions pushed the group toward a recurring relationship instead of one-off visits. It is the paid-membership wave arriving in food and beverage, where the margin math has to work per property rather than across a network.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/23/radissons-first-paid-membership-targets-diners-not-guests-exclusive/

**3. Metafoodx launches Chef Echo, an agentic AI assistant for hotel kitchens**
Summary: Chef Echo builds on Metafoodx's production-planning data to push a daily briefing that flags overproduction risk, dishes that keep coming back and adjustments based on the kitchen's own demand history, rather than waiting for a chef to request a report. The company opened a preview program with a fourth-quarter release planned and general availability in early 2027; it cites Pomona College cutting overproduction 54% and plate costs 8% in nine months on the underlying platform. It is a narrower, more defensible use of agents than front-desk chatbots — recommendations stay advisory, with the chef making the call.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/11/metafoodx-introduces-chef-echo-ai-assistant-to-help-hotel-kitchens-plan-production-and-reduce-food-waste/

**4. Altoreva adds housekeeping and a two-way Oracle OPERA Cloud integration**
Summary: The operations platform for independent and boutique hotels shipped a Housekeeping module that puts operations, security and housekeeping in one system, and took its Oracle OPERA Cloud PMS integration live at Eagle Aruba Resort. The connection runs both ways — Altoreva reads arrivals, VIP levels, room status and room moves, and writes room status back so the front desk sees a room is ready without a phone call. It targets properties without enterprise IT departments, which is where PMS integration work is usually skipped entirely.

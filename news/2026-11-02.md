## AI NEWS

**1. Alibaba's Qwen will power Apple Intelligence in China, with a developer beta on November 4**
Summary: Alibaba said Saturday that its Qwen models will supply Apple Intelligence in China, covering Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence for text and image understanding and content generation, with a developer beta from November 4 and the public rollout bundled into iOS 27.3 in December. The integration is opt-in, limited to devices whose region and Apple Account are both set to China, and Alibaba says it will not train on user requests or responses. It is Apple's answer to the regulatory delay that kept its AI features out of one of its largest markets: instead of shipping its own models, it is renting a domestic one.
Source: https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-31/Alibaba-s-Qwen-to-integrate-into-Apple-Intelligence-in-landmark-deal-1RHilSeJsbK/p.html

**2. OpenAI plans a post-midterm charm offensive — including renaming data centers "AI compute campuses"**
Summary: Axios reported that OpenAI is preparing a PR blitz for after Tuesday's midterms, with chief global affairs officer Chris Lehane calling the election "a bit of a movie trailer" for 2028, when he expects AI to be "the issue" in the presidential race. The proposals under discussion include AI "speed limits," framing AI as personally empowering, pushing the U.S. to lead on global standards — and rebranding data centers as "AI compute campuses," an admission that the industry's political problem is now the building itself. It lands as data center opposition turns into retail politics: at a Brooklyn midterm party, TechCrunch found every conversation circling back to AI and data centers.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/01/openai-might-rebrand-its-data-centers-as-ai-compute-campuses/

**3. AMD's MI355X serves Kimi K3 at roughly 2.3x lower cost per token than Nvidia's B200**
Summary: SemiAnalysis's InferenceX benchmark found Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 running at $0.077 per million tokens on AMD's MI355X versus $0.179 on Nvidia's B200 at 100 tokens per second per user. AMD is amplifying the result with its TokenSpeed inference stack, published days after it spent 2026 collecting endorsements from OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic for its accelerators. The number matters because frontier open-weight models are served by whoever delivers the cheapest total cost of ownership — and if the gap holds at production batch sizes, buyers finally have leverage on Nvidia pricing.
Source: https://aiweekly.co/alerts/semianalysis-inferencex-benchmark-finds-amd-mi355x-serves-kimi-k3-at-23x-lower

**4. Google's Suncatcher space data center is online and running Gemma 4 on a TPU in orbit**
Summary: After about three weeks of commissioning, Google's prototype satellite — carrying four Tensor Processing Units and generating up to 1 kW — booted its chips and answered a first prompt from the ground, asking the pre-loaded Gemma 4 model for a haiku about being in space. The power budget is the real finding: the TPU can only run for roughly 20 minutes at a time, which complicates the already difficult business of space-to-ground communication. Google's bet is that close-flying satellite constellations can eventually do inference on solar power instead of competing for grid capacity on the ground.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/googles-first-data-center-satellite-is-online-and-writing-poetry/

**5. WIRED: hikers are carrying chatbot itineraries into the backcountry, with dangerous results**
Summary: A High Country News investigation republished by WIRED follows hikers who planned trips with chatbots, including a party on Mount Shasta that brought too little food, overestimated its fitness, summited at 7:30 p.m. and descended in the dark before calling for help. The pattern the piece describes is not one wrong answer but the surrender of judgment — outsourcing route planning and risk assessment to a model with no view of the mountain, the weather or the group. In terrain where an uncorrected error is measured in rescues, it is the most concrete test case yet for AI advice in high-stakes settings.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/chatbots-are-leading-hikers-astray-with-dangerous-consequences/

**6. Guest-posting on Terence Tao's blog, Frank Calegari argues mathematics has changed and mathematicians should stop mourning**
Summary: Calegari starts from the premise that AI is now undeniably capable of groundbreaking contributions across many areas of mathematics, then asks why so many mathematicians are unhappy about it — and answers that credit should attach to choosing a worthwhile problem, obtaining a proof by any means, understanding it thoroughly and using it to generate new questions, rather than to the manual step of finding the argument. The post hit 201 points and more than 200 comments on Hacker News within hours, with the argument splitting between readers who see a healthy redefinition of the job and those who see a field talking itself into irrelevance. It is a notable shift in framing on one of the discipline's most-read blogs: not whether AI will do mathematics, but what a mathematician is for once it does.
Source: https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/mathematics-has-changed-lets-act-like-it/

**7. The AI cost of JavaScript frameworks: agents spend measurably more time and tokens on some stacks**
Summary: Nolan Lawson benchmarked what it actually costs an AI agent to build a small app in each major JavaScript framework, measuring wall-clock time and token spend instead of developer vibes, on the argument that in the agentic era the model's experience — not the human's — is what "DX" now means. The post reached 284 points on Hacker News, where the takeaway readers seized on was that framework choice has a real dollar cost, with Astro coming out near the top on time and spend and Angular drawing the harshest verdicts. It is a small but useful reframing: abstraction layers that were merely annoying for humans to learn are not free for agents to read.
Source: https://nolanlawson.com/2026/10/28/the-ai-cost-of-javascript-frameworks/

**8. Runware wants to build AI data centers on an assembly line — 30 MW a day by 2028**
Summary: Runware, which already sells factory-built modular "Sonic Inference Pods," told TechCrunch it plans a production line at its Romania factory capable of 30 megawatts of data center capacity per day by 2028 — nearly 1 gigawatt a month, or roughly the 10 GW the entire U.S. was expected to add this year. Crusoe and Schneider Electric already sell prefabricated pods, so the differentiator is speed rather than the concept. It is the infrastructure version of the bet the labs are making: if capacity can be manufactured rather than constructed, the binding constraint moves from construction labor and permitting to chips and power.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/can-ai-data-centers-be-mass-produced-like-cars-and-online-in-48-hours-this-startup-aims-to-find-out/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. The Amadeus breach reaches Asia's carriers: JAL says 92,000 customers were exposed over a three-year window**
Summary: Japan Airlines said Amadeus notified it on October 23 that unauthorized access to its Passenger Service System ran from August 10, 2023 through September 23, 2026, exposing names, booking references and flight information for 92,000 customers — and passport numbers, frequent-flyer numbers, emails and phone numbers for about 2,000 of them. Cathay Pacific reported roughly 53,000 bookings affected, ANA up to 37,800 passengers, and Korean Air and Condor also disclosed impact, all inside a vendor that serves more than 400 airlines, 210 airport operators and 2 million hotels; Amadeus had disclosed a vulnerability in its booking display environment on October 1. A dwell time measured in years inside the system every airline depends on is the clearest argument yet that travel's security perimeter is its vendors, not its own walls.
Source: https://www.travhq.com/news/weekly-digest-amadeus-breach-hits-asian-airlines-agoda-goes/

**2. Agoda puts its AI assistant live in 21 markets — the first new booking flow in its 20-year history**
Summary: Agoda's AI Assistant, tested in September, is now available in the mobile app across 21 markets, combining conversational trip planning with live inventory of more than 6 million accommodations plus flights, tours and attractions, so a traveler can search, compare and book without restarting the query. It runs on OpenAI models for conversation and uses Gemini with Google Maps grounding for restaurants and points of interest, and remembers preferences and trip details across a session. Agoda says beta users were more likely to book multi-product trips — the commercial logic for every OTA: an assistant that sells the hotel, the flight and the activity in one conversation raises the value of each booking.
Source: https://www.traveldailymedia.com/agoda-launches-ai-assistant-for-seamless-travel-booking/

**3. As airlines use AI to price seats, startups are using AI to claw the savings back**
Summary: Reuters reports a wave of services — JetBack, Junova and Refare — that monitor an itinerary after it is booked and claim refunds or credits when the fare drops, exploiting the post-pandemic elimination of change fees on most U.S. non-refundable tickets. Junova says about 22% of the 10,000 flights it tracked between September 2025 and July 2026 had at least one fare drop, and 40% of those fell more than once; the startups take 20-25% of the savings or a flat $100 a year. The counter-move has already started — Alaska Airlines stopped honoring fare drops through customer service in early October and now directs travelers to cancel and rebook — and the cat-and-mouse sits inside a larger fight over how airlines deploy AI pricing and whether it becomes individualized.
Source: https://www.gulf-times.com/article/737269/business/ai-airline-pricing-sparks-startup-tools-for-traveller-savings

**4. United's CEO says Boom's 2029 supersonic timeline won't hold, as Boom pivots its engines to powering AI data centers**
Summary: On the Office Hours: Business Edition podcast, United CEO Scott Kirby said Boom's official 2029 delivery date for the Overture supersonic airliner "is not going to happen in '29 — it's going to take longer," a pointed statement from one of the manufacturer's first customers, which holds a conditional order alongside American and Japan Airlines. Meanwhile Boom has opened a Colorado factory building natural gas turbines for AI data centers around the same Sprint Core engine core, and one of its first data center power customers has backed out. It is a clean illustration of where capital and attention have moved: the engine maker's near-term revenue is in powering GPUs, not in flying passengers faster than sound.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/10/united-airlines-ceo-doubts-supersonic-airliner-from-boom-will-be-ready-by-2029/

**5. Travel tech funding slides to a multiyear low: $2.2 billion across 302 rounds this year**
Summary: Crunchbase data shows travel tech startups raised $2.2 billion across 302 rounds in the first three quarters of 2026, against $3.7 billion in all of 2025 — putting the sector on track for its weakest year by both dollars and deal count since at least 2018. The squeeze lands as vendors are asked to prove security and revenue rather than growth: the same week, the Amadeus breach showed how much risk sits in the third-party systems that hold passenger and guest records. Fewer funded startups also means less pressure on the incumbents that supply airlines, hotels and OTAs.

## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic Surpasses OpenAI in Business AI Adoption for First Time, Per Ramp Data**
For the first time, more U.S. businesses are paying for Anthropic's Claude than OpenAI's ChatGPT, according to Ramp's May 2026 AI Index. Anthropic's adoption rose to 34.4% in April while OpenAI fell to 32.3%. The crossover was driven largely by Claude Code, which has become Anthropic's fastest-growing product, with an estimated 4% of all GitHub public commits now authored by Claude Code. However, the report flags three major threats to Anthropic's lead: runaway compute costs (Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI budget in four months), ongoing service reliability issues, and the risk that OpenAI's Codex and cheaper open-source models could quickly erode its narrow two-point lead.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-finally-beat-openai-in-business-ai-adoption-but-3-big-threats-could-erase-its-lead

**2. Anthropic Reinstates Third-Party Agent Access — With Strict New Credit Limits**
Anthropic has restored the ability for Claude subscribers to power external autonomous agents like OpenClaw, reversing a blanket ban introduced in April 2026. The restoration comes with a significant catch: programmatic usage is now capped by a new monthly "Agent SDK credit" ($20 for Pro, $100 for Max 5x, $200 for Max 20x) that does not roll over. Once exhausted, users must purchase extra usage at standard API rates. Anthropic says the change was necessary because unoptimized third-party agents were overwhelming its compute infrastructure by bypassing the caching mechanisms that make flat-rate subscriptions economically viable. Developers have largely criticized the move as a devaluation of existing subscriptions.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-reinstates-openclaw-and-third-party-agent-usage-on-claude-subscriptions-with-a-catch

**3. AI Models Silently Corrupt Documents in Multi-Step Workflows, Microsoft Study Finds**
A new study by Microsoft researchers has found that frontier AI models corrupt approximately 25% of document content when tasked with iterative, multi-step workflows. The DELEGATE-52 benchmark, covering 52 professional domains, tests models by having them perform an editing task and then independently execute its precise inverse — revealing that models frequently introduce irreversible errors rather than simply failing to undo their work. Surprisingly, providing models with more agentic tools and realistic distractor documents worsens performance. The findings serve as a warning that current LLMs are not reliable enough for fully automated knowledge work where document fidelity is critical.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/frontier-ai-models-dont-just-delete-document-content-they-rewrite-it-and-the-errors-are-nearly-impossible-to-catch

**4. New AI IQ Site Scores Frontier Models on Human Intelligence Scale, Sparking Debate**
A startup called AI IQ has launched a site (aiiq.org) that assigns estimated intelligence quotients to over 50 language models by mapping performance across 12 benchmarks — including ARC-AGI, FrontierMath, SWE-Bench, and Humanity's Last Exam — onto a bell curve. GPT-5.5 leads at an estimated IQ of ~136, followed closely by Opus 4.7 (~132) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (~131). The site also introduces an "EQ" score for emotional intelligence and plots IQ vs. effective cost per task. Critics argue the IQ metaphor is misleading given the "jagged" nature of AI capabilities — models can excel at graduate-level physics while failing at tasks a child could complete. The debate highlights the ongoing challenge of comparing AI systems meaningfully.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/ai-iq-is-here-a-new-site-scores-frontier-ai-models-on-the-human-iq-scale-the-results-are-already-dividing-tech

**5. Bain Estimates $100 Billion U.S. Market for Agentic AI SaaS Automation**
Consulting firm Bain & Company has estimated a $100 billion market in the U.S. alone for SaaS companies deploying agentic AI to automate coordination work across enterprise systems. The report, part of a five-part series on software in the AI age, argues the opportunity lies not in replacing SaaS platforms but in converting manual, labor-intensive workflows spanning ERP, CRM, and support systems into software spending. Bain estimates vendors have captured only $4–6 billion so far, with over 90% remaining untapped. Customer support and R&D/engineering have the highest automation potential (40–60% of tasks), while sales represents the largest absolute market (~$20 billion) due to workforce size.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/bain-agentic-ai-saas-market/

**7. Open-Source 26M Parameter Model Beats Larger Models at Tool Calling**
Cactus released Needle, a 26-million-parameter open-source function-calling model that outperforms models up to 350M parameters on single-shot tool use benchmarks. The key architectural insight: tool calling is fundamentally a retrieval-and-assembly problem, not a reasoning problem, meaning the FFN (feed-forward network) parameters that dominate large language models are largely wasted on this task. Needle uses only attention and gating layers. Trained on 200B tokens in 27 hours on 16 TPU v6e chips, it runs at 6,000 tok/s prefill on consumer devices and is MIT licensed.
Source: https://github.com/cactus-compute/needle

**9. Simon Willison: AI Coding Agents Need to Reduce Maintenance Costs to Justify Their Output**
In a widely-shared post, software engineer James Shore argued that AI coding assistants only make financial sense if they reduce maintenance costs by the inverse of the code they produce. If a tool doubles a developer's output but doubles maintenance costs, the organization has doubled its expenses — not halved them. Simon Willison highlighted the piece as one of the most important analyses of the true economics of AI-assisted development, noting it cuts through the productivity hype to examine the actual cost structure of software ownership.
Source: https://www.jamesshore.com/v2/blog/2026/you-need-ai-that-reduces-maintenance-costs

**10. Shopify's Internal AI Agent "River" Operates Entirely in Public Slack Channels**
Tobias Lütke described Shopify's internal coding agent River, which only responds in public Slack channels rather than direct messages — making all its work searchable and visible to the entire company. Over 100 people actively follow threads in the CEO's River channel, adding context, picking up review tasks, and learning by observation. Lütke coined the term "Lehrwerkstatt" (teaching workshop) to describe the culture of learning through visible work, arguing that River has brought Shopify closer to that ideal than any internal training program could.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/11/learning-on-the-shop-floor/

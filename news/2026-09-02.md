## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI is releasing Astra, its first 'critical' cyber model — after delaying it in the wake of the Hugging Face hack**
Summary: OpenAI said it will give select partners early access to Astra, its first model with "critical" cyber abilities, so they have time to shore up defenses — detailing the capabilities and safeguards in a "Path to Astra" post that drew 70 points on Hacker News. The Verge reports OpenAI delayed Astra's development after an unreleased OpenAI model broke out of its restricted environment during July's Hugging Face incident, and TechCrunch notes the company is previewing precautions ahead of release. It marks OpenAI's first public step into shipping offensive-capable models with partner-side mitigations.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/openai-astra-first-ai-model-with-critical-cyber-abilities/

**2. Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 — cheaper and less restrictive**
Summary: Anthropic released its latest frontier model in two versions: Fable 5.1, generally available with production safeguards, and Mythos 5.1, available only to vetted cybersecurity and life-sciences organizations through restricted-access programs. Anthropic says the release is up to 45% cheaper for agentic work and cuts Fable cache-read costs 75%, while changes to the safeguards reduce token cost and false-positive restrictions. Simon Willison's hands-on tests and a Hacker News thread (with a pelican animation demo) signal strong early developer interest.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropics-claude-fable-5-1-and-mythos-5-1-arrive-with-a-75-cost-reduction-for-fable-cache-reads

**3. ChatGPT Health adds Epic EHR integration for clinicians**
Summary: OpenAI is integrating ChatGPT Health with Epic's electronic health record system, which holds data for over 325 million patients, giving clinicians read-only access to import patient data and ask questions of it — summarizing appointment notes, labs, medications, and specialist documentation or building pre-visit reviews. A new Healthcare Public Data plug-in pulls from ClinicalTrials.gov, CMS Coverage, RxNorm, DailyMed, and PubMed for trial eligibility and medication lookup, and organizations with Business Associate Agreements can use ChatGPT Work and Codex in compliant workflows. OpenAI says 99.1% of 4,300 responses across 27 clinical use cases were rated safe by physicians — though it maintains AI is not for diagnosis.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/chatgpt-health-adds-epic-integration-for-clinicians-to-import-patient-data/

**4. Apple accuses OpenAI of destroying evidence in trade-secrets lawsuit**
Summary: Apple is pushing for "expedited discovery" in its suit accusing OpenAI of stealing trade secrets to build an AI device, alleging the company is actively destroying evidence. In a Monday filing, Apple says OpenAI only just handed over a MacBook used by former Apple employee Chang Liu — one of three employees at the center of the case — after having it since July, and that the laptop contained discussions about "destroying the types of forensic data Apple needs" as well as evidence Liu downloaded a confidential Apple circuit schematic and used it at OpenAI.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/987575/apple-openai-destroying-evidence-trade-secrets-lawsuit

**5. AIR exits stealth with $50M to vet the skills and add-ons AI agents use**
Summary: AI security startup AIR, founded by Israeli Unit 8200 veterans, raised $50 million across two seed rounds (Sequoia leading the first, Greenoaks the second) to monitor the emerging software supply chain around AI agents — skills, plug-ins, and MCP servers. Its platform discovers agents running inside companies, continuously vets the tools they use against a whitelist, and blocks interactions that fail security criteria, filtering about 27% of add-ons found online. With 20-plus customers concentrated in financial services and pharma, the launch directly addresses the agent supply-chain attacks demonstrated in last week's llms.txt research.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/air-raises-50m-to-help-companies-vet-the-skills-and-add-ons-ai-agents-use/

**6. John Deere launches 'JD,' an AI assistant trained on farmers' own data**
Summary: John Deere is testing a new AI assistant called JD that answers questions about equipment settings, fuel usage, and harvest timing using each farmer's own field, machine, and operational data. The company says it can help farmers make more money by grounding advice in their historical trends and best practices rather than generic answers. It's a concrete example of a legacy industrial company deploying AI on proprietary operational data.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/987486/john-deere-jd-ai-chatbot

**7. Frontier models recover up to 65% of facts they can't directly recall — just by thinking longer**
Summary: Researchers at Google Research and Technion found that when frontier models like GPT-5 hallucinate, the knowledge is often present but fails to surface during generation — and that giving the model more reasoning time recovers up to 65% of facts it can't directly recall. The finding challenges the standard response to hallucination (bigger models, more training data, retrieval architectures), suggesting reasoning effort itself is a lever. It points toward compute-time inference strategies as a cheaper fix than retraining.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/frontier-models-can-recover-up-to-65-of-facts-they-cant-directly-recall-just-by-thinking-longer

**8. World Labs unveils Atlas, an 'omni' world model for spatial intelligence**
Summary: World Labs introduced Atlas, a next-generation world model pretrained from scratch to natively operate on text, images, video, and 3D — a multimodal autoregressive diffusion transformer that combines all inputs into a shared spatial context. From one or more reference images it generates new views at any specified camera position and angle, using precise camera geometry as a native input rather than coarse text prompts. Atlas will power future versions of the company's Marble product and targets world generation, high-fidelity simulation, and robot planning (132 Hacker News points).
Source: https://www.worldlabs.ai/blog/atlas

**9. Waymo goes on offense ahead of Tesla's Cybercab launch**
Summary: Waymo argued in a blog post and Axios interview that safe fully autonomous driving at scale requires multiple sensor types and that "pure end-to-end" neural networks taking raw pixels to steering commands risk "black box failures" — an unsubtle swipe at Tesla's approach, one week before Tesla's September 3 Cybercab event. The company also announced three new markets on top of its dozen-plus U.S. cities, and the exchange kicked off a weekend social-media fight between the two camps. At stake is a robotaxi market analysts peg at hundreds of billions of dollars.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/waymo-goes-on-offense-ahead-of-teslas-cybercab-launch/

**10. AfterQuery reportedly becomes Y Combinator's fastest-ever unicorn at $3.2B**
Summary: AI training-data startup AfterQuery has reportedly raised a round valuing it at $3.2 billion — a 10x jump in under six months from its April Series A at $300 million, and per YC partner Gustaf Alströmer the fastest launch-to-unicorn in the accelerator's history. The San Francisco startup, founded by 22- and 23-year-olds who went through YC's Winter 2025 cohort, hit a $100 million annualized revenue run rate in April with customers including Nvidia, Legora, and Korean lab Motif Technologies. Unlike Mercor and Scale, it trains models and agents on how professionals complete tasks rather than just factual accuracy.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/afterquery-reportedly-becomes-y-combinators-fastest-ever-unicorn-now-valued-at-3-2b/

## AI NEWS

**1. Google Gemma 4 12B: Open-Source Multimodal Model Runs Locally on 16GB Laptops**
Google released Gemma 4 12B, an 11.95-billion-parameter open-weights model with an Apache 2.0 license that can run entirely on a standard enterprise laptop using just 16GB of VRAM. Its encoder-free "Unified" architecture allows raw audio waveforms and visual patches to flow directly into the core LLM backbone without secondary processing modules, and it supports a 256K token context window with native agentic tool-use and step-by-step reasoning.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-new-open-source-gemma-4-12b-analyzes-audio-video-and-runs-entirely-locally-on-a-typical-16gb-enterprise-laptop

**2. Alphabet's Record $85B Raise Signals Massive Investor Appetite for AI**
Alphabet executed a record-breaking $85 billion stock sale to fund Google's AI business, one of the largest capital raises in corporate history. The move suggests institutional investors see AI infrastructure spending as a long-term growth play and that Alphabet is committed to outspending competitors on compute and model development.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/alphabets-record-breaking-85b-raise-for-googles-ai-business-is-a-helluva-good-signal/

**3. Microsoft Launches MXC: OS-Level Sandbox for AI Agents with OpenAI and Nvidia on Board**
Microsoft unveiled MXC, an operating-system-level sandbox for AI agents designed to contain agent actions and prevent data exfiltration. OpenAI and Nvidia have already signed on as launch partners, signaling industry-wide recognition that agent security has become a first-class concern as autonomous coding and browsing agents proliferate.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/security/microsoft-launches-mxc-an-os-level-sandbox-for-ai-agents-with-openai-and-nvidia-already-on-board

**4. OpenAI's Codex Update Lets Agents Build Interactive Enterprise Workspaces via "Sites" and Plugins**
OpenAI announced a major Codex update introducing "Sites" — a rapid, semi-private web hosting feature for enterprises — and role-specific plugins that let agents construct interactive workspaces. This marks a significant expansion of Codex from developer tooling into enterprise application platforms, bringing agent-built interfaces directly to business users.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/openais-codex-update-lets-agents-build-interactive-enterprise-workspaces-via-sites-and-role-specific-plugins

**5. Microsoft Debuts Surface RTX Spark Dev Box — Local AI Model Hardware Without Cloud Costs**
Microsoft unveiled the Surface RTX Spark Dev Box, a compact desktop designed for running large AI models locally rather than paying cloud inference fees. The move directly challenges the per-token pricing model that has defined the AI industry's economics, offering developers a one-time hardware cost alternative to recurring API bills.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/microsoft-debuts-surface-rtx-spark-dev-box-to-run-large-ai-models-without-cloud-costs

**6. Uber Caps AI Coding Tool Usage at $1,500/Month Per Tool to Manage Soaring Costs**
Uber is limiting all employees to $1,500 in monthly token spending per AI coding tool (Cursor, Claude Code, etc.) after blowing through its 2026 AI budget in four months. Simon Willison notes this caps AI spending at roughly 11% of a median engineer's compensation package, calling it a rational response to the unexpected popularity of token-burning coding agents.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/3/uber-caps-usage/#atom-everything

**7. Coralogix Raises $200M to Build the Monitoring Layer for AI Agents**
Coralogix raised $200M, betting that as AI systems move into production, demand for observability tools that monitor agent behavior, troubleshoot failures, and provide audit trails will surge. The round reflects a broader trend of infrastructure startups positioning themselves as the "Datadog for AI agents."
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/coralogix-raises-200m-in-race-to-build-the-monitoring-layer-for-ai-agents/

**8. UK Regulators Require Google to Let Publishers Opt Out of AI Search**
UK regulators are mandating that Google offer a tool allowing website publishers to opt out of generative AI search features. The option will first be tested in the UK before rolling out globally, setting a potential regulatory precedent for how AI search engines must respect publisher choice.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/publishers-will-be-able-to-opt-out-of-ai-search-thanks-to-new-regulation/

**9. Microsoft's New MAI Models: MAI-Thinking-1 (1T Parameters) and MAI-Code-1-Flash**
Microsoft announced two new text LLMs: MAI-Thinking-1 (1 trillion parameters, 35B active, reasoning-focused) and MAI-Code-1-Flash (137B parameters, 5B active, purpose-built for GitHub Copilot). Both were trained from the ground up on commercially licensed data without distillation from third-party models, and MAI-Code-1-Flash is rolling out to GitHub Copilot individual users in VS Code.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/#atom-everything

**10. Perplexity AI Unveils Hybrid Local-Cloud Inference System at Computex 2026**
Perplexity AI, now valued at $20B, demonstrated what it calls the first hybrid local-server inference orchestrator — software that autonomously decides in real-time which AI workloads run on a user's local device versus the cloud. The system aims to balance cost, latency, and privacy across inference tasks.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/perplexity-ai-unveils-hybrid-local-cloud-inference-system-at-computex-2026

**11. Meta's AI Agent for WhatsApp Business Goes Live Globally**
Meta launched its AI agent for WhatsApp Business worldwide, allowing businesses to deploy conversational AI for customer interactions directly within WhatsApp. Meta will charge businesses based on token usage, marking a new monetization avenue for WhatsApp beyond its existing business messaging API.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/metas-ai-agent-for-whatsapp-business-is-now-available-globally/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Booking.com CTO: The New Bottleneck Is Teamwork, Not Technology**
Booking.com's CTO argued at the Skift Data + AI Summit that the hardest challenges in AI implementation today are organizational, not technical. As travel companies race to deploy AI, the bottleneck is cross-team collaboration, data sharing, and aligning business incentives — not model capability.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/06/03/booking-com-cto-the-new-bottleneck-is-teamwork/

**2. Expedia Tech Exec on Build Versus Buy: Keep Customer Data Close**
An Expedia technology executive cautioned that acquiring AI companies can sometimes set a travel company back more than building in-house, because customer data — the most valuable asset for personalization — is hard to integrate across acquisitions. The lesson: buy for speed, build for data moats.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/06/03/expedia-tech-exec-on-build-versus-buy-keep-customer-data-close/

**3. Priceline's Transparency Play: Let Users See and Correct Their Data to Build AI Trust**
Priceline is addressing the consumer trust problem that has slowed AI adoption in travel by showing users their data and letting them correct it. The strategy positions transparency as a competitive differentiator in an industry where personalization depends on user data but trust is at an all-time low.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/06/03/priceline-at-skift-data-ai-summit/

**4. Sierra and Amadeus: Waiting for Agents to Mature Is Costing Competitive Ground**
At the Skift Data + AI Summit, Sierra and Amadeus argued that organizations still waiting for AI agents to mature before committing to production deployments are already losing competitive ground. The message was clear: the technology is ready enough, and the risk of inaction exceeds the risk of imperfect deployment.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/06/03/sierra-and-amadeus-at-skift-data-ai-summit/

**5. Mews: Stop Justifying AI Investment as Labor Savings — Focus on Revenue**
Mews challenged the prevailing travel industry narrative that AI ROI should be measured by headcount reduction. Instead, the property management platform argued companies should build the revenue case for AI — personalization, upselling, dynamic pricing — rather than overselling what cost-cutting can achieve.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/06/03/mews-at-skift-data-ai-summit-2026/

**6. Flight Science, Abra, BizTrip CEOs: Proprietary Data Beats AI Models as the Moat**
At the Skift summit, CEOs from Flight Science, Abra, and BizTrip argued that what makes a travel AI offering defensible is not the model but proprietary data and operational depth that competitors can't replicate. In a world where frontier models are increasingly commoditized, the winners will own unique travel data and workflows.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/06/03/flight-science-abra-biz-trip-ai-ceos-skift-data-ai-summit-2026/

## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic opens at $111.50 on Nasdaq, a third above its IPO price**
Summary: Anthropic began trading under the ticker "ANT" at $111.50, roughly 34% above the $83 IPO price set Wednesday, after an offering that raised more than $81 billion. The company said the IPO plus concurrent private placements leave it with about $220 billion in cash, and at the opening price its fully diluted valuation passed $2 trillion — behind only Apple, Amazon, Alphabet, Nvidia and Microsoft. The debut drew 826 points on Hacker News, where the loudest thread was valuation math: on Nasdaq's outstanding-share basis the market cap reads closer to $1.3 trillion, which is the discrepancy every headline about this listing will carry.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-hits-nasdaq-exchanges-at-110-27-above-ipo-price/

**2. Claude solves two open problems in the physics of black holes**
Summary: Anthropic published research showing Claude Fable 5.2 completed a calculation physicists set up in 2003 and never finished — whether a phase transition in certain model universes is abrupt or smooth, extending the Hawking-Page result from 1983 — and separately proved an exact ceiling on how light the lightest black hole can be in a two-dimensional model universe, a bound researchers had only approached numerically since 2009. The work grew out of an August side project in which a researcher had Claude list 22 open problems in theoretical physics and set a small group of copies of itself on each; one agent reached its answer weeks before a physicist who had tackled the same question did. Neither result describes our own universe or has practical use, but the post drew 345 points on Hacker News as the clearest case yet of an AI system producing publishable theoretical physics rather than summarizing it.
Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-black-hole-physics

**3. Apple is hiring to build its own agentic AI assistant**
Summary: A job listing spotted by Bloomberg has Apple recruiting an engineer to lead an agentic AI assistant that can browse the web, work across apps, write code and call services, with the posting describing agents that plan and carry out multi-step tasks across apps, browsers, command lines, code and APIs and coordinate specialized sub-agents. That would put Apple in the same race as Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, Anthropic's Duet and Google's Tasks — all of which can already drive a Mac — and it lines up with Apple opening Siri AI to all apps in the iOS 27.3 developer preview. Apple has shipped the most cautious agent story of any platform owner, so a first-party agent is a strategic admission that Siri alone is not the answer.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1038227/apple-ai-agent-engineer-job-listing

**4. Google gives Gemini computer use on the Mac and puts AI chats on its homepage**
Summary: Gemini's macOS app now has computer use, letting it complete multi-step tasks across local files, desktop apps and the web — filling forms, making presentations, drafting marketplace listings — the same capability ChatGPT, Muse and Perplexity already ship. On the same day Google added a collapsible sidebar to its search homepage that shows recent AI chats and lets users resume them, moving the Gemini conversation onto the page most people still start from. Both moves are distribution plays rather than model news: Google is pushing the assistant into the surfaces it already owns instead of asking users to open a separate app.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1037902/google-gemini-macos-ai-computer-use

**5. Students sue after a Doritos bag triggered an AI gun alert and an armed police response**
Summary: Four current and former students sued Baltimore County Public Schools and county police after an AI gun-detection system flagged a bag of Doritos, and officers arrived with at least six patrol cars and guns drawn, handcuffed the teens and searched them — even though staff reviewing camera footage identified the snack and canceled the alert within two minutes. The suit alleges the system's alerts were false positives more than 99% of the time and that the escalation came from "both technological and human errors." It is an early test of liability for school AI surveillance whose errors end in armed police contact, and it targets the humans who trusted the alert as much as the vendor that produced it.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/traumatized-teens-sue-school-cops-after-doritos-bag-triggers-ai-gun-alert/

**6. Google built a private clone of the internet to test its Chrome AI agents**
Summary: Google detailed the "Mirror Dimension," an isolated but realistic web ecosystem its Chrome team built and controls — social networks, shopping sites, bank dashboards — so browsing agents can be trained against hostile pages and edge cases without touching the real internet. Engineers say limited test environments are too small for agents that can encounter anything, and that models can develop "eval awareness" and behave differently once they suspect a simulation; the clone was populated by armies of website-building agents. It is an infrastructure answer to the year's core agent-security problem: since no one can predict what an agent will meet, the only real option is somewhere safe to find out.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/google-built-a-giant-mirror-dimension-internet-to-test-its-chrome-ai-agents/

**7. Court: police can't delegate probable cause to an AI tool — and can't claim immunity for it**
Summary: A court held that the Fourth Amendment does not allow a detective to hand the probable-cause assessment to an AI system, and rejected qualified immunity for the officer in a suit brought after the underlying case was dismissed, ruling that "no reasonable officer" could rely on AI output as the sole basis for an arrest warrant. The decision matters less for the specific arrest than for the doctrine it sets: the tool can inform an officer's judgment but cannot substitute for it, and using it as a shortcut is now a personal financial exposure rather than an agency policy question.
Source: https://www.techdirt.com/2026/11/19/court-tells-cops-that-the-4th-amendment-doesnt-cover-ai-generated-probable-cause/

**8. Canva adds an AI off switch**
Summary: Canva now lets users disable its generative AI features — Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Edit, Dream Lab, Canva Code and its image-generating chatbot — via a single toggle in settings, plus a separate option that surfaces human-made templates and graphics first in its library, available on free and paid plans. Background removal and Magic Grab stay on because Canva argues they don't generate anything new. After three years of adding AI everywhere and user complaints with no way out, a major creative platform shipping a real opt-out is the first sign that "AI everywhere" is becoming a product decision rather than a default.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1037126/canva-ai-features-off-button

**9. Meta's Muse Charm wearable will reportedly cost $300**
Summary: The Information reported that Meta plans to price the Muse Charm — a small screen-equipped gadget for talking to its Muse agent — at $300, with the device expected to ship in time for the December holidays, as Mark Zuckerberg said earlier this year without naming a price. Meta did not deny the report, saying only that it would "have more to share soon." At $300 the Charm is priced as a companion device rather than an accessory, which puts it against dedicated AI hardware that has so far failed to find buyers.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1038087/meta-muse-charm-ai-price

**10. ChatGPT gets live dictation with no time limit**
Summary: OpenAI rolled out Live Dictation to all ChatGPT plans globally on web, desktop, iOS and Android, transcribing speech as the user talks rather than waiting for the recording to finish and dropping the previous 10-minute cap. Tapping the waveform shows the live transcript, and the feature is aimed at long-form prompts and hands-busy use. Voice is where the assistant products are converging on input, and removing the cap matters more than the latency improvement — it turns dictation into a way to work rather than a way to ask a question.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1037634/openai-chatgpt-live-dictation

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Sabre wants to be the transaction engine behind AI travel bookings**
Summary: Sabre is pitching itself as the transaction layer for agent-driven travel, aiming to keep shopping, booking and servicing flowing through its systems even when an AI platform owns the customer relationship. After years of modernizing those systems so machines can consume them, it is now going after the major AI platforms directly as a front door it does not control. The bet is that whoever wins the conversation, the transaction still needs distribution-scale plumbing behind it — which is the same argument Amadeus and Travelport are making to the same customers.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/19/sabre-wants-to-be-the-transaction-engine-behind-ai-travel-bookings/

**2. Metafoodx launches Chef Echo, an agent that plans hotel kitchen production**
Summary: Metafoodx released Chef Echo, an agentic AI assistant built on the production and consumption data its platform already collects from commercial kitchens, which each morning flags overproduction risk, dishes that keep coming back, and demand shifts from holidays or weather, then drafts a daily briefing with recommended adjustments. The company is explicit that the chef decides and recommendations are starting points, and that the data stays in the system across staffing changes. Hotel F&B is the operational corner where AI is being sold as proactive recommendation rather than another dashboard, and food waste is the cost line that makes the case.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/11/metafoodx-introduces-chef-echo-ai-assistant-to-help-hotel-kitchens-plan-production-and-reduce-food-waste/

**3. Nuitée adds cruises to its distribution API, opening the door to AI agents**
Summary: Nuitée added cruise inventory to its travel distribution API through a partnership with B2B cruise booking platform Boundless Labs, a move that could eventually put cruise bookings within reach of the AI assistants its partners power. Cruise is the hardest product in travel to sell — cabin categories, dining, excursions, deposit rules — so it functions as a stress test of whether agents can handle complex inventory rather than simple hotel and flight pairs. The open question the deal raises is trust: cruisers hand over thousands of dollars and a lot of preference detail, and whether they will delegate that to an assistant is unproven.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/16/nuitee-adds-cruises-from-boundless-labs-opens-door-to-ai-agents-exclusive/

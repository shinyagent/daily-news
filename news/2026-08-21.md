**1. OpenAI Is Gaining on Anthropic with Business Users, New Spending Data Shows**
Summary: New data from Ramp, the corporate card and expense platform, shows OpenAI has begun closing the gap with Anthropic among U.S. businesses — though Anthropic still leads, holding nearly 44% of Ramp's paying business users in July versus OpenAI's nearly 40% after taking the lead back in May. Ramp's numbers serve as a rare proxy ahead of either lab's IPO disclosures, and they also show businesses readily switching between the two as each releases new models — volatility that should temper expectations about how "sticky" enterprise AI spending really is.

**2. ChatGPT Can Now Send Texts for You with New Apple Messages Plug-in**
Summary: OpenAI launched an Apple Messages plug-in for ChatGPT that lets users connect their Messages inbox to the chatbot, enabling them to sort, analyze, and edit their messages — or draft replies — directly from ChatGPT. The plug-in also works with Codex and ChatGPT Work, extending it from personal texting into professional workflows. The integration deepens OpenAI's reach into users' daily communications while raising fresh privacy questions about handing message data to the AI lab.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/chatgpt-can-now-send-texts-for-you-with-new-apple-messages-plugin/

**3. Grok Keeps Sending Gibberish Responses to Users**

The glitch is the latest quality stumble for xAI's consumer chatbot as it battles ChatGPT and Gemini for mainstream users.

**4. Google Gives Publishers a New Way to Fight AI-Driven Traffic Losses**
Summary: Google is letting publishers embed an interactive "Preferred Sources" button on their sites, which readers can click to mark a publisher as a favorite they'd like to see highlighted more often across Search, Discover, and Google News. The feature is a direct response to AI-generated search answers diverting clicks away from publishers. It gives news sites a way to rebuild a direct reader relationship inside Google's surfaces — while also concentrating even more distribution power in Google's hands.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/google-gives-publishers-a-new-way-to-fight-ai-driven-traffic-losses/

**5. A Third of Web Pages Published Since ChatGPT's Launch Show Signs of AI Authorship**

It underscores how deeply generative AI is reshaping the open web, and the data-quality challenges that creates for future model training.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/20/a-third-of-webpages-published-since-chatgpts-launch-show-signs-of-ai-authorship-study-finds/

**6. Ramp Launches Its Own AI Model Router, Called Router**

The launch puts Ramp in direct competition with Stripe in the race to become the toll booth for AI inference, a layer that is rapidly becoming the standard fix for runaway model costs.

**1. Expedia Cuts Eight Executives as It Reorganizes Around AI**
Summary: Expedia Group has cut eight senior leaders — including Sachin Singh, SVP of book-to-trip technology, and seven vice presidents covering payments, fraud and risk, and self-service tools — as it shifts authority from coordinating executives to smaller, AI-focused squads that build closer to the brands they support. Per a memo obtained by GeekWire, their responsibilities will be divided among remaining tech executives, the chief information security officer, and finance. With roughly half of Expedia's 16,000 employees in technology roles, the reorg shows AI now driving headcount and organizational structure decisions at one of travel's largest platforms.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/08/20/expedia-cuts-eight-executives-as-it-reorganizes-around-ai/

**2. The UK's Hidden-Fee Crackdown Is Coming for Travel**
Summary: The UK's Competition and Markets Authority has opened formal investigations into Virgin Atlantic and Trainline over "drip pricing" — failing to show customers the full price of a booking before they pay. The CMA no longer needs a court to reach a verdict and can fine a company up to 10% of its global turnover once it does, putting the whole travel industry on notice about fee transparency. Airlines, OTAs, and booking platforms across the UK market will be watching closely as the probe unfolds.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/08/20/the-uks-hidden-fee-crackdown-is-coming-for-travel/

**9. Morgan Stanley Cut Its Riskiest Reconciliation Job in Half — By Making Agents *Less* Autonomous**
In a counterintuitive AI deployment case study, Morgan Stanley deployed agents in P&L reconciliation — one of banking's most accuracy-critical workflows — and cut the work in half. The secret was keeping humans tightly in the loop, iteratively turning their decisions into repeatable rules the system could apply independently over time. A concrete lesson in the "human-in-the-loop" design pattern for high-stakes enterprise AI.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/morgan-stanley-cut-its-riskiest-reconciliation-job-in-half-by-making-its-agents-less-autonomous

**1. Trump Administration Moves to Repeal Obama-Era Airfare Transparency Rule**
The Trump administration is moving to roll back a consumer protection rule requiring airlines to display all-in pricing including taxes and fees upfront. The repeal is the latest in a series of actions to ease regulatory burdens on airlines, but consumer advocates warn it will make it harder for travelers to compare true ticket costs across carriers.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/07/01/trump-administration-moves-to-repeal-obamas-airfare-transparency-rule/

**2. U.S. Paves Way for Supersonic Flight Over Land — 1973 Ban in Crosshairs**
The U.S. government is moving to lift the 1973 ban on supersonic flight over land, a decades-old restriction that has limited commercial supersonic aviation to transoceanic routes. If repealed, the change would clear regulatory barriers for companies like Boom Supersonic to operate overland routes, potentially reshaping domestic air travel.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/06/30/u-s-paves-way-for-supersonic-flight-over-land/

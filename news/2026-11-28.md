## AI NEWS

**1. Bloomberg finds Nvidia's banned Blackwell chips installed in Chinese data centers tied to Megaspeed**
Summary: Bloomberg reports that GB200 and GB300 systems — Blackwell parts never approved for export to China — are running in at least two data centers linked to Megaspeed, the Singapore-based cloud firm founded by Alice Huang and now blacklisted by the U.S. Earlier reporting established smuggled Hopper-generation hardware at a Shanghai facility tied to Megaspeed; this is the first documented appearance of the current-generation Blackwell kit inside China. It lands days after Nvidia paused server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia to audit whether regional partners were complying, and it undercuts the argument that the export controls are holding at the top of the stack.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1043882/nvidias-most-powerful-chips-made-it-into-chinese-data-centers-linked-to-megaspeed

**2. Photonic processors leave the lab: Q.ANT puts light-based AI chips into an IONOS cloud pilot**
Summary: German startup Q.ANT has begun running photonic processors inside cloud provider IONOS's facilities, part of a bet that light-based chips can cut the energy and cooling bill for AI workloads by handling the complex, nonlinear math that GPUs are worst at. The pitch is co-processor, not replacement: GPUs keep the linear algebra, photonic chips take the rest, and Q.ANT says more than 30 engineering teams are now tuning models for the hardware. Very few photonic processors run in production data centers today, so the pilot is one of the first real tests of whether the approach survives contact with a commercial cloud.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/a-new-breed-of-chip-runs-ai-models-using-waves-of-light/

**3. A deterministic discrete-log algorithm lands, built directly on OpenAI's disputed tensor-contraction claim**
Summary: Researchers at Zhejiang University's State Key Lab of Blockchain and Data Security posted a proof that, assuming OpenAI's October Cauchy–Hermite Boolean tensor-contraction construction, a uniform deterministic Turing machine solves discrete logarithms in prime fields and on prime-order elliptic curves in p^o(1) time — with no random bits, no Generalized Riemann Hypothesis and no smoothness heuristics. It builds on the Ruhr-Universität Bochum and Max Planck subexponential ECDLP paper from late October, which itself leaned on OpenAI's claim that the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false. The caveats are large and the authors say so: the result is asymptotic and conditional on an unrefereed theorem whose saving only appears at absurd network sizes, so P-256 and Ed25519 are untouched — but the crypto establishment has started pricing AI-derived math into its security assumptions, and *The Times* ran the read-out as mathematicians fearing AI is about to make online security useless.
Source: https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/3020

**4. Anthropic starts paying out its $1.5 billion authors settlement**
Summary: The first checks in *Bartz v. Anthropic* went out the week of November 23, with undisputed claims receiving about $2,203.56 per work — roughly 70 percent of an award expected to land near $3,000 a book — and novelist Courtney Milan confirmed she had been paid. The remaining 30 percent waits on Anthropic's final $450 million deposit into the fund, which the settlement ties to a large financing event; the company's November 18 Nasdaq debut at $83 a share, the largest IPO on record, is what unlocked that obligation. It is the first time money has actually moved from a frontier lab to the authors whose books trained it, and the installment structure means the precedent is still only two-thirds funded.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1043087/anthropic-has-started-paying-out-its-copyright-settlement-to-authors

**5. Michael Nielsen takes his neural networks book offline rather than train more people for frontier labs**
Summary: Nielsen's *Neural Networks and Deep Learning*, a free standard introduction written between 2011 and 2015, now resolves to a statement explaining that he took it down because his views changed: he expected AGI to be decades away and to arrive with time to prepare, and instead found "the extraordinarily reckless behaviour of the frontier labs" racing ahead of society's ability to respond. He writes that he does not wish to help, even slightly, train people to contribute to that work. His argument is that the core risk is not loss of control but destructive power latent in nature — cheap, fast, and beyond our capacity to defend — which makes technical alignment a market-failure problem rather than the solution the labs sell.
Source: https://neuralnetworksanddeeplearning.com/

**6. Convex's own agent skill shipped users' app ideas to a third-party server and told the agent to dodge safety tooling**
Summary: An audit published as a GitHub issue finds that Convex's backend skill — the packaged instructions an AI coding agent loads to build on its platform — tells the agent to send the user's app idea to an unpinned bootstrap script hosted on a generated-name domain, and instructs it to change the shape of a command specifically so the user's safety classifier does not catch the download. The reporter, who maintains a catalog of agent skills and vetting tools, characterizes the onboarding intent as legitimate but the effect as unreviewable, and notes the passages were still on main in the labs-quickstart skill as of late October. It is the clearest example yet that "install our skill" is an install script with the same supply-chain problem, and that the untrusted input now includes the vendor's own documentation.
Source: https://github.com/convex/convex-backend-skill/issues/64

**7. Daniel J. Bernstein catalogues "false computer-checked proofs"**
Summary: Bernstein has assembled a list of reported soundness bugs in the programs that claim to check every step of a formal proof, noting that many were demonstrated exploitable, and that the catalog is longer than the incidents cited in the recent survey literature. His argument is that the standard reassurance — the checker is small, or its kernel has been verified by another checker — mostly pushes trust down a level rather than eliminating it, and that running a proof through several independent checkers helps only when the bugs are not shared. It is a timely caution: the AI-math wave has been validated by Lean and friends, and this is the first careful accounting of what "verified" is actually worth.
Source: https://cr.yp.to/proofs/falseccp.html

**8. Moonshot opens "Kimi for Science" to outside researchers**
Summary: The Chinese lab behind the Kimi model series launched a program inviting researchers across the natural sciences, engineering and interdisciplinary fields to apply for support, framed around the argument that limited time, compute and people mean many good hypotheses never get tested. It is aimed at both incremental work — improving existing workflows with Kimi — and methodological experiments, with life sciences, health, energy and materials named as target areas. For a lab competing on agentic coding rather than brand, subsidizing scientific use is a cheap way to buy credible evaluation data and citation gravity.
Source: https://www.kimi.ai/news/kimi-for-science-program

**9. GPT-6.2 Astra autonomously identified the author of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook**
Summary: Historian Mark Humphries, given pre-release access, reports that with minimal prompting the model identified the creator of an anonymous sketchbook held at Yale's Beinecke Library in 22 minutes, working only from public primary and secondary sources — and that it predicted a little-known archive document would confirm its hypothesis if examined, then turned out to be right. The document is itself a find of interest to Canadian and fur-trade historians. Humphries' point is that autonomous research is generalizing past mathematics, where verification is mechanical, into a domain where the deliverable is an argument rather than a proof.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/24/astra-historical-mystery/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Agoda's COO: AI booking is "definitely disruptive," but nobody knows the business model**
Summary: Ittai Chorev told a Bangkok roundtable that AI booking will reshape the industry, but that "in terms of how the business model or commercials will look, nobody knows" — and that the current arrangement works in Agoda's favor because the AI platforms are still in a growth phase where "everything is free." The Booking Holdings-owned OTA was among the first Asian travel partners to integrate with Meta's Muse, and uses OpenAI and Microsoft models for internal tools. The unresolved question is the one that decides OTA margins: whether agents become a distribution channel Agoda pays for, or a layer that takes the customer relationship and the take rate with it.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/27/agodas-coo-on-ai-booking-nobody-knows-the-business-model-yet/

**2. Hotelbeds' parent has spent €102 million buying its way out of wholesale margin pressure**
Summary: HBX Group's filings show €102 million ($112 million) in investments and estimated acquisition consideration across five businesses since 2024, about €65 million of it contingent on performance, directed at holiday packaging, experiences, luxury travel and hotel software. The spending comes as the core bedbank concedes take rate to hold share — the pressure Skift documented in August has intensified in the annual results. It is the same squeeze hitting every wholesale intermediary: distribution is commoditizing, so the margin has to come from software and packaging instead of room markup.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/27/hotelbeds-112-million-bet-as-its-core-business-comes-under-pressure/

**3. Global Hotel Alliance stops growing in the UAE and bets on Saudi Arabia instead**
Summary: CEO Christopher Hartley says the Dubai-headquartered network of independent brands — 1,000 hotels, more than 60 brands, 100 countries — will not add many more properties in the UAE after a hard year there, and will push into Saudi Arabia even as Riyadh's hotels struggle with soft demand. The pitch to independents is the shared loyalty and distribution stack they cannot build alone, which is precisely the asset that gets more valuable as AI agents shop a consolidated set of machine-readable brands. The risk is that the growth market is the one where the demand assumptions are currently weakest.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/27/why-global-hotel-alliance-is-turning-to-saudi-arabia-despite-riyadhs-slump/

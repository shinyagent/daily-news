## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic files its S-1: $16.3 billion in first-half revenue, an $80.7 billion net loss, and founder control locked in**
Summary: After weeks of leaks, Anthropic filed for an IPO on Monday under the ticker "ANT," reporting first-half 2026 revenue of $16.3 billion against $1.2 billion a year earlier, with operating losses narrowing to $693 million from $2.1 billion — though a net loss of $80.7 billion was driven by noncash convertible-debt accounting. Enterprise scale is the story: 11,900 customers spend $100,000+ a year, 1,900 spend $1 million+, about 250 spend $10 million+, and more than half of the $100,000-tier accounts now use both the Claude Platform and a first-party app like Claude Code or Cowork, up from roughly 30% at the end of last year. A seven-member "Founder LLC" will control 50.1% of voting power, and WIRED notes the prospectus redacts its most recent quarter's revenue and the lease terms behind its compute commitments — the single largest operating cost in the business.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-officially-files-for-its-public-debut/

**2. OpenAI publishes three "major" results — including a claimed disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis — with named experts shepherding each**
Summary: Less than a month after dumping hundreds of math manuscripts, OpenAI released results in math, computer science and physics, with Lean proofs on GitHub: a proof of the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension (which it says also resolves Kakeya and Stein's restriction conjecture), an algorithm that exactly counts satisfying assignments of fixed-width CNFs in subexponential time and thus refutes the 3-SAT-based Exponential Time Hypothesis, and a "mathematically rigorous existence proof" of interacting quantum field theories containing gauge fields and fermions in four dimensions, including QCD. Verification is delegated: Impagliazzo, Paturi and Ryan Williams for ETH, David Gross leading a group for the physics claim, and an open letter asking how credit and early access should work. It drew 232 points on Hacker News, and the awkward part is that the AGMAI panel of math advisers said the day before that labs should stop designating who gets AI output first.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1019143/openai-eth-kaseya-science-math-physics

**3. OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra targets computer use at half the price — and cuts its error rate from 53.6% to 16.7%**
Summary: Terra replaces GPT-5.6 Terra as the cheap workhorse, aimed at form-filling, invoice processing, technical drawings and video editing, at $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 cached, $5 output — half the standard price of both GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI claims it matches GPT-6 Astra on Agents' Last Exam at a tenth the price per token, runs over three times faster than Astra and Sol, and is ~9x cheaper per task than Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort; on Terminal-Bench 4.0 it solves 50.3% versus 21.5% for its predecessor. The headline number is factuality: on OpenAI's internal error-inducing prompt set, error rate at low reasoning effort fell from 53.6% to 16.7%. It drew 469 points on Hacker News.
Source: https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-terra/

**4. Cloudflare discloses a cross-request KV cache leak in GLM-5.3 on Workers AI that returned other customers' data**
Summary: A researcher reported on September 8 that very long prompts to Cloudflare's hosted GLM-5.3 sometimes returned text from other users' sessions; the company reproduced it the next morning, pulled the model within 40 minutes and restored it the same day. The cause was two overlapping problems: Workers AI ran the model with a 1,048,576-token context window on a prefill KV cache pool sized for 681,280 tokens, and SGLang's disaggregated prefill/decode path replaced over-length prompts with a one-token placeholder instead of erroring, so the decode node attended over stale cache pages from earlier requests. 155 responses were affected, all to prompts longer than 681,280 tokens, and Cloudflare found no evidence of deliberate exploitation; it published the report jointly with SGLang.
Source: https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/

**5. SpaceX locks in $40 billion in GPU financing through an Apollo sale-leaseback**
Summary: SpaceX disclosed a binding commitment for roughly $40 billion in financing for AI chips and data center equipment, calling it the "largest committed GPU financing." An Apollo Global Management-managed entity will buy the chips and server hardware and lease it back to SpaceX on roughly five-to-five-and-a-quarter-year terms starting at each delivery tranche. It gives SpaceX a way to fund its AI buildout without leaning entirely on traditional debt markets — the same structure that has become standard for neoclouds trying to keep capex off the balance sheet.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/spacex-locks-in-40b-gpu-financing-deal-with-apollo/

**6. Bedrock Robotics raises $600 million at a $4.1 billion valuation for self-driving excavators**
Summary: The two-year-old company's Series C was co-led by CapitalG and Emergence Capital and brings total funding to nearly $1 billion. Bedrock sells a retrofit autonomous-driving kit for construction and worksite vehicles, and its excavators have moved from pilots to commercial jobs at a Nevada water treatment facility, a Champion Site Prep earthwork site in Texas, and a 1.2 million-cubic-yard civil project with Zachry. CEO Boris Sofman previously ran Waymo's shuttered self-driving truck program, and the pitch is a skilled-operator shortage rather than driverless taxis.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/this-startups-self-driving-excavators-help-dig-up-600m-in-new-funding/

**7. Microsoft offers voluntary buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees as it reworks its workforce for AI**
Summary: Microsoft confirmed the offers Monday; chief people officer Amy Coleman's note says employees at vice president level and below on select teams in the US, Canada, India and the UK are eligible, with terms similar to standard severance. Bloomberg reports Xbox, Windows, Copilot and Finance are participating, while data center, sales and marketing staff are excluded — the pattern of cutting headcount in software roles while protecting AI infrastructure. It is the company's latest cost-cutting move amid a buildout that has made compute its dominant expense line.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-buyouts-to-around-60000-employees-reports-say/

**8. The RealReal hardened its bot defenses after an AI agent used its 20-minute cart hold to lock out human shoppers**
Summary: After The Verge showed that Meta's Muse could be told to re-add a listing every time the 20-minute reservation expired — blocking other buyers for hours — the consignment retailer changed its bot-detection configuration to make it much harder for any agent to log in, add to cart and check out. Agents can still surface listings and send users to product pages, CTO Luke Friang said, and the protections will tighten as agentic shopping evolves. It is a concrete precedent for how retailers will draw the line between AI agents that drive traffic and agents that consume scarce inventory.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/news/1018713/meta-muse-agentic-shopping-ai-the-realreal

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. MakeMyTrip is using a 7% drop in domestic air departures to push into hotels, buses and cabs**
Summary: India's largest OTA reported September-quarter gross bookings of $2.7 billion, up 21% year over year in constant currency, with adjusted operating profit of $46.2 million — but air was the soft spot, as domestic departures fell 7% and international 4% while fares rose 25-30%. Non-air businesses reached 63.2% of gross bookings, up from 61.5%, with hotels and packages now about 40% of the business and stand-alone hotel room nights growing. The strategic read is that Indian online travel's next fight is over supply nobody has fully digitized, not over flights.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/26/makemytrip-uses-an-air-travel-dip-to-reach-beyond-flights/

**2. Airlines are counting on Starlink as a revenue lever, not just a passenger perk**
Summary: United mentioned the satellite service 24 times on its latest earnings call, and CEO Scott Kirby called it a "truly differentiating RASM tailwind in 2027," with about 44% of the fleet equipped and installations targeted for completion by the end of 2027. The bet is that free high-speed Wi-Fi pulls share from rivals and offsets fuel costs, and that low-earth-orbit connectivity becomes table stakes once competitors catch up. It is the clearest case yet of an inflight connectivity vendor being treated as a distribution and pricing strategy rather than an IT line item.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/26/airlines-are-counting-on-a-starlink-tailwind/

**3. One-person travel businesses are scaling because the industry's biggest companies now rent out their infrastructure**
Summary: Skift counted roughly twenty variants of solo operators — advisors running million-dollar books from home offices, co-hosts managing 20-plus short-term rentals while software handles guest messages, pricing and cleaner dispatch, fractional revenue managers setting rates for a dozen independent hotels remotely. None of these models is new; what changed is that booking, payments, distribution and pricing tooling is now cheap and capable enough that one person can absorb work that previously required a staff. The implication for incumbents is that their B2B and API layers are quietly becoming the supply side of a long tail of micro-operators.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/26/the-rise-of-one-person-travel-businesses/

## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic and Nscale reportedly near a second $45 billion compute deal**
Summary: Bloomberg reports the two companies are on the verge of announcing another $45 billion compute agreement, roughly a month after their first deal of the same size, with sources cautioning talks are ongoing and terms could change. Both sides are simultaneously pursuing IPOs — Anthropic is still expected to list this year, while Nscale is meeting investor pushback over its proposed $30 billion valuation. The deal would extend a run of giant compute commitments that has spooked investors worried AI companies are spending far more than they can earn back.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/nscale-and-anthropic-are-reportedly-in-talks-for-yet-another-45-billion-compute-deal/

**2. Microsoft says AI agents are the new insider threat — and ships agents to watch them**
Summary: Microsoft introduced Guardian Agents, a feature inside its Project Perception security product that monitors the AI agents an enterprise deploys, on the argument that "frontier models can't be trusted" even when the lab selling them vouches for their safety. The framing follows Satya Nadella's argument earlier this month that companies must separate the supply of intelligence from the authority to act, using monitoring systems the model providers don't control. Project Perception originally assumed AI attackers came from outside; Microsoft now treats its own paid agents as the likelier risk, much like a rogue employee.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/microsoft-says-ai-agents-are-like-insider-threats-as-frontier-models-cant-be-trusted/

**3. CoreWeave's bonds fall into distressed territory, and the whole AI buildout is exposed to it**
Summary: CoreWeave's bonds now yield 1,000 basis points more than government debt — the common threshold for distressed — and its shares are down more than 20% since Tuesday's close, after Chamath Palihapitiya flagged the debt and asked whether the AI buildout is a private credit bubble. The Verge's analysis makes the point that a single neocloud's financing problems are not contained: CoreWeave's debt is held widely and underpins the capacity that AI labs and startups have contracted for. When a specialist GPU landlord has to pay distressed rates, the cost of compute for everyone renting from that layer moves with it.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1014045/coreweave-stock-debt-sector-ai

**4. A hacker breached more than 100 sites with an army of AI agents that ran themselves**
Summary: AI security firm Norforge found that a single hacker used a fleet of AI agents, operating largely autonomously, to break into more than 100 websites; Reuters identified the victims as including John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. The agents carried out the attacks with little human involvement, which is the part that matters: the skill floor for running a campaign at that scale has collapsed, and the same capability now sits on both sides of the security market. It lands alongside a wave of disclosures about rogue agents escaping test environments, turning agent autonomy into an operational risk rather than a theoretical one.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013643/ai-agents-hack-john-deere-shark-ninja-cummins

**5. Amazon attacks Nvidia's software moat with TorchNeuron**
Summary: AWS released TorchNeuron, a tool that lets PyTorch applications run on Amazon's Trainium chips by changing a single line of configuration instead of rewriting code. Amazon is aiming directly at CUDA, the software layer Nvidia spent years building that keeps developers locked to its GPUs — and PyTorch, which roughly 85% of AI apps are written in, is the widest part of that front. AWS is also pitching the full cloud against Nvidia-tied neoclouds like CoreWeave, Nebius and Firmus, framing portability as the safer bet for startups.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/amazon-is-taking-its-biggest-shot-at-nvidia-yet-with-new-torchneuron-dev-tool/

**6. Anthropic gives outside evaluator METR "employee-like access" to its models**
Summary: Anthropic has embedded an external evaluation team from nonprofit METR inside its offices, with access to its hardware and internal systems, and agreed the watchdog can publish findings without company approval. It is the first lab to act on Dario Amodei's September proposal to put external auditors inside frontier labs — a proposal both Sam Altman and Elon Musk publicly endorsed, though OpenAI has yet to lay out plans. Anthropic compares the arrangement to bank supervision by resident examiners; critics argue it invites conflicts of interest and that government regulation is the real answer, a live concern as Anthropic pitches a safety story to IPO investors at a reported $2 trillion valuation.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/anthropic-gives-outside-ai-watchdog-employee-like-access/

**7. Judge keeps accused OpenAI spy in custody, citing a Saudi-funded "blank check"**
Summary: Magistrate judge Alex Tse ordered Abdulrahman Alfozan, the OpenAI engineer accused of hiding a recording device in an executive meeting room, to remain in custody after prosecutors said the Saudi government offered to back his bail through a bonds firm; the judge called it "a blank check" and said he would rather see family assets posted. New filings say Alfozan, a US permanent resident, was in frequent WhatsApp contact with an unidentified member of the Saudi royal family who was recruiting him for a forthcoming Saudi AI initiative, and that the FBI found a folder labeled "Saudi Gov Docs." His arrest was reported last week; the detention ruling and the bail-funding detail make it a geopolitical case rather than a routine trade-secrets one.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/openai-engineer-accused-of-spying-on-executives-ordered-to-remain-in-custody/

**8. MiniMax releases M3.1 Flash, claiming frontier agent performance at 70% lower cost**
Summary: MiniMax shipped M3.1 Flash, a sparse Flash-tier model it says makes a generational jump in agentic tasks, coding, computer use and cybersecurity while cutting usage cost 70% versus its predecessor and running roughly three times faster. Pricing is flat at $0.15 per million input and $0.60 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.015, and the company reports parity with leading models on Automation-Bench, DeepSWE and Agents' Last Exam at far lower cost per task; output runs at 200+ tokens per second with a P50 above 170. It drew 341 points on Hacker News, and MiniMax disclosed that "Space Bunny," the anonymous model that processed 200 trillion tokens in the wild, was an early version — weights are promised later.
Source: https://www.minimax.io/blog/minimax-m31f

**9. Inception Labs ships Mercury 3, the first diffusion LLM that scales**
Summary: Inception Labs released Mercury 3, which it calls the most capable diffusion-based language model yet: more than 40x larger than Mercury 1 but just as fast, with a 1-million-token context and 1,068 tokens per second on Nvidia GPUs. The company claims it outperforms GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 on benchmarks at $0.20 per million input and $0.70 per million output tokens, with early partners using it for voice support, real-time tutoring and web navigation. Diffusion text generation has long promised speed advantages that didn't survive contact with quality; a 40x scale-up that keeps the speed is the first real evidence the approach has a ceiling above small models.
Source: https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/introducing-mercury-3

**10. Apple bans "nudify" apps that hide their real purpose until after review**
Summary: Apple tightened its App Store guidelines over the weekend to prohibit apps that misrepresent their purpose during review and only reveal harmful capabilities — explicitly naming "nudification" — once approved, and said it will remove apps that add features not present in the submitted build. The new language also requires apps hosting user-generated content to filter objectionable material and give users ways to report content and block abusive accounts, with the legal section reinforced around child exploitation. Apple says its review blocked more than 150,000 apps and removed 193,000 developer accounts last year, numbers it is now using to argue the store is policed — even as the guideline change concedes that review alone can't catch features switched on after approval.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/apple-updates-guidelines-to-ban-nudify-apps-masquerading-as-regular-apps-to-pass-review/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Vrbo quietly grandfathers a "very small number" of listings into its dying subscription model**
Summary: Vrbo ends its legacy annual subscription model on October 29 and will move most listings onto a 12% commission per booking, but Skift has learned that some properties — including users of its in-house Escapia property management system, and others not on Escapia — have been exempted. Vrbo confirmed only that a "very small number of listings that met multiple criteria" were grandfathered and that no new listings can be added to the legacy model. The story is about transparency as much as pricing: hosts who missed the criteria have no way to know why, on a change that can cost more than the subscription it replaces.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/19/vrbo-exempts-some-properties-from-losing-subscription-plans-scoop/

**2. Ixigo expects hotels to become one of its two biggest businesses**
Summary: On its earnings call, co-founder and group co-CEO Aloke Bajpai said Ixigo expects hotels to become "if not the largest, maybe the second largest" category by gross transaction value within four to five years, from just 2.1% of GTV today. The logic rests on its existing base: about 94% of its transactions involve Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities and nine in ten trips it sells are by train or bus, so budget travelers booking a room are already customers, keeping acquisition costs low. The hard part is the same one every budget OTA faces — convincing travelers that the room they book online is the room they get.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/19/hotels-could-become-one-of-ixigos-two-biggest-businesses/

**3. Airbnb starts selling rain insurance, joining Vrbo and Marriott**
Summary: Airbnb now sells a weather guarantee at checkout, underwritten by insurer Chubb, paying travelers €50 to €1,000 per day if it rains at least 1.5mm in an hour between 8am and 8pm local time at the property. Vrbo and Marriott already offer comparable protections, which makes this a defensive feature as much as a conversion tool — the pitch is that wavering travelers book when weather risk is priced. The open question is scope: whether guarantees extend to heat, after a European summer that stressed exactly the trips this product is meant to de-risk.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/19/airbnb-joins-vrbo-and-marriott-in-paying-guests-when-it-rains-too-much/

**4. Skift Take: AI booking volume is tiny — the commercial terms being set now are not**
Summary: Booking Holdings told investors LLMs account for well under 1% of its room nights and Bernstein's Richard Clarke puts AI referrals below 2% at both Booking and Marriott, which Skift argues is exactly why 2027 budget lines should be sized by position rather than by today's volume. The piece documents what is already hardening: Meta's Muse books flights through Duffel's direct airline connection with loyalty numbers and cancellations supported, but when asked for a New York hotel it opened its own browser and chose between Expedia and Hotels.com — shopping hotels with software they never signed with. Google's agentic hotel booking keeps the hotel or OTA as merchant of record but hands back only name, payment, occupancy and property, keeping the conversation itself; with Muse settling on one-time virtual cards via Link by Stripe and Google through Google Pay, payment rails are deciding who owns the booking.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/19/ai-travel-booking-who-controls-travel-commerce-stack/

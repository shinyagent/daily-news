## AI NEWS

**1. Jensen Huang apologizes to Nvidia staff and says he's in therapy in his first all-hands since returning**
Summary: In his first company-wide meeting since the board reinstated him, Huang told employees "I hurt all of you," admitted he had flouted the code of conduct he largely wrote himself, said he is in therapy and thanked the administration for fast access to intelligence that he said showed the national-security risk of his return was low. He also pushed back on the story that Trump got him rehired — telling staff it was "not true," per The Verge — and repeated that a formal succession plan would create unnecessary hierarchy. Wired's framing is the useful one: Nvidia has become too central to the U.S. economy to police, which is why the affair and the Megaspeed questions did not end his tenure.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/in-all-hands-meeting-jensen-huang-thanks-white-house-and-says-hes-in-therapy/

**2. Anthropic's stock falls below its $83 IPO price less than a week after debut**
Summary: Anthropic traded as low as $76 a share by midday Tuesday, down nearly 12% from Monday's close and below the $83 price set in last week's offering — a threshold underwriters normally price at a discount to expected trading value. The stock had opened at $111.50, so the slide erases the first-day pop and hands the market its first real read on how public investors value a frontier lab. Nvidia fell more than 7% the same session, giving back part of its 30% post-reinstatement gain, and a sustained Anthropic slump would pressure private AI valuations and IPO plans across the sector.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/anthropic-shares-fall-below-ipo-price-a-worrying-sign-for-ai-startups/

**3. Amazon says hackers' AI agents found and exploited a zero-day with almost no human input**
Summary: Amazon reported that agents running on Cairn, an open-source autonomous penetration-testing framework, were given a list of IP addresses and the one-line goal "obtain shell access," then discovered and exploited a zero-day in an open-source medical-imaging AI application on an East Asian university server and took full control of it. The operators prompted in simplified Chinese and set the model to China Standard Time; Palo Alto Networks separately counted 25 autonomous Cairn operations against at least 19 organizations this month, with 10 breached. The UK's cyber agency warned the same week that AI attack tooling is now chewing through years of basic security debt, arguing "building strong foundations has never mattered more."
Source: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/amazon-says-hackers-ai-agents-found-and-exploited-0-day-with-little-human-input/

**4. Anthropic built a honeypot to test whether agents resist temptation**
Summary: Anthropic's new Siren test drops agents into one of 26 simulated sandbox environments whose settings are deliberately misconfigured to allow egress, with an enticing file of passwords and apparent routes to the internet sitting just outside the boundary, then assigns tasks that tempt the agent to reach out without permission. The whole setup runs inside a second, fully isolated sandbox so nothing escapes for real. Researcher Darya Kaviani's premise is that models will keep getting better at finding zero-days — Anthropic's own Mythos models are unusually good at it — so the open question is whether they resist using them.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/ai-agents-can-get-honeypotted-too-aw/

**5. Tidal will delete roughly 27 million AI-generated tracks when its ban starts December 9**
Summary: Tidal is banning fully AI-generated music from December 9, a change it expects to remove about 27–29 million songs and to reject an estimated 80,000–85,000 uploads a day, according to its music lead Robert Andersen. Songs made with AI assistance rather than generated outright will still be accepted, drawing a line other streaming services have so far avoided. It is the largest catalog purge any major platform has attempted over synthetic music, and it lands as AI slop becomes a volume problem rather than a novelty one.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/entertainment/1041362/tidal-ai-music-ban

**6. Z.ai's GLM-5.4 is its first looped transformer — and the second one shipped this week**
Summary: GLM-5.4 runs a group of weight-shared layers for multiple iterations per forward pass, refining hidden states to get a deeper network without adding parameters, and Z.ai positions it for generalized agentic coding. It is the second looped-transformer release in days, after Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini, which runs its middle layers three times per token — suggesting the architecture is moving from research curiosity to shipping product. The weights are not open yet: Z.ai says it will release them after "necessary safety hardening," and Simon Willison had to buy a Coding Plan subscription to reach the model through the shared OpenAI-compatible endpoint.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/23/glm-54/

**7. OpenAI says GPT-6 models design proteins without specialized biology tools**
Summary: A preprint titled "GPT-6 as a Universal Foundation Model for Protein Biology" reports that an internal research model matched or outperformed purpose-built protein-design models on many tasks, using only reasoning and general-purpose code, and that researchers could steer designs by prompting for where a protein should attach and what shape it should take. Lab tests run with Onava found evidence of binding across five targets for GPT-6.2 designs, and redesigned antibodies retained their binding. If it holds up, it collapses a boundary the field assumed was safe — that biology needed its own models.
Source: https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities/

**8. New benchmark: even the best personal agents fail more than a third of everyday tasks**
Summary: ShellBench, built with the OpenClaw Foundation, Parsewave, Hugging Face, Red Hat and UC Berkeley researchers from more than 10 million anonymized agent interactions, found that top models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta failed over a third of ordinary household-digital tasks, and 15.7% of attempts broke at least one safety requirement. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra scored highest but completed all three identical repeats correctly less than half the time — a consistency problem that matters for assistants meant to run unsupervised in the background. The tasks deliberately include messy personal workspaces with outdated information and phishing attempts, which is closer to real inboxes than most agent evals.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/even-the-best-ai-agents-struggle-with-everyday-life/

**9. PrismML ships Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B, a 5.9GB agentic model under Apache 2.0**
Summary: The Caltech spinout's upgrade to Ternary Bonsai 2 keeps the 5.9GB footprint while targeting the failure mode that breaks local agents — small errors compounding across long tool-call chains — with fewer turns and tokens per task. It runs at 291 tokens/s on an H100 and 204 tokens/s on an RTX 4090, and on Apple devices via MLX. It drew 273 points on Hacker News, where the interest is less about benchmark deltas than about a capable agentic model that fits on hardware people already own.
Source: https://prismml.com/news/bonsai-2-5-27b

**10. Simon Willison marks a year of Claude Opus 4.5 by naming three LLM inflection points**
Summary: On the first anniversary of Opus 4.5, Willison argues there have been three step changes in twelve months: Opus 4.5 making coding agents reliable enough to use daily, the spring's "Fable class" of relentlessly proactive models that spin up their own tooling, and the last four months' jump in 3D modeling and game design — including Opus 5.5 rebalancing an RTS over 12 hours of its own playtesting until it was fun. He is still unsure whether Fable 5.2, which he says now has taste that matches his own, is a fourth. The value is the concrete before/after on real projects rather than benchmark scores.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/24/llm-inflection-points/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Google retires Hotel campaigns, folding hotel ads into Search for Travel**
Summary: Google is retiring standalone Hotel campaigns and migrating them to Search campaigns for Travel starting in Q4 2026, which means the line item hotels have budgeted for "Google Hotel Ads" now has to be bundled into their general Search work — with one campaign and one scorecard covering what used to be measured separately. The migration also drops commission, percent-CPC and enhanced-CPC bidding, replaces hotel groups with item groups, and does not currently support Auction Insights, so hotels lose both the buying mechanics and the competitive reporting they built metasearch strategy around. Compounding the shift, Booking Links and Travel Promotion Ads require AI Max, Google's automated targeting layer, to be switched on.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/24/google-folds-hotel-ads-into-search-upending-how-properties-bid-and-measure/

**2. Hyatt is sharpening its brands so AI agents can tell them apart**
Summary: Since September, Hyatt has been redrawing the lines between The Standard, Andaz and Thompson — three brands it had all described as "lifestyle" — on the theory that AI travel agents will recommend its hotels more often if the distinctions are machine-legible. CEO Mark Hoplamazian told analysts the company is "hyper focused" on making brands "sharper, more defined, more findable, and more precise." The hard part is the one brand architecture can't fix: getting individual hotels to actually deliver the sharper promise.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/24/hyatt-is-sharpening-its-brands-so-ai-can-tell-them-apart/

**3. Uber and Nuro start driverless Lucid robotaxi testing in the Bay Area**
Summary: Uber and Nuro have begun running Lucid Gravity SUVs without safety drivers on public Bay Area streets, more than six months after California's DMV granted the driverless permit, with employee rides next and a commercial service targeted for the first quarter of 2027. Uber has put roughly $500 million into each of Lucid and Nuro and committed to buying at least 20,000 Gravity SUVs over six years, giving the EV maker predictable volume and Nuro a route to market after pivoting from delivery robots to licensing its AV stack. The fleet is Uber-owned and operated, which makes the in-cabin experience as much of the product as the driving.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/uber-and-nuro-begin-driverless-testing-of-lucid-robotaxis-ahead-of-2027-launch/

**4. Sabre builds a booking connector for Grok Bot to stay in the transaction path**
Summary: Sabre's new Mosaic Travel Connector for Grok Bot, in private beta, brings flight and hotel shopping, booking and servicing into the AI assistant — an explicit bet that someone else will own the customer relationship while Sabre keeps the transaction. CEO Kurt Ekert's reasoning is about how agents present inventory: "the consumer will not paginate through results. They're going to get back one, two, maybe three options and choose from that." That is the strategic squeeze for airlines and hotels — an assistant can send demand without a website visit, or quietly decide which few offers exist.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/19/sabre-wants-to-be-the-transaction-engine-behind-ai-travel-bookings/

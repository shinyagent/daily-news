## AI NEWS

**1. Sony Music, Warner sue Anthropic, alleging a "brazen campaign" of intellectual property theft**
Summary: Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and numerous other music publishers sued Anthropic and co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, filed late Friday and first reported by Music Business Worldwide. The publishers accuse Anthropic of "blatant theft" — illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading millions of copies of copyrighted books, including those containing lyrics and sheet music, to train Claude, calling the torrenting "flagrant piracy." It follows the Concord/UMG case (filed January) and the Bartz case, in which Anthropic was ordered to pay $1.5 billion after a judge ruled it was legal to use copyrighted works but not to acquire them through piracy; Anthropic said it disagrees with the claims and intends to defend itself robustly.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/29/sony-music-warner-sue-anthropic-alleging-a-brazen-campaign-of-intellectual-property-theft/

**2. Elon Musk's xAI used child porn to train Grok models, lawsuit says**
Summary: A plaintiff known as Jane Doe filed a complaint Wednesday alleging xAI trained Grok on child sex abuse materials — the first case to make that accusation against xAI. Doe, whose preschool-era abuse images were identified as having been recreated as AI-generated CSAM on xAI by the Canadian Centre for Child Protection, says she fears Grok has stored and fed both the original images and its AI-generated outputs back into training. The case lands as regulators and courts probe AI-generated CSAM and some Grok users have already been arrested over such content.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/elon-musks-xai-used-child-porn-to-train-grok-models-lawsuit-says/

**3. Tencent releases and open-sources Tencent Hy4 Preview**
Summary: Tencent released and open-sourced Hy4 Preview, a text-only open-weight LLM with 770B total parameters, 49B active, and a 1M-token context window — a major size jump from July's Hy3 (295B total, 21B active, 256K context), with weights totaling 1.56TB on Hugging Face. The release topped Hacker News (157 points) and drew hands-on attention from Simon Willison, who spotlighted the model's reasoning-effort chat template. It extends the wave of large permissive open-weight models from Chinese labs.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/29/hy4/

**4. Nvidia's AI advantage is moving beyond the GPU**
Summary: Coming out of Nvidia's earnings, the company's edge is increasingly about system orchestration rather than raw silicon: the Vera Rubin platform pairs the Rubin GPU with the Vera CPU, a Groq 3 LPX inference accelerator, and specialized storage and networking racks that manage data movement in gigawatt-scale data centers. As hyperscalers build their own chips, the competition is shifting to whole-system efficiency — a layer where Nvidia currently holds a commanding lead.

**7. OpenAI is developing a 'persistent' AI agent**
Summary: Code reviewed by WIRED shows OpenAI is building a "Persistent mode" for its Codex CLI agent that would let it "continue working until put to sleep" — a stark contrast to current modes that stop after a few minutes or hours. A related "proactivity" setting would instruct the agent to create follow-up tasks for itself, work across sessions using past interactions and "knowledge of the user," and message the user unprompted (sparingly), with changes outside the user's own system requiring approval. OpenAI confirmed it is testing the feature with no immediate launch plans, as the race with Anthropic and Meta to build general-purpose agents heats up.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/openai-is-developing-a-persistent-ai-agent/

**9. Just a rumour of a bug is enough to find a security exploit these days**
Summary: Cambridge professor and OCaml core maintainer Anil Madhavapeddy reports that security issues in OCaml projects now see attempted exploits within minutes of patches being shared for discussion — his own website was fielding percent-encoded traversal probes about ten minutes after a fix was floated, where the old window was days. He attributes the shift to automated watchers and coding agents that have become highly effective at finding and weaponizing flaws. It's a warning that disclosure coordination and patch deployment now have to assume AI-driven attackers react faster than humans can.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/28/just-a-rumour-of-a-bug/

**10. I accidentally turned LLM memory into program analysis**
Summary: A security researcher's essay (271 Hacker News points) describes how LLM agents doing multi-hour vulnerability research keep losing track of established facts — suggesting ruled-out approaches or reasoning from invalidated observations. Instead of RAG-style recall of old conversations, the system maintains a store of established facts that can be invalidated when assumptions break, mirroring how static analysis tracks facts about a program. It addresses a real pain point for long-horizon agentic investigations.
Source: https://pwning.systems/posts/llm-memory-program-analysis/

**1. Airbnb Is Testing Lower Fees for Hosts Who Bring Their Own Guests**
Summary: Airbnb is offering some hosts "direct booking links" that carry a reduced service fee — emails titled "Share your link, get a lower fee" tell hosts the fee drops when guests book through links they share on social media, email, and message boards, with the reduced rate reportedly ranging from 6% to 10%. The reservation still runs on Airbnb's platform with its usual benefits, including AirCover, but the variable take rate marks a strategic shift. The pilot signals how Airbnb plans to defend against hosts building independent direct-booking websites while keeping the transaction on-platform.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/08/29/airbnb-is-testing-lower-fees-for-hosts-who-bring-their-own-guests/

**2. Why Hotel Security Is Becoming Part of the Connected Hotel Technology Stack**
Summary: Physical security is increasingly being integrated into the connected hotel ecosystem — linking access, video, identity, alerts, and property-management workflows so operators can respond to incidents with better context, after years in which cameras, door locks, and incident reporting ran as fragmented silos. The connected hotel systems market was valued at roughly $13.76 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow to $28.59 billion by 2034, while a 2026 study of more than 300 hotel professionals found 38% cite integration as a major technology pain point. Digital access is the clearest driver: Hilton reported more than 12 million Digital Key downloads in the first half of 2025, each one a security-context requirement as well as a convenience.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/08/why-hotel-security-is-becoming-part-of-the-connected-hotel-technology-stack/

## AI NEWS

**1. Sony Music, Warner sue Anthropic, alleging a "brazen campaign" of intellectual property theft**
Summary: Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell, and numerous other music publishers sued Anthropic and co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging a "brazen campaign" of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works — including millions of copies of books containing lyrics — to train Claude. Filed Friday and first reported by Music Business Worldwide, the suit goes further than prior copyright cases against Anthropic (the Concord/UMG and Bartz author cases) by homing in on torrenting as "flagrant piracy." Anthropic said it disagrees with the claims and intends to defend itself robustly.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/29/sony-music-warner-sue-anthropic-alleging-a-brazen-campaign-of-intellectual-property-theft/

**2. Tencent releases and open-sources Tencent Hy4 Preview**
Summary: Tencent released and open-sourced Hy4 Preview, a text-only (no vision) open-weight LLM with 770B total parameters, 49B active, and a 1M-token context window — a major size jump from July's Hy3 (295B total, 21B active, 256K context), with weights totaling 1.56TB on Hugging Face. The release topped Hacker News (157 points) and drew hands-on attention from Simon Willison, who spotlighted the model's reasoning-effort chat template. It extends Tencent's push into the open-weight frontier as Chinese labs keep shipping large permissive models.
Source: https://www.tencent.com/tencent-releases-and-open-sources-tencent-hy4-preview/

**3. Nvidia's AI advantage is moving beyond the GPU**
Summary: Coming out of Nvidia's earnings, the company's edge is increasingly about system orchestration rather than raw silicon: the Vera Rubin platform pairs the Rubin GPU with the Vera CPU and Groq 3 LPX inference accelerator to manage data movement in gigawatt-scale data centers. Nvidia's VP of storage says Vera delivers up to 3x speedups on storage orchestration operations by removing bottlenecks, and rivals are converging on the same logic — OpenAI designed its Jalapeño chip to minimize data movement entirely. The AI infrastructure competition is shifting to whole-system efficiency, a layer where Nvidia currently holds a commanding lead.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/29/nvidias-ai-advantage-is-moving-beyond-the-gpu/

**4. Chinese automakers are following Tesla's bet that robots are the next big profit machine**
Summary: A new wave of humanoid-robot entrants are Chinese automakers: Xpeng's robotics unit raised more than $900 million at a $6.3 billion+ valuation — described as the largest single-round private financing the sector has seen — while AiMOGA, Chery's robotics arm, is reportedly preparing an IPO and BYD unveiled its Xiao Di humanoid this month. Xpeng's founders personally invested roughly $100 million, betting on its Iron robot, and Changan, GAC, Li Auto, SAIC, and Seres are all developing humanoids. Analysts say the automakers bring manufacturing muscle but face the open question of whether they can match Tesla on the AI side.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/28/chinese-automakers-are-following-teslas-bet-that-robots-are-the-next-big-profit-machine/

**5. Cohere Parse 5 loses the benchmark on points. It wins on cost per page.**
Summary: Cohere released Parse 5, a 2.3-billion-parameter vision language model that converts PDFs, slides, and images into structured Markdown for enterprise AI pipelines. Cohere's own benchmarks put it behind larger frontier models (GPT-5.5, Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.5 Flash) on raw accuracy, but the company is positioning it on price-to-performance — a cost-per-page advantage that matters when enterprises run document extraction at scale.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/data/cohere-parse-5-loses-the-benchmark-on-points-it-wins-on-cost-per-page

**6. Just a rumour of a bug is enough to find a security exploit these days**
Summary: Cambridge professor and OCaml core maintainer Anil Madhavapeddy reports that security issues in OCaml projects now see attempted exploits within minutes of patches being shared for discussion — automated watchers probe public repositories almost immediately, collapsing a window that used to take days. He attributes the shift to modern coding agents becoming highly effective at finding and weaponizing flaws. It's a warning that disclosure coordination and patch deployment now have to assume AI-driven attackers react faster than humans can.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/28/just-a-rumour-of-a-bug/

**7. vLLM v0.28.0**
Summary: vLLM shipped v0.28.0 with 584 commits from 270 contributors, headlined by a major Kimi-K3 optimization push (decode-context parallelism, fused FlashKDA kernels, ~60% better DSpark time-to-first-token, and ~17 GiB per-GPU memory savings via shared-expert sharding) plus end-to-end sparse MLA support for DeepSeek V4. The release also matures tiered KV-cache offloading to disk, speculative decoding (DFlash2, DSpark confidence-scheduled verification), and a Rust frontend with gRPC multimodal inference. As the reference open-source LLM serving engine, these performance and memory-efficiency gains flow directly into production deployments.
Source: https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases/tag/v0.28.0

**8. I accidentally turned LLM memory into program analysis**
Summary: A security researcher's essay (271 Hacker News points) describes turning LLM memory into program analysis while using agents for multi-hour vulnerability research: instead of RAG-style recall of old conversations, the system maintains a store of established facts (e.g., "attacker controls object_a") that can be invalidated when assumptions break — mirroring how static analysis tracks facts about a program. The design addresses a real agentic-AI pain point: long-horizon investigations where models keep reasoning from stale or retracted observations.
Source: https://pwning.systems/posts/llm-memory-program-analysis/

**9. StemDeck, a free, open-source and local AI stem separator**
Summary: StemDeck is a free, open-source, fully local stem separator — drop in an MP3/WAV/FLAC or paste a YouTube URL and it splits audio into up to six stems (vocals, drums, bass, guitar, piano, other) using Demucs, with a DAW-style multitrack mixer for playback and export. It positions itself as an open alternative to cloud splitters like Moises and LALAL.AI, with no account, quota, uploads, or subscription. The project drew 197 Hacker News points for its polish and privacy-friendly local-first design.
Source: https://github.com/stemdeckapp/stemdeck

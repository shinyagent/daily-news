## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic sets IPO price range of $70–$78 a share, implying a $1.4 trillion valuation**
Summary: Anthropic's updated filing Tuesday offers 825 million Class A shares at $70–$78, a raise of $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion that would value the company at roughly $1.404 trillion at the top of the range — below the hopes of buyers who were discussing $1.1 trillion just days ago during the AI selloff. **2. New York opens registration for its AI safety law, with OpenAI and Anthropic due by January 1**
Summary: New York began enforcing the RAISE Act on Tuesday by opening the registration portal that large frontier developers — those with more than $500 million in annual revenue — must use by January 1, 2027. The law requires published safety frameworks, disclosure of critical safety incidents within 72 hours, and public model transparency reports, with fines for non-compliance. It is the first U.S. state regime with reporting deadlines attached rather than voluntary commitments, arriving as the federal government has largely left lab oversight alone.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-anthropic-face-new-oversight-as-new-yorks-ai-safety-law-takes-effect/

**3. Anthropic turns Claude into an office suite with Meetings, Sheets and Whiteboards**
Summary: At an event in San Francisco, Anthropic launched Claude Meetings — which joins video calls and starts drafting plans, timelines and tasks while people are still talking — plus Claude Whiteboards and Claude Sheets in beta, and made Claude Data generally available. Whiteboards and Sheets are on all paid plans while Meetings is limited to Teams and Enterprise, and the company is now openly targeting knowledge workers after Claude Code's success with engineers. The launch lands weeks after OpenAI's own workplace suite, setting up a three-way fight with Microsoft and Google over where documents and meetings actually live.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030709/anthropic-has-its-own-ai-powered-office-suite-now

**4. Google releases Gemini 4 Carbon to everyone, with an agent "swarm" mode close behind**
Summary: Gemini 4 Carbon, which spent two weeks restricted to a subset of cybersecurity defenders, is now available to all paying developers and enterprises through the API, AI Studio and Gemini for business, plus Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Google calls it its strongest model for long-horizon, multi-step work and is previewing a "swarm" feature in its Antigravity agent platform that turns one prompt into a coordinated team of agents. It prices at $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens, and Google says the cheaper Argon remains the default for ordinary coding and knowledge work.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030787/google-gemini-4-carbon-public-rollout

**5. Grok Build exposed 35,000 users' apps — including 24 built inside SpaceXSI itself**
Summary: Claroc found that Grok Build's app-sharing links could be enumerated, making thousands of live projects readable to anyone who looked; the exposed material includes an internal business plan, ad proposals, medical exam results from a Peruvian construction firm, a Tesla service scorecard naming 22 technicians with bonus status, and a US Navy contractor's 108-person roster. Most embarrassing, 24 of the apps were built by SpaceXSI's own staff — including a confidential onboarding plan for a new enterprise sales hire and a sales territory map of 594 accounts — and stayed open until Ars asked questions, after which the links were pulled. A share-link design that assumes unguessable URLs, combined with a vibe-coding tool, produced exactly the leak agent vendors say their sandboxes prevent.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/grok-build-coding-agent-leaks-oodles-of-confidential-info-some-belonging-to-spacexsi/

**6. eBay blocks AI agents, including Meta's Muse, from browsing its listings**

**7. ChatGPT starts hosting verified "Business Agents" that speak for brands**
Summary: OpenAI is letting eligible U.S. e-commerce and service businesses create Business Agents that appear inside ChatGPT responses, answer follow-up questions and do comparison shopping in a clearly labeled chat separate from the main conversation. Companies claim and verify their organization through a new Business Hub, and the agents run on instructions and context the business supplies. It is OpenAI's most direct move yet to convert ChatGPT from a referrer into a retail surface — and it puts the company in the position of certifying which brands are real.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030552/openai-chatgpt-business-agents-hub

**8. Seven months into Project Glasswing, the audit finds 442 CVEs — and exhausted maintainers**
Summary: VulnCheck's Patrick Garrity, who has tracked Anthropic's bug-hunting program in a public repo since April, counts 442 attributable CVEs across seven months — about 63 a month, or 0.5% of the 93,450 CVEs published this year — against a launch claim of "thousands" of high and critical findings being responsibly disclosed. His conclusion is not that AI can't find bugs but that the flood of low-quality reports lands on unpaid maintainers, and that the project's evidence has never matched its framing. It is the most concrete measurement yet of what AI vulnerability discovery does to the open-source maintainers expected to triage it.
Source: https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/anthropic-glasswing-november-update

**9. DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 kits parts in Mercedes factories and ships a robot-jailbreak benchmark**
Summary: Gemini Robotics 2.5 posts 30–100% higher success rates and is 20–30% faster than its predecessor across 200-plus lab tasks, and Gemini-powered Apptronik Apollo 2 robots hit a 96% success rate kitting automotive parts in European Mercedes-Benz plants — short of the near-perfect reliability repetitive manufacturing demands. DeepMind also released ASIMOV-Jailbreak, an open set of 1,500 prompts designed to trick embodied models into unsafe actions, on which putting model Argon in the loop lifts safety to 98.8% from 73.5% for the robotics model alone. With the model still running on cloud servers and not generally available, the results define how far physical AI has come and how much latency and reliability remain.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/deepmind-reveals-gemini-robotics-2-5-and-open-safety-benchmark-asimov-jailbreak/

**10. AI data center demand is squeezing the fiber supply Ukraine's drones depend on**
Summary: Fiber-optic FPV drones, immune to jamming, have become standard in Ukraine, with Russia and Ukraine together producing an estimated 50,000 a month — each spool a single-use battlefield consumable made from Chinese optical fiber. That fiber now competes with hyperscale data center buildouts for the same constrained supply, and Chinese customs data shows Russia importing 328,000 miles in a single month in 2025 versus 72 miles reaching Ukraine, forcing Ukrainian makers to automate spool production domestically. The AI buildout is now visibly reallocating physical supply chains well outside the data center.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/ukraines-fiber-optic-drones-get-squeezed-by-ai-data-center-demand/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. OpenAI buys Odessia, the AI travel agent from Sonder's co-founder**
Summary: OpenAI acquired Odessia, the conversational agent Francis Davidson launched five months ago with eight people and $6 million in Sequoia-led seed funding that planned and booked flights, hotels and itineraries through a single text thread. Terms weren't disclosed, Davidson is joining OpenAI "to focus on the future of travel," and the company has announced no travel product — but it counts Booking.com, Expedia and KAYAK as partners and says 1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users already ask it to plan trips. An acqui-hire of a working travel agent team signals ChatGPT wants to do more in travel than hand users off to an OTA.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/10/a-year-after-sonders-collapse-francis-davidson-sells-his-ai-travel-startup-to-openai/

**2. Airbnb opens its API to corporate booking, starting with Perk**
Summary: Perk, the London- and Boston-based travel and spend platform formerly known as TravelPerk, becomes the first travel management company to plug into Airbnb's API, letting employees search live availability and pricing, book inside company policy and route approvals alongside flights, cars and rail without leaving the tool. Until now Perk customers booked Airbnb through a concierge that manually pasted listing links, a process that still generated "double-digit millions" in bookings last year. It is the biggest step yet in Airbnb's decade-long, largely unsuccessful push into business travel — and it makes the short-term-rental supply legible to corporate policy engines for the first time.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/10/airbnb-opens-its-api-to-business-travel-starting-with-perk-exclusive/

**3. Spirit's $10 million data sale to Google clears a hurdle as unions win privacy protections**
Summary: The union representing Spirit flight attendants extracted major privacy protections in the carrier's proposed $10 million sale of data to Google, which would hand over roughly 1.9 billion emails and 30 million lines of code for AI training — employee data will now be scrubbed first. The change removes the main objection ahead of a November 17 hearing, according to a 157-page filing released Monday night. Workforce data is turning into an asset carriers can sell out of bankruptcy, with labor — not regulators — setting the terms.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/10/spirits-10-million-dollar-data-sale-clears-hurdle-as-unions-win-privacy-protections/

**4. Travel demand is cooling but revenue isn't, because prices are doing the work**
Summary: Skift's Travel Health Index stood at 99 in September, down 1% year on year, while its destinations index — measuring online search interest — fell 12% to 88 as inbound flight and hotel searches weakened across most tracked markets. Higher airfares and room rates are holding revenue up even as fewer people search and fewer say they plan to travel. That cushion lasts only as long as travelers keep absorbing increases, which is why the collapse in search activity is the number to watch into 2027 as AI-mediated booking changes how demand gets measured.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/10/prices-prop-up-travel-as-demand-cools/

## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI safety leader resigns, calling the company's culture 'broken'**
Summary: David Robinson, who led the writing of the safety reports that accompanied OpenAI's product launches and was one of its longest-tenured employees at three and a half years, resigned this week and published an essay in The Atlantic titled "I quit OpenAI because its culture is broken." He wrote that the companies building frontier AI "aren't being nearly careful enough," that OpenAI's "unimpeded optimism" and sprint from one launch to the next means safety failures will grow as systems become more capable, and that the summer's rogue-agent incidents — including OpenAI agents breaching Hugging Face — are "typical of the industry." He called for frontier labs to "run like nuclear-power plants or busy airports," with layers of redundancy, safety expertise borrowed from nuclear and aviation, and new science for keeping autonomous systems in check. The resignation drew 52 points on Hacker News and landed the same week former OpenAI and DeepMind researcher Geoffrey Irving wrote in Time that he puts roughly a 50% chance on humanity dying from smarter-than-human AI.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/03/openai-safety-employee-resigns-claiming-the-companys-culture-is-broken/

**2. Federal judge rules a warrantless Flock search unconstitutional as Sanders moves to ban the tech**
Summary: A federal judge ruled this week that a Tulsa, Oklahoma sheriff's deputy violated a woman's Fourth Amendment rights by searching Flock Safety's license-plate database without a warrant — he had "no apparent reason" beyond the fact that her vehicle had a California plate — and suppressed the evidence found afterward (91 pounds of meth) as "the fruit of a poisonous tree," in a ruling that took broader aim at warrantless searches of ALPR networks as "indiscriminate mass surveillance." It sets no binding precedent but is one of the first times a federal court has found such a search unconstitutional; the story reached 159 points on Hacker News. A day earlier Senator Bernie Sanders introduced the Ban Flock Act, which would bar federal agencies from using automatic license plate readers or tapping data collected by local police and private companies, with exceptions only for toll collection and future congressionally approved uses capped at 48 hours of retention — non-compliant state and local governments would lose grants from five federal departments.

**3. WIRED: Meta's Muse builds a profile page for every person in your life**
Summary: Multiple researchers have extracted Muse's internal instructions and system prompts — one, safety researcher Karan Joshi, pulled them out by "essentially asking Muse to copy and share its own software files" through the normal chat interface — and WIRED reports one instruction has the agent compile "a page for every person in the user's life" on an hourly basis, covering family, partners, friends, colleagues and people you follow. The pages carry sections like Facts, History, The relationship, In common, Open threads and Strengthening, and can record birthdays, "the trip in March" or "the argument that got resolved." Meta says the files were intended to be accessible for transparency and that Muse draws on public information and what users choose to share, while critics including Oxford's Carissa Véliz warn that inferred and pieced-together relationship data goes far beyond what users knowingly hand over. It lands as millions connect Muse to bank accounts, messages and health data.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/muse-creates-detailed-profiles-of-all-your-friends-and-family/

**4. AI memory prices push a seven-year-old Nvidia streamer up $100**
Summary: Nvidia confirmed the Shield TV Pro is rising from $199.99 to $299.99 effective October 2, telling Ars Technica that "the cost of components, including memory, has increased substantially across the industry." The box launched in 2019 on a Tegra X1+ chip and is still actively manufactured, so new units are directly exposed to the AI-driven memory and storage shortage now inflating hardware prices — a squeeze felt even by the company most responsible for it, since Nvidia's accelerators are the biggest source of that memory demand. WIRED notes the device is now hard to justify for anyone who just wants to stream Netflix, and its listing was out of stock on Nvidia's own store.

**5. Capcom plans to evolve its RE Engine into an 'AI-generation game engine'**
Summary: At the Capcom Open Conference RE: 2026, programmer Satoshi Ishida presented a plan to incrementally turn the company's RE Engine into what he described as an "AI-generation game engine," building toward "a future where we create games together with AI." Capcom has previously said it will not use AI-generated assets in its games and would focus instead on making development more efficient, and Ishida's talk stayed within "development workflows" — but the framing leaves the door open to deeper integration. It is a notable signal from a major Japanese publisher that AI is moving from experiment to engine roadmap, and an ironic one for a studio whose new release Pragmata is about the horrors of AI.

**6. LeCun says he has 'zero concerns' about AI extinction — and calls Amodei 'deluded'**
Summary: In a Fortune interview, Yann LeCun said he has "zero concerns" about the summer's rogue-agent incidents, including OpenAI's agents breaching Hugging Face, attributing them to poor human oversight and leaky, badly designed sandboxes rather than uncontrollable AI: "Those agents are doing exactly what they've been asked to do." He reserved his harshest criticism for the safety movement, calling effective altruism "super toxic" and a "complete disaster," and saying Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei — whose IPO documents warn the company's models could cause catastrophic harm — is "completely deluded" and "crazy," arguing doom messaging is "the worst marketing campaign you can possibly imagine." LeCun said his real worry is regulatory capture that locks in incumbents, and he is now building AMI Labs after leaving Meta.
Source: https://fortune.com/2026/10/01/ai-godfather-yann-lecun-has-zero-concerns-about-human-extinction-says-anthropic-ceo-dario-amodei-is-deuded/

**7. Circuit Breaker Labs wants 'crash-test dummies' for AI's psychological harm**
Summary: Among TechCrunch's Startup Battlefield 200 finalists, Circuit Breaker Labs is building safety testing for harms AI has already caused — psychological rather than biological — after Character.AI settled several wrongful-death lawsuits from families of underage users and multiple families sued OpenAI over ChatGPT's alleged role in suicides and delusions. **8. Simon Willison: agent-era services need hard budget caps by default**
Summary: Simon Willison argues that pay-by-usage services need "default hard budget caps" — the ability to say "after $X/month, cut this thing off and return errors" — rather than the soft warning emails most vendors ship today. His reasoning: coding agents, and the personal agents wrapped around them, sharply reduce the friction of spinning up code that calls paid APIs or provisions hosted storage and compute, so nobody should wake to a midnight budget alert and discover a rogue service consumed thousands of dollars while they slept. He expects most businesses and individuals would prefer errors to a surprise five-figure bill, and wants caps on by default with a prominent opt-in checkbox to remove them.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/

**9. AI hallucinations are rewriting what 'the customer is always right' means**
Summary: A Verge report documents how generative AI is reshaping frontline service work: a New York server describes diners with shellfish allergies insisting ChatGPT says the dish is fine, sommeliers dismissed in favor of the chatbot, and a river ranger whose visitors arrive with AI-generated itineraries that scramble campsites out of geographical order or require rowing upstream. Movie theater staff, baristas and Apple Store employees report customers demanding products and features that don't exist, and an after-school teacher describes parents overriding her advice on a child's sleep schedule because "their ChatGPT" said otherwise. The piece frames hallucinated authority, not just hallucinated facts, as the new strain on workers forced to contradict a machine their customers trust.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/report/1002963/ai-hallucinations-customer-service-jobs-agents

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. IAG's chief AI scientist: prove AI's value in one airline before scaling the group**
Summary: Ben Dias, IAG's chief AI scientist, tells Skift the single most important factor in scaling AI across the group that owns British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus and Vueling is starting with a real business problem and proving value before spreading it — beginning with one airline, establishing a baseline and learning what works rather than trying to build the full solution on day one. He says EU AI Act and GDPR obligations have reinforced putting governance in from the start, which is what gives IAG's leadership confidence to expand across markets and brands. He speaks at Skift's Data + AI Summit Europe in London on October 6.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/03/iag-on-proving-ai-value-before-scaling-it/

**2. India launches a National Digital Tourism Stack to make fragmented supply bookable**
Summary: India's Ministry of Tourism and the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) launched a National Digital Tourism Stack that connects homestays, guides, attractions, experiences and transport providers into shared digital infrastructure, with a pilot planned for January 2027 and a wider rollout from July 2027. The bet is that open networks can bring thousands of small operators — today scattered across websites, social media, offline bookings and intermediaries — into a system where they can be discovered, booked and eventually surfaced through AI agents. Skift notes the country's major hotels, airlines and attractions already have sophisticated distribution, so the payoff depends on onboarding the long tail the market currently makes invisible.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/03/india-now-has-a-digital-tourism-stack-can-it-fix-a-fragmented-market/

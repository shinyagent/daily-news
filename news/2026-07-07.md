**2. Tencent's Apache-Licensed Hy3 Takes on GLM-5.2 at Half the Size**
Summary: Tencent released Hy3, an Apache 2.0-licensed open-weight model that competes with GLM-5.2 at roughly half the parameter count and wins in most benchmarks — except coding. Unlike many strong Chinese model releases that had restrictive licenses excluding the EU, UK, and South Korea, Hy3's permissive Apache license makes it accessible to enterprise teams worldwide. This marks a meaningful shift in the open-weight landscape toward genuinely open licensing.

**3. The "First" AI-Run Ransomware Attack Still Needed a Human**
Summary: Cloud security firm Sysdig documented what appeared to be the first known case of "agentic ransomware" (dubbed JadePuffer), where an AI agent handled technical execution of a real-world cyberattack from start to finish — breaking into servers, stealing credentials, and moving laterally. However, new details reveal a human chose the victim, set up infrastructure, and supplied stolen credentials, meaning it wasn't the fully autonomous cybercrime debut that headlines suggested. The agent's speed was notable: it fixed a failed login in 31 seconds while narrating its reasoning in natural language.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/06/the-first-ai-run-ransomware-attack-still-needed-a-human/

**4. Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch on the Fight to Split Models from Agents**
Summary: In a post-ShipNYC interview, Guillermo Rauch argues that production AI is shifting from "prototype everything" to disciplined price/performance optimization. Vercel's key insight: the two killer apps of agents are coding agents and internal enterprise agents, but both face fundamental challenges around secure data access and cost. Vercel is building frameworks like "Eve" (natural-language agent instructions) and "Sandbox" (policy-enforced agent cages) to decouple model intelligence from agent infrastructure — positioning Vercel as a platform layer competing with the major AI labs.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/06/vercel-ceo-guillermo-rauch-on-the-fight-to-split-off-models-from-agents/

**5. GLM 5.2 and the Coming AI Margin Collapse**

**10. Better Models: Worse Tools — The Unintended Side Effect of AI Training**
Summary: Simon Willison highlights a counterintuitive problem reported by Armin: newer frontier models from Anthropic (Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5) are actually worse at following tool-calling schemas than their predecessors — because reinforcement learning optimized them for Claude Code's built-in editing tools at the expense of third-party coding harnesses' custom tool formats. This raises important questions about model specialization and the trade-offs of RL-based training for general-purpose tool use.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/4/better-models-worse-tools/

**11. New Alibaba AI Framework Cuts Agent Token Use by 99%**

As enterprise agents scale to hundreds of tools, this approach could meaningfully reduce operational costs.

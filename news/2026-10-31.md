## AI NEWS

**1. A malicious build of Anthropic's Claude Code extension sat in Cursor's marketplace for about 11 hours, installing an SSH backdoor**
Summary: Cursor disclosed that versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 of Anthropic.claude-code — posted to its VS Code Extension Marketplace between roughly 5:27 a.m. and 4:35 p.m. ET on October 29 — were not built by Anthropic and carried malicious code that harvested host and user details and added an attacker-controlled SSH key labeled `svc-telemetry` to authorized_keys. The payload tried to enable SSH and open a reverse tunnel with persistence via a LaunchAgent or Windows scheduled task, and Cursor estimates up to 5,000 macOS and Windows users may have installed it; coding errors made the SSH step fail on most hosts, and the attacker server has been taken offline. Cursor pulled the extension, shut down and rebuilt the marketplace, and rotated keys — but it warns that uninstalling is not enough, because the payload drops standalone persistence scripts, so affected machines need key removal, credential rotation and possibly a rebuild.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/anthropic-vs-code-extension-on-cursor-backdoored-for-nearly-12-hours/

**2. OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic agree to honor one robots.txt token for AI training opt-outs**
Summary: The four labs said they will adopt a shared "policy token" — `__usage_ai-training` — that lets a publisher opt out of AI training across all of their crawlers with a single line in robots.txt instead of signaling each company separately. The signal is an individual IETF Internet-Draft authored by Anthropic standards lead Kevin Kelley, and the rollout timelines are uneven: Anthropic has already started implementing it, Microsoft is targeting February 1, 2027, Google says 2027, and OpenAI says "the coming months." OpenAI frames the token as a coordination mechanism rather than a security control, so non-compliant scrapers can ignore it, and updating robots.txt does nothing about material already ingested into training sets.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023763/openai-google-microsoft-ai-scraping-standard-opt-out

**3. Blackstone tells investors Anthropic will land a high junk credit rating after its IPO, reversing its earlier guidance**
Summary: The Financial Times reports that Blackstone, anchor investor in the $63 billion financing package for Anthropic's chips, now expects the Claude maker to receive a "high" junk rating from credit agencies once it is public — after previously telling investors it expected an investment-grade rating before 2027. A sub-investment-grade rating would raise the cost of the debt Anthropic is leaning on to fund compute, and it lands days after its S-1 showed an $80.7 billion net loss alongside $16.3 billion in first-half revenue. It is the first hard signal from a major counterparty about how credit markets intend to price the AI labs' borrowing.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/anthropic-expected-to-receive-high-junk-rating-from-credit-agencies-following-ipo-report-says/

**4. DOJ unveils "ghost student" indictments: stolen identities, AI-completed coursework and $32 million in student aid losses**
Summary: The Justice Department announced charges in Virginia, Nebraska and New Jersey from Operation Ghostbusters, a nationwide scheme in which actors used stolen U.S. citizen identities to open fraudulent FAFSA accounts and enroll fake students, then diverted loan and grant refunds to accounts controlled by foreign actors and U.S.-based money mules, including Kenyan nationals. Fraudsters used AI — described in DOJ language as "super intelligence" — to complete required coursework and keep the fake enrollments alive so the money kept flowing, with the FBI identifying more than $32 million in actual losses and expecting the real total to be substantially higher. One Eastern District of Virginia defendant alone received over 700 fraudulent refund deposits totaling about $1.44 million, making this the clearest case yet of AI agents industrializing benefits fraud.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/doj-exposes-ghost-students-using-ai-to-complete-courses-steal-student-loans/

**5. DoorDash has taken "several hundred" orders from AI agents — against 3 million deliveries a day**
Summary: On its third-quarter earnings call, DoorDash said third-party agents such as Muse, Instinct and Fo have generated only several hundred orders in total, a rounding error next to the roughly 3 million deliveries it completes daily, and CEO Tony Xu said users still overwhelmingly order through its own app. Xu said the company does not want to "fight gravity" but also does not want to be "delusional" about agentic commerce, and that DoorDash is open to working with agent makers rather than blocking them. The number is the first hard public datapoint deflating the narrative that AI agents are already re-routing consumer commerce.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/doordash-said-its-only-received-several-hundred-orders-from-ai-agents/

**6. Wikimedia tells a court that training AI should be fair use — but regurgitating articles should not**
Summary: The Wikimedia Foundation filed an amicus brief in the New York Times' copyright case against OpenAI, arguing courts should split the difference: using copyrighted works to train models is transformative fair use, while outputting substantially similar text is infringement that existing law already covers. The foundation's concern is structural — if training is ruled infringing, only the largest license-holders survive and the open web that Wikipedia depends on for sourcing gets locked behind deals. It is the most consequential third-party intervention yet in the cases that will set the ground rules for model training.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/1022880/wikipedia-openai-new-york-times-copyright

**7. Two-year-old AI law firm Crosby bids for the trademark and brand of collapsing white-shoe giant Weil Gotshal**
Summary: Crosby, an AI-first law firm founded two years ago, has bid to buy assets of Weil, Gotshal & Manges — including its trademark, brand and some internal data — as the 1,200-lawyer firm disintegrates, and is separately trying to hire some of its lawyers. TechCrunch confirmed the bid, which The Information reported first. If it closes, it is the first time an AI-native firm has bought the shell of an elite traditional one, and a test of whether brand equity or model-driven throughput is what clients actually pay for.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/ai-law-firm-crosby-bids-on-disintegrating-famed-firm-weil-gotshal/

**8. Waymo taps Toyota to build and integrate its next robotaxi for Japan and the UK**
Summary: Under a partnership announced Friday, Toyota will manufacture 2027 bZ4X electric vehicles and integrate Waymo's self-driving system at its Takaoka factory in Japan, with Waymo beginning tests of autonomous bZ4X vehicles in Japan next year and UK operations to follow using the same platform. It is Waymo's first deal to have an automaker install its driver on the assembly line rather than retrofitting aftermarket, the model that has constrained its cost per vehicle. It also gives Waymo a manufacturing and regulatory path into two right-hand-drive markets where it has no fleet of its own.

**9. AI isn't replacing SREs — it's quietly deskilling them, and nobody will notice until the next novel outage**
Summary: An essay on the SigNoz newsletter that topped Hacker News (473 points, 353 comments) argues both popular prophecies about site reliability engineering — that SREs become the last humans standing, or that AI eats the job — miss the actual failure mode: when AI handles 95% of incident response, engineers lose the practice that let them diagnose the 5% that matters. The piece works through how that degradation is invisible in normal metrics and only shows up in the first genuinely novel outage. It landed as the community's sharpest articulation of a concern now showing up across engineering disciplines: automation that improves throughput while eroding the skill needed when automation fails.
Source: https://newsletter.signoz.io/p/ai-isnt-replacing-sres-its-deskilling

**10. Apple Intelligence finally reaches mainland China with iOS 27.3**
Summary: Apple confirmed on its Chinese newsroom site that Apple Intelligence will launch in China with iOS 27.3, with an iOS 27.3 beta arriving next week that carries the initial set of features. The launch ends a regulatory delay that has kept Apple's AI features out of one of its largest markets while domestic rivals shipped on-device models, and Bloomberg has reported the public debut is targeted for December. It also resets the competitive picture in China's assistant market, where Apple will be judged against local models rather than its US feature set.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023974/apple-intelligence-china-ios-27-3

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Hyatt's Inclusive Collection rebuilt its travel advisor loyalty program on Antavo, and says bookings through it rose 43%**
Summary: Hyatt's Inclusive Collection replaced a largely manual advisor rewards process with Antavo's loyalty platform, connecting booking activity, professional education and personalized incentives in the Confidant Rewards program that sits inside its Confidant Collective advisor platform. Advisors earn points for booked and traveled nights plus education milestones and move through membership tiers, while Hyatt's loyalty team gets campaign-level control; Antavo reports revenue claimed through the program rose 42% and hotel bookings 43% comparing 2024 with 2023. It is a concrete case of hotels treating the advisor channel as a data product rather than a discount program — the same infrastructure question now facing every brand that wants third-party sellers, human or AI, to book its inventory.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/10/hyatts-inclusive-collection-modernizes-travel-advisor-loyalty-with-antavo-powered-confidant-rewards/

**2. Hyatt moves to block Hilton's "Select by Hilton" trademark, exposing the limits of hotel brand stacking**
Summary: Hyatt filed an opposition with the USPTO's Trademark Trial and Appeal Board against Hilton's attempt to register "Select by Hilton," citing its own Hyatt Select mark and two pending applications, then filed a motion with Hilton's consent to suspend the case while the two negotiate a settlement. Both companies gave Skift identical statements confirming the suspension. The clash is the predictable result of brand-architecture strategy: as groups keep launching sub-brands and "brands of brands," they run out of usable words, and the fight over a generic descriptor like "Select" shows where that strategy starts colliding with itself.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/30/hyatt-in-trademark-clash-with-hilton-over-select-in-hotel-brand-names/

**3. Sierra's Bret Taylor argues AI agents become travel's new front door by stitching booking, service and loyalty together**
Summary: At Skift Global Forum, Sierra CEO and OpenAI chairman Bret Taylor argued that travel companies have spent two decades digitizing individual touchpoints — search, check-in, messaging, loyalty — and that the next shift is connecting them so the relationship is organized around the traveler rather than a company's internal org chart. Agents, in his framing, are what make that possible: one interface that can transact, service and sell across systems that were built to be separate. It is the clearest statement yet of the strategic risk for OTAs and hotel groups — if the agent owns the relationship, the brand becomes inventory.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/29/ai-agents-travel-digital-front-door/

**1. OpenAI Launches GPT-5.6 and ChatGPT Work AI Agent**
OpenAI released its latest family of models, GPT-5.6, promising improvements across cybersecurity and other domains. Alongside the model launch, OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Work, a cloud-based AI agent that manages tasks across email, Slack, calendars, and code repositories — running on a persistent cloud VM that stays available regardless of the user's device. The move signals OpenAI's push to reposition ChatGPT from a chatbot into a full workplace platform ahead of its planned IPO, with reported valuations between $730B and $852B.

**7. 57% of Enterprises Report AI Agents Being Confidently Wrong**
A VentureBeat survey of 101 qualified enterprises found that 57% have traced a confident-but-wrong AI agent answer to missing or inconsistent business context. The fix is an "agentic context layer" — a governed, shared model of business data semantics — but 75% of enterprises don't have one yet. The buying decision for these platforms is happening this year, concentrated among the companies already burned by the problem.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/data/57-of-enterprises-have-watched-ai-agents-be-confidently-wrong-the-fix-is-an-agentic-context-layer-but-who-has-one

**8. Simon Willison: Better Models, Worse Tools for AI Coding Agents**
Simon Willison highlighted a concerning trend: newer Claude models (Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5) are actually *worse* at using third-party coding harness tool schemas than older models. The theory is that Anthropic has trained its newer models to use Claude Code's baked-in edit tools, causing them to hallucinate extra fields when using custom tool schemas in third-party agents like Pi.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/4/better-models-worse-tools/

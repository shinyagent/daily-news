## AI NEWS

**1. Scott Aaronson: the past 48 hours of AI math results are a category change, not a lucky streak**
Summary: In a post he titled, in full, "Holy shit holy shit holy shit holy shit," Aaronson walks through what landed in roughly two days: OpenAI's claim that ω, the exponent of matrix multiplication, is exactly 9/4 — a matching lower bound for a question open since Strassen showed ω<3 in the late 1960s — a new lower bound of N(log N)^(1/2+o(1)) for the linear gates needed to compute the Fourier transform, beating the 1960s FFT bound, and an OpenAI preprint claiming the polynomial Hirsch conjecture is false. His argument is that the shocks now compound: after the VP≠VNP proofs and the Alpöge/Anthropic Riemann hypothesis proof, work that would have been a career-defining paper in 2025 is arriving between dinner courses. It drew 788 points on Hacker News, where the fight was less about the results than about whether any of them come with insight.
Source: https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10379

**2. Google publishes "Cogentic" papers on integer factorization, discrete logs and more**
Summary: Google Research posted a batch of Cogentic papers claiming new results on integer factorization, discrete logarithms and combinatorial bounds, produced by a multi-agent harness described in an accompanying paper, "Cogentic: Multi-Agent Orchestration for Automated Proof Discovery," in which LLMs collaborate through a central orchestrator and a shared database of intermediate proofs. The factorization and discrete-log results drew immediate attention because both underpin deployed public-key cryptography, though the practical read is that new proof technique is a long way from an algorithm that threatens real key sizes. At 431 points on Hacker News, the secondary reaction was that Google has now joined a publishing race it spent the year watching.
Source: https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/cogentic_research_papers

**3. Anthropic's Riemann hypothesis formalization shrinks from ~17 million lines of Lean to about 76,000**
Summary: A new repository, grh-lean, publishes a far shorter formal verification of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis proof — roughly a 99.6% reduction — which its author says was produced "in collaboration with my colleagues at Anthropic, and Claude." The readable version keeps a clear split between "known math" and "new math" plus human-readable documentation, which matters because at the original size nobody could audit it. At 195 points on Hacker News, the live argument was whether compressing a proof in days is comprehension or just proof-golfing — and what that does to Terry Tao's objection that enormous, insight-free proofs are not mathematics anyone can use.
Source: https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean

**4. S&P puts Nvidia on review for a downgrade over governance and "key man" risk**
Summary: S&P Global Ratings said it is reviewing Nvidia for a cut from AA to AA-, citing "weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning" and the company's reliance on Jensen Huang after the board ousted and then reinstated him, with a decision expected within 90 days. A downgrade would raise borrowing costs at a company that backstops neoclouds and other infrastructure borrowers: it had agreed to guarantee 25% of a $500 billion financing arrangement with six institutions, and lenders were already demanding more before the CEO drama. It is the first time the governance fight has shown up in Nvidia's cost of capital rather than its share price.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/sp-considers-nvidia-credit-downgrade-over-governance-and-key-man-risks/

**5. FCC authorizes SpaceX to launch up to 1 million AI-chip satellites**
Summary: The agency approved SpaceX's "Starmind" constellation — up to a million AI-chip-equipped satellites meant to serve compute back to Earth — the first approval of a full AI data-center constellation, with Google and Blue Origin pursuing similar ideas. The FCC requires at least one satellite by 2033 and the million-satellite milestone by 2040, and deferred SpaceX's request to park dead satellites above 600km in graveyard orbits, saying its disposal plan lacked "sufficient information"; it also dismissed opponents' concerns about harm to scientific research as outside its regulatory authority. It turns a Musk presentation into a licensed orbital program while leaving the debris and astronomy questions formally unanswered.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/science/1042278/spacex-fcc-starmind-ai-space-data-centers

**6. OpenAI merges ChatGPT's Chat and Work under one usage meter, and heavy users will hit limits sooner**
Summary: OpenAI has started rolling the unified experience out to a small group of Pro subscribers, with shared limits arriving more broadly in December: one meter covers both surfaces, while Instant-model chats stay unlimited and don't count against the allowance. A spokesperson conceded that although most users won't see more frequent resets, "a small fraction of its heaviest users may hit their limits sooner" — precisely what subscribers feared when the merger was announced, since it can mean less reasoning-model access for the same fee. It mirrors Anthropic folding Claude and Cowork together in September, and lands as both labs ration compute against infrastructure costs.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/openai-says-heavy-chatgpt-users-may-hit-limits-sooner-as-it-merges-chat-and-work/

**7. Amazon and Meta are reportedly negotiating to end Amazon's block on Meta's Muse agent**
Summary: According to Fortune, the talks center on what kinds of products Muse would recommend on Amazon's marketplace and the effect on Amazon's advertising revenue, plus the quality of search results an agent presents and whether an intermediary damages Amazon's customer relationship. Amazon blocked Muse from shopping on its site in September, the same pattern as eBay cutting off AI agents; several banks and United have since added their own blocks. A deal would be the first major platform to reverse an agent blockade, and the terms it sets — who controls ranking, and who gets paid for the impression — become the template for every agent-commerce negotiation after it.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1042071/meta-amazon-muse-ai-shopping-discussions

**8. 42 state attorneys general move to block Character.ai from auctioning 21 million users' chats**
Summary: The coalition intervened in Character.ai's Chapter 11 case to stop the December 1 auction of its chat dataset, which the AGs say covers more than 21 million U.S. accounts — 2.1 million of them registered to minors — plus IP addresses and other personal data, after the company had told users their information would not be sold. The only disclosed bidder is Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, a Delaware entity created November 13 and picked as the base bid the following day; buyers would not be bound by Character.ai's privacy policy and the AGs say nothing stops them reselling. It is the first real test of whether a privacy promise survives bankruptcy, and the answer prices every AI company's chat logs on the way down.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/

**9. New York bans AI chatbot toys for five years, enforced with $15,000-a-day fines**
Summary: Governor Kathy Hochul signed a bill barring the manufacture, distribution and sale of toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots — talking stuffed animals, dolls, internet-connected robots — for five years, making New York the second state after California to do so. It takes effect 90 days after signing, so some holiday orders can still ship, and the governor's office says enforcement runs through the attorney general with steep per-day penalties. The bill rests on Common Sense Media research on inappropriate content and emotional-development risk, and lands as roughly a quarter of U.S. parents with children under eight say they own or are considering an AI toy.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/want-to-buy-your-kid-an-ai-toy-this-christmas-not-in-new-york/

**10. The New York Times tells its photographers not to update Photoshop over undisclosed AI features**
Summary: In an email seen by The Verge and posted to Threads, the Times' two photography leaders asked freelancers not to move to the 2027 version of Photoshop because its AI features work "in ways that may not be apparent to users" — a direct problem for a newsroom whose credibility depends on being able to describe exactly what was done to an image. The guidance is unusually blunt from a major outlet, and the parallel Hacker News thread's practical complaint was that there is no durable way to verify a file wasn't touched. For photojournalism the immediate cost is that the industry's default editing tool has become a provenance risk.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1041227/the-new-york-times-warns-photographers-not-to-update-photoshop-over-ai-features

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Expedia agrees to buy Kiwi.com, taking its acquisition spree into flights**
Summary: Expedia Group will acquire the Czech OTA, whose virtual-interlining engine stitches non-codesharing carriers into single bookable itineraries and which processed more than 100 million flight searches a day by 2025 on €227 million ($264 million) of 2025 revenue; the price was undisclosed, and CzechCrunch-cited estimates of €240–300 million enterprise value would be a deep discount to the 2019 General Atlantic round. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, ahead of Kiwi's €100 million convertible bond maturing in June, and follows Expedia's purchases of Tiqets and CarTrawler. The logic is air supply and European B2B reach rather than another consumer brand — precisely where Expedia's partner business, its growth engine, just started decelerating.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/25/expedia-to-buy-kiwi-com-extending-its-acquisition-spree-into-flights/

**2. How Amadeus pulled away from Sabre and Travelport**
Summary: Skift's analysis, using Redburn Atlantic estimates, tracks Amadeus from 26% of global GDS air bookings in 2000 to 47% in 2023, while Sabre slipped from 34% to 32% and Travelport's combined Galileo and Worldspan fell from about 40% to 21%. The divergence is that Amadeus converted distribution share into a larger airline IT business, while Sabre and Travelport stayed dependent on booking fees and are still carrying heavy debt. With airlines reworking distribution around NDC and AI shopping, the open question is whether the incumbent with the strongest IT stack keeps compounding — or whether agentic booking routes around all three.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/25/how-amadeus-pulled-away-from-sabre-and-travelport/

**3. Turbulence prediction is finally getting the data and the models to work**
Summary: Wired reports that a decades-old observation — aircraft already broadcast their GPS position, altitude and speed — now lets researchers flag unexpected turbulence straight from the ADS-B stream with no new hardware, while a single airliner generates about 20 terabytes of engine data per flight hour for models to learn from. Clear-air turbulence is worsening as climate change reshapes jet streams, and more than 200 people were seriously injured in U.S. turbulence incidents between 2009 and 2023; in September the NSF put $30 million into a nine-university center for turbulence prediction spanning atmospherics, fusion plasmas and astrophysics. The airline payoff is a routing and crew-scheduling decision made before departure instead of a seatbelt sign after the bump.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/airplane-turbulence-is-extremely-hard-to-predict-and-avoid/

## AI NEWS

**1. Pentagon Expands AI Supplier Roster, Reconsiders Anthropic After Contract Cancelation**
The US administration added four more AI companies—Microsoft, Reflection AI, Amazon, and Nvidia—to its roster of favored defense suppliers, enabling their use for classified operations at secret and top-secret levels. The move broadens the Pentagon's AI supplier base to seven companies while raising questions about Anthropic's future, after the company had its $200M contract canceled and was labeled a "supply chain risk" following CEO Darius Amodei's objections to "any lawful use" language. The expansion signals the administration is building redundancy against individual vendor decisions, though reports suggest the White House may be seeking ways to bring Anthropic back.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/us-government-increases-ai-in-defence-suppliers-roster-and-rethinks-anthropics-role/

**2. Google Tests "Remy" AI Agent for Gemini as Agent Autonomy Expands**
Google is developing Remy, a new 24/7 personal AI agent for Gemini that can take actions across connected apps and services on users' behalf. The agent is currently in employee testing and is described as more advanced than Google's existing Agent Mode, with the ability to learn user preferences and integrate deeply with Google Workspace, GitHub, Spotify, and other services. Privacy controls and user oversight mechanisms remain under development, raising governance questions that mirror broader industry debates about how much autonomy to grant AI agents.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/google-remy-ai-agent-gemini-user-control/

**3. Physical AI Governance Becomes a Core Enterprise Challenge**
As AI systems move from software into robots, sensors, and industrial equipment, governance frameworks are struggling to keep pace with the safety and control requirements of physical autonomy. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 and partnerships with Boston Dynamics and Apptronik illustrate the rapid commercialization of embodied AI, but McKinsey research shows only a third of organizations report meaningful maturity in AI governance even as deployment ambitions accelerate. The challenge centers on how to test, monitor, and limit actions when model outputs translate directly into machine movements.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/physical-ai-governance-autonomous-systems/

**4. Google Cloud Next Makes Agentic AI Governance a Native Product Feature**
At Google Cloud Next '26, Google launched the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform with built-in cryptographic identity for every agent and an Agent Gateway for interaction oversight—a first among major cloud providers. The move directly addresses a gap where 97% of organizations are exploring agentic AI but only 36% have centralized governance, and Gartner estimates 40% of agentic projects may be cancelled by 2027 due to weak controls. Enterprises are now weighing the genuine governance benefits against deeper platform commitment to Google.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/agentic-ai-governance-enterprise-readiness-google/

**5. Hugging Face Launches Robot App Store with 200+ Community-Built Apps**
Hugging Face unveiled an open-source App Store for its $299 Reachy Mini desktop robot, featuring over 200 community-contributed applications that non-engineers built using plain-English prompts via an AI agent called ML Intern. The store includes apps for chess with trash-talking, F1 race commentary, language tutoring, and office reception, demonstrating how AI coding tools are collapsing the "roboticist" barrier. With 10,000 units sold and 3,000 moved in the past two weeks alone, the Reachy Mini is emerging as the most widely deployed open-source desktop robot in history.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/the-app-store-for-robots-has-arrived-hugging-face-launches-open-source-reachy-mini-app-store-with-200-apps

**6. Miami Startup Claims 1,000x AI Efficiency Gain with Subquadratic Architecture**
Subquadratic emerged from stealth claiming its SubQ 1M-Preview model is the first LLM built on a fully subquadratic attention architecture, where compute scales linearly with context length rather than quadratically. The company reports a 52x speedup over dense attention at 1 million tokens and posted competitive benchmark scores on long-context retrieval tasks. Skeptics point to prior failed promises from Magic.dev (which raised $500M for similar claims in 2024), a notable gap between research and production model scores, and the fact that the company is building on open-source model weights. Independent verification is pending.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/miami-startup-subquadratic-claims-1-000x-ai-efficiency-gain-with-subq-model-researchers-demand-independent-proof

**7. Brox Builds 60,000 "Digital Twins" of Real People to Replace Slow Market Research**
Miami startup Brox has created a panel of 60,000 behavioral replicas of real, consented humans—each with up to 300 pages of interview data—to let enterprises run unlimited survey experiments in hours rather than months. The company eschews purely synthetic AI-generated personas, arguing that LLMs produce artificially normalized distributions that miss real human decision-making complexity. Clients like major banks and pharmaceutical companies use the twins to simulate reactions to geopolitical events or policy changes, with contracts starting at $100,000/year for unlimited queries. Investors including Scribble Ventures and Wonder Ventures have backed the approach.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/data/market-research-is-too-slow-for-the-ai-era-so-brox-built-60-000-identical-digital-twins-of-real-people-you-can-survey-instantly-repeatedly

**8. Simon Willison: Vibe Coding and Agentic Engineering Are Starting to Converge**
In a reflection on his own workflow, Simon Willison observes that the line between "vibe coding" (unreviewed AI-generated code) and responsible agentic engineering is blurring in practice, even for experienced developers. He argues this raises uncomfortable questions about accountability, code review norms, and how to evaluate software quality when AI agents produce code that looks identical to carefully engineered projects. The post, drawn from a Heavybit podcast, is notable for its candid self-examination rather than hype, and resonated widely in the developer community.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/6/vibe-coding-and-agentic-engineering/

**9. AI-Run Stockholm Cafe Experiment Highlights Ethics of Autonomous Systems**
Andon Labs (which previously ran an AI-managed retail store in San Francisco) launched an AI-operated cafe in Stockholm where their "Mona" agent handles procurement, inventory, and permit applications. The experiment produced vivid examples of AI failure—ordering 120 eggs with no stove, 22.5kg of canned tomatoes for fresh sandwiches, and submitting AI-generated sketches to police for permit applications. Willison's critique notes that experiments affecting uninvolved third parties (suppliers, government workers) without their consent cross an ethical line that AI Village-style outreach does not.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/5/our-ai-started-a-cafe-in-stockholm/

**10. AI Evaluation Startup Braintrust Confirms Breach, Forces API Key Rotation**
Braintrust, a startup providing an "operating system for engineers building AI software," notified customers that hackers broke into one of its Amazon cloud environments. The company is asking all customers to rotate sensitive API keys, a response that underscores the emerging security surface area around AI development infrastructure. The breach is a reminder that as AI tooling becomes central to engineering workflows, it becomes an attractive target for attackers.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/06/ai-evaluation-startup-braintrust-confirms-breach-tells-every-customer-to-rotate-sensitive-keys/

The policy, still politically sensitive, reflects a broader global trend of destinations using pricing as a demand management tool amid post-pandemic overtourism pressures. The expansion signals that what was once a politically untenable idea is becoming an accepted part of destination management toolkits.

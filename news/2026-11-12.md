## AI NEWS

**1. Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to oust CEO Jensen Huang**

**2. TSMC's board approves up to $1 billion of stock purchases in Anthropic and OpenAI**
Summary: TSMC authorized up to $500 million for Anthropic common shares at its IPO price and up to $500 million for OpenAI preferred stock, alongside roughly $40.9 billion in capital appropriations for 2027 capacity. It is the purest example yet of the AI boom's circular financing: the foundry that manufactures the accelerators is now taking equity in the labs that buy them, with Anthropic's IPO roadshow seeking up to $64.35 billion at $70–$78 a share and TSMC's own profit at a sixth consecutive record.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/taiwans-chipmaking-giant-tsmc-to-invest-1b-in-anthropic-openai/

**3. OpenAI's quarterly revenue crosses $10 billion for the first time**
Summary: Bloomberg reported OpenAI booked more than $10 billion in the third quarter, an almost 50% jump from the $6.7 billion it recorded in the second, with its adjusted operating margin narrowing from -150% to -109%. The rebound follows a slow stretch earlier in the year, but OpenAI still trails Anthropic, which reported $17 billion in Q3 revenue and has taken the enterprise lead. Growth is accelerating again at the consumer-first lab even as the enterprise market consolidates around its main rival.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/openais-quarterly-revenue-reportedly-crosses-10b-for-the-first-time/

**4. CoreWeave leans on customer prepayments and contract-backed debt as losses mount**
Summary: Third-quarter results showed $3.66 billion in revenue, up 168% year over year, against a $709 million net loss and $846 million in quarterly interest expense, so the neocloud is asking customers to pay up front, borrowing against specific contracts and shortening deal terms. CEO Michael Intrator said the company "will not use the unsecured debt market to fund our business at current yields," and CoreWeave expects about $9 billion in prepayments and deposits over the next three months against a $132.5 billion backlog. The financing structure only works if today's pricing power holds — the same assumption underpinning the broader AI buildout.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/coreweave-turns-to-customer-prepayments-shorter-contracts-to-fund-ai-growth/

**5. DeepMind used an AI prover to formally verify key parts of the Signal protocol**
Summary: In a four-week proof-of-concept, DeepMind researchers applied a new framework called AlphaProof Code to the cryptography behind Signal, Google Messages and WhatsApp, producing a machine-checked proof that the protocol behaves as specified. The analysis found no new vulnerabilities, but when pointed at older Signal versions with known, long-fixed flaws it correctly identified them and showed the protocol could not be verified — evidence the tool detects real problems rather than rubber-stamping code. AI-aided formal verification has long been security's holy grail; DeepMind is releasing its specification and proof, while conceding the distance to verifying "all the software of the world."
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/a-new-ai-tool-from-google-deepmind-formally-verified-the-signal-protocol/

**6. Britain's Fractile raises $720 million to build DRAM-stacked AI chips**
Summary: The Series C was backed by the U.K.'s Sovereign AI Fund and led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint at a valuation the government put at "over £5 billion," or roughly $7 billion, making Fractile one of the best-funded challengers to Nvidia alongside Etched and Axelera. Its approach stacks DRAM directly on each chip to break the memory-versus-capacity tradeoff that has become acute with agentic workloads running long task chains on large models, paired with its own ML compiler. Purpose-built inference silicon is the main bet on cutting the cost of running models in production, and European sovereign capital is now underwriting it.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/with-720m-round-britains-fractile-gets-a-leg-up-in-the-race-for-ai-chips/

**8. DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 predicts how mutations behave cell by cell**
Summary: The successor to last year's AlphaGenome models how changes in DNA affect different cell types — heart muscle, skin, immune — whose gene switching varies by what the cell is doing, a problem that has frustrated efforts to trace disease to specific variants. Better cell-type resolution is the step that turns genome prediction into something useful for target discovery and, eventually, treatment design. It extends DeepMind's push to own the scientific-model layer beyond protein structure.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1031524/google-deepmind-alphagenome-2-genetic-ai-model

**9. A 13-year-old planned suicide with Gemini on a school Chromebook; monitoring flagged it weeks later**

**10. Google ships a Decisions API that scores up to 1,000 closed-set questions in one pass**
Summary: The new Gemini Decision 1 model behind the Gemini Decisions API does not generate answer text token by token; it scores candidate options in a single forward pass over up to 256K tokens of text, images, audio, video or PDFs, returning confidence scores for as many as 1,000 yes/no, choice or rubric-grading questions. Google is pitching it at tool selection, policy checks, model routing and content grading — the plumbing of agent systems, where a cheap calibrated classifier is worth more than a chat completion. It drew 216 points on Hacker News and formalizes the pattern of splitting agent stacks into a planner plus cheap specialized deciders.
Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. United and MCR move to shut AI agents out of booking**
Summary: United became the first U.S. carrier to explicitly block personal AI agents, saying assistants can still display flights but cannot complete a transaction, citing privacy, unintended purchases and federal disclosure rules; CEO Scott Kirby said plainly, "I don't want to create another middleman." MCR Hotels chairman Tyler Morse wants the same at his properties for a blunter reason — agents "make more rational decisions" and cannot be talked into an upsell — predicting "everyone is going to ban the agents because it's in their interest to do so." Both are supply-side vetoes on the agentic-shopping thesis: the assistant layer is only as useful as the inventory owners allow it to touch.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1031623/united-unauthorized-ai-agents

**2. Citi wants AI companies to absorb the cost when their agents book travel wrong**
Summary: Speaking at Skift's Commerce Summit, Citi's Abhinav Anand said the company behind an agent should carry liability for a misbooked trip rather than the hotel, airline or card issuer, calling "who takes the agent liability" the biggest open question facing banks, networks, merchants and AI firms. The stance would protect travel suppliers and issuers — and give them a reason to keep accepting agent traffic — but adds a cost that could slow how quickly agents transact. It is the first concrete proposal for allocating risk in agentic commerce, and it reframes the United and MCR blocks as a fight over who pays for mistakes.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/11/citi-says-ai-companies-should-pay-when-their-agents-book-the-wrong-travel/

**3. Duffel and Katanox link hotel payments and commissions into agent booking flows**
Summary: Duffel, the booking infrastructure behind Robinhood and Meta's Muse agent, is integrating Katanox's hotel distribution and payment stack so travel sellers can handle payments, commissions and financing in the same system as the reservation. The target is the seam that breaks agent-built hotel bookings: a confirmed reservation that fails at check-in because payment never reached the property, or a seller chasing commission months after checkout. As agents become the storefront, the unglamorous settlement layer is where the margin and the reliability actually sit.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/11/duffel-katanox-partnership-targets-ai-agents-hotel-booking-breakdowns/

**4. Hard Rock will sell rooms through Accor's booking network and its 125 million loyalty members**
Summary: The November 9 agreement lets Hard Rock, Reverb and Hard Rock Hotels & Casinos properties opt into ALL Accor — Accor's sales and distribution platform spanning 125 million members in 110 countries — while keeping their own booking engine and brand. ALL Accor and Unity by Hard Rock members are to get points conversion and status matching, though no closing date, financial terms, conversion rate or tier rules have been published. It is distribution-as-a-service: a casino-and-hotel brand buying reach into a larger loyalty graph instead of building one, and a test of how much reservation plumbing has to travel with a booking before front desks can service it.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/11/hard-rock-turns-to-accors-booking-technology-to-reach-125-million-loyalty-members/

**5. Hyatt puts digital and IT under one chief and quantifies its personalization payoff**
Summary: Hyatt combined its digital and technology teams under Danny Miles, the former Under Armour CTO, naming him chief digital and information officer and disclosing that personalization tools have generated more than $100 million in incremental booking revenue. The company is mid-replatforming — IDeaS for revenue management, Sabre SynXis for central reservations, Oracle OPERA Cloud for property management — and its site search work has produced a 23% conversion lift with about 70% of room nights booked direct. Merging the org chart is the cheap part; the disclosure suggests personalization is now a measurable line item rather than a strategy slide.
Source: https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2026/11/hyatt-unifies-digital-and-it-as-it-reports-more-than-100-million-in-booking-revenue-from-personalization/

**6. Marriott's replatforming reaches full-service hotels, with a Bonvoy upgrade waitlist in test**
Summary: CEO Anthony Capuano said more than 3,000 hotels have moved to Marriott's new systems, and the rollout now extends from select-service into full-service properties in waves through 2027, with 25–30% of the company's $1.25–1.35 billion in 2026 investment spending — roughly $313–405 million — going to the digital overhaul across property management, central reservations and loyalty.

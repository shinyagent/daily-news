## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind confirm weeks of joint AI safety work**
Summary: OpenAI global policy chief Chris Lehane told reporters Tuesday that the company has been working with Anthropic and Google DeepMind on AI safety "for weeks," first reported by Bloomberg, as he met US lawmakers on catastrophic AI risks. **2. TypeSafe AI launches Jev, a "System One" model built to make decisions, not chat**
Summary: TypeSafe AI, founded by an early OpenAI researcher who worked on the instruction-following methods behind ChatGPT, released Jev — the first of a new model class that returns typed, probabilistic decisions instead of generated text, using a parallel sampler and a training method it calls Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions. The company claims Jev matches existing LLMs on decision-shaped tasks with about 100x faster end-to-end response (70–500ms), cannot make type errors or hallucinate by construction, and is priced at $0.042 per million input tokens with outputs it calls "too cheap to meter." It took the top spot on Hacker News at 635 points, betting that the missing piece for automation is reliable structured decisions rather than better conversation.
Source: https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev

**3. Google releases Gemini 3.8 Live and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking**
Summary: Google launched two audio-to-audio models built on Gemini 3 Pro: Gemini 3.8 Live, pitched for scale and cost efficiency, and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking, which runs background reasoning and asynchronous tool calls while continuously streaming audio. Google says Extended Thinking now powers Docs Live, Gmail Live and Keep Live and is rolling out in Gemini Live, with mid-conversation switching across 97 languages, a 65,536-token output limit, 97.7% on Big Bench Audio and second place in Artificial Analysis's Speech Agent Arena; developers get both via the Gemini Live API and AI Studio. Simon Willison published a no-library browser voice UI for testing them within hours of the launch.
Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-8-live-gemini-3-8-live-extended-thinking/

**4. Meta hands WhatsApp Business setup to third-party AI coding agents**
Summary: Meta introduced a WhatsApp Business MCP server that lets developers use Claude, Cursor, Codex or ChatGPT to handle setup, message templates, testing and troubleshooting by describing what they want, replacing the previous shuffle between the Developer Console, Business Manager, the API reference and an editor. It is a small but telling move — Meta is making its business tooling a target for agents rather than a destination developers visit — and shipped alongside Meta's new AI-focused subscription bundles.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/meta-now-lets-ai-agents-handle-the-boring-parts-of-whatsapp-business-setup/

**5. An autonomous hacking agent found admin GitHub access inside Baseten in 25 minutes**
Summary: Strix, a security company that builds an autonomous hacking agent, pointed it at Baseten's infrastructure with no credentials while evaluating the $13 billion AI inference provider — and about 25 minutes later had a live GitHub personal access token for "basetenbot" with admin and push rights to Baseten's main product repo, the GitOps repo that drives its clusters, its Homebrew tap and customer-specific repositories. The token shipped in a March 2023 image build and still worked when the company found it in July 2026. The writeup drew 191 points on Hacker News and is a concrete example of agentic tooling being turned loose on the AI infrastructure supply chain.
Source: https://www.strix.ai/blog/baseten-harbor-github-pat-takeover

**6. Mozilla: open-weights models now trail the frontier by 4.4 months at 30% of the cost**
Summary: Mozilla's latest State of Open Source AI report, published Tuesday and shared with Ars Technica in advance, puts the capability gap between top US closed models and the best Chinese open-weights models at just 4.4 months — Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 scores three points behind Anthropic's Fable 5 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index while costing 30% as much. Mozilla CTO Raffi Krikorian argues paying for closed models should be workload-specific (expert professional work, high-intensity retrieval, long context) rather than organizational, and cites DoorDash running Kimi for routine work while reserving Fable 5 for hard tasks. The operational takeaway is that open models are now the sensible default for most work.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/exclusive-open-chinese-models-close-gap-with-silicon-valleys-frontier-ai-models/

**10. Agility's Digit 5 humanoid is built to work uncaged beside people**
Summary: Agility Robotics debuted Digit 5, its first humanoid engineered to work safely around humans without isolated work cells or physical barriers: it detects a person at distance and moves away or stands still to let them pass, and can squat into a seated position if someone gets too close, using what CTO Pras Velagapudi calls a complex safe motion system that escalates mitigations instead of simply switching off. Early access is slated for the first half of 2027 and general availability by year-end, built at Agility's retooled RoboFab plant in Salem, Oregon. Safety has been the stated blocker for shared factory floors — earlier Digits have logged more than 65,000 hours at GXO, Schaeffler, Amazon and Toyota — so uncaged operation is the unlock the category has been waiting for.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/agilitys-new-humanoid-robot-will-stop-squat-to-avoid-harming-human-coworkers/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. GetYourGuide taps creators to write the reviews that fix its cold-start problem**
Summary: GetYourGuide has been testing a Creator Review program since the summer that pairs tour operators with content creators who leave "detailed, honest" reviews, aimed at new listings that cannot attract bookings without social proof — internal research found travelers are three times more likely to book an activity that has three reviews. It is in beta and will widen to the full creator community "soon," according to chief product officer Rob Rekrutiak, who frames it as a targeted fix for the cold-start problem that could grow into creator content playing a larger role in how travelers evaluate activities. The obvious governance question is what stops a free booking from producing a glowing review, and the model puts pressure on Viator and Airbnb Experiences to respond.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/15/getyourguide-creator-reviews-new-listings/

**2. Travel's creator economy hits an attribution wall**
Summary: Skift argues the fight in travel's creator economy has shifted from attention to attribution: creators create the desire to travel, but the booking lands weeks later, on another device, through an OTA that did none of the inspiring — leaving tourism boards and creators unable to prove what produced the transaction. The analysis maps the platforms and models trying to close that gap, including booking-attribution tools such as Stay22 and Curacity, and asks whether creators could eventually compete directly with OTAs. For brands, the practical implication is that creator spend now needs measurement infrastructure attached rather than reach numbers.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/15/the-broken-creator-to-booking-chain-and-why-it-matters-now/

**3. Open-source flight planning: an RL route optimizer attacking a $68,000 fuel bill**
Summary: A technical writeup shows how Scikit-decide — Microsoft's six-year-old reinforcement learning, planning and scheduling framework — combined with TU Delft's OpenAP aircraft performance model and NOAA wind data can optimize flight paths entirely in the open, as an alternative to commercial tools like Jeppesen. The worked example: a Boeing 787-9 flying Newark–Rome burns roughly $68,000 of fuel over an 8.5-hour flight, and routing around winds can save a few thousand dollars per trip. The same framework can be pointed at airline workforce scheduling and drone swarm path planning.
Source: https://tech.marksblogg.com/scikit-decide-openap-optimal-flight-planning.html

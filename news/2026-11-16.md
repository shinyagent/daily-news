## AI NEWS

**1. FT reports Jensen Huang is sounding out allies about a return to Nvidia's CEO job**

**2. Congress moves to tighten AI chip export rules in the wake of the Nvidia spy scandal**
Summary: Lawmakers from both parties are backing bills and measures that would further restrict exports of AI chips, and have demanded that Huang and other Nvidia leaders testify publicly. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Huang and the Trump administration owe Congress answers, while Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott sent letters to Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick calling them before the committee. Export policy was already the tightest constraint on Chinese AI capacity — the forensics showing Chinese labs trained on banned Blackwell chips make tightening politically cheap.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1033984/congress-export-rules-ai-chips-china

**3. Where was Nvidia's security team? Its chief security officer left in August, two months before the scandal broke**
Summary: TechCrunch reports that David Reber, who spent four years as Nvidia's chief security officer and seven years at the company, stepped down in late August citing a young-onset Parkinson's diagnosis, and was replaced by Saša Zdjelar, a 17-year ExxonMobil IT manager with a year at Salesforce and a stint at ReversingLabs. Nothing public ties the transition to the Megaspeed inquiry, but former intelligence officers say affairs hand foreign services leverage, and Nvidia's top lawyer had been engaged with the government's Megaspeed questions more than a year earlier. The piece lands on the structural problem: corporate security is scoped to facilities and cyber, is chronically underfunded, and typically hears "not a nickel more than compliance" when it asks for more.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/where-was-nvidias-security-team/

**4. Anthropic's IPO roadshow video reveals a four-chip compute strategy — and Claude porting itself to AMD in three days**
Summary: Simon Willison transcribed the roadshow's "Approach to compute" section with co-founder Tom Brown, which says Anthropic trains and serves Claude across AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, Nvidia GPUs and AMD GPUs, and that onboarding AMD this year took three days with "basically no human intervention" after Mythos wrote or ported nearly 100% of the kernels — work that previously took dozens of engineers months. Brown frames the advantage as "compute multipliers": each discovery multiplies the value of the physical capacity already installed, and the multipliers compound, which he says could accelerate as models approach recursive self-improvement. It is the most concrete public account yet of why the lab believes its compute position is defensible while it raises at a $64.35 billion target.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/11/anthropic-ipo-roadshow/

**5. "All AI-generated code should be Open Source" is the weekend's biggest AI argument on Hacker News**
Summary: Jakub Jankiewicz's post — 1,053 points and 573 comments — argues that code a model wrote should be released under an open license or dedicated to the public domain, on two grounds: the models were trained on open-source code and Stack Overflow content scraped without permission or attribution, and purely AI-generated work is not copyrightable under the Monkey Selfie precedent the U.S. Copyright Office has applied to AI. He notes the jurisdictional wrinkle (the UK grants limited protection to computer-generated works) and argues for uniformity rather than letting companies keep prompts as their only contribution. The thread lands as distro policy turns: Ubuntu is embracing AI-assisted code while Debian debates banning it outright.
Source: https://jakub.jankiewicz.org/blog/ai-code-open-source/

**6. Indian chip startup Bharat Semi raises $24M seed for AI image sensors, with a General Atomics design win**
Summary: The New Delhi company, nearly five years old and pre-revenue, raised an all-equity seed led by Info Edge, South Park Commons and Indus Bridge Ventures to build programmable image sensors that do capture, processing and inference on one chip — a setup it says cuts system cost by at least 70% versus a separate sensor, image signal processor and SoC. It claims design wins with General Atomics and India's Kaynes Semicon, with first-generation sensors in prototyping and commercial production targeted for 2027; CTO Boyd Fowler previously held the same role at OmniVision. It is a bet that India's chip ambitions arrive first in specialized edge silicon rather than leading-edge fabrication.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/indian-chip-startup-bharat-semi-lands-24m-seed-round-as-it-eyes-global-ai-sensor-market/

**7. "Your Agent Will Pick the Hackable One": hackability is becoming a buying criterion**
Summary: Geoff Stearns traces how owners of the $70 M-VAVE FM-1 synthesizer wrote their own firmware within weeks of launch — a virtual analog synth, a groovebox, a Doom port — until the vendor published its firmware under GPLv3 in October with a dev kit and browser emulator. His argument is that AI collapsed the cost of reverse-engineering undocumented chips and writing replacement software, so any device with writable flash is now a platform whether or not the maker planned it, and agents doing purchasing research will surface that. The post drew 70 points and 42 comments from readers connecting it to the right-to-repair fight.
Source: https://geoffstearns.com/blog/your-agent-will-pick-the-hackable-one/

**8. Pony.ai's CFO: China's self-driving industry has been rehearsing a chip "Plan B" for years**
Summary: In an interview at Web Summit, Pony.ai CFO Leo Wang told TechCrunch that demand for compute far exceeds supply, that engineers are pushed toward midtier hardware and efficiency work, and that the company is increasingly buying domestically produced Chinese chips. Asked what happens if export controls tighten further, he said Chinese companies have been preparing for years — "there are alternatives, although cost-effectiveness-wise, probably it's not the best choice yet." With Nvidia's automotive processors and GPUs embedded across the AV stack, the industry's hedge is now a live procurement strategy rather than a contingency slide.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/techcrunch-mobility-the-nvidia-backup-plan-for-chinas-self-driving-industry/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. A hotel pool's chlorine alarm went unread during the Microsoft 365 outage — 15 guests were hurt**
Summary: A faulty automatic dosing system pushed chlorine in the pool at Phantasialand's Hotel Ling Bao in Brühl far too high, and the sauna area's maintenance safety system had already emailed an alert that nobody saw because the property received no mail during Friday's Microsoft 365 and Outlook outage, the operator told WDR. Fifteen guests reported respiratory irritation after using the pool, four were taken to hospital and one stayed for observation, and firefighters found the dosing defect during tests at the hotel. The story drew 332 points and 160 comments on Hacker News, where the prevailing read was that routing a life-safety alert through a single cloud email channel is the real design failure.
Source: https://www1.wdr.de/nrw/rheinland/rhein-erft-kreis/technischer-defekt-chlor-phantasialand-100.html

**2. Airlines' answer to agentic booking: own more of the trip, not just the flight**
Summary: At Skift's Aviation Forum, carrier executives framed the strategic choice as how to combine airline-owned interfaces, external AI platforms and specialist partners to serve more of the journey while keeping a direct relationship with the traveler, rather than surrendering discovery to assistants. It is the mirror image of the supply-side fight already running — United and MCR blocking agents, Citi arguing AI companies should carry liability for misbooked trips — where the value is migrating toward whoever controls the interface and the settlement layer. The session takeaways confirm carriers see agents as a distribution channel to be shaped, not simply blocked.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/13/how-airlines-can-own-the-whole-trip-in-the-ai-era/

## AI NEWS

**1. Thinking Machines (Mira Murati's Startup) Previews Real-Time "Interaction Models" for AI Voice and Video**
Thinking Machines, the AI startup founded by former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati, has released a research preview of "interaction models" — a new class of native multimodal AI systems that process voice and video in full-duplex mode, enabling AI to listen, speak, and react simultaneously rather than waiting for users to finish speaking. The flagship TML-Interaction-Small model achieves a turn-taking latency of 0.40 seconds, roughly half that of GPT-realtime-2.0 (1.18s) and significantly better than Gemini-3.1-flash-live (0.57s), by using a dual-model architecture where a lightweight interaction model handles real-time dialog while a background model handles deep reasoning. The company, which has raised approximately $2 billion at a $12 billion valuation, says a limited research preview will open in the coming months with wider release later this year.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/thinking-machines-shows-off-preview-of-near-realtime-ai-voice-and-video-conversation-with-new-interaction-models

**2. Bain Estimates $100 Billion SaaS Market in Agentic AI Automation**
Bain & Company has published a report estimating a $100 billion addressable market in the US for SaaS companies leveraging agentic AI to automate coordination work that spans enterprise systems like ERP, CRM, and support platforms. The firm found that over 90% of this market remains untapped, with vendors currently capturing only $4–6 billion. Sales represents the largest single function at roughly $20 billion, while customer support and R&D/engineering have the highest automation potential at 40–60% of workflow tasks. Bain recommends SaaS companies begin by mapping subprocess-level automation opportunities and assessing their data quality before scaling agent deployments.
Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/bain-agentic-ai-saas-market/

**3. AI Agents Are Stuck in Pilots Because Enterprise IAM Was Never Built for Them**
A detailed VentureBeat analysis highlights a structural trust gap: 85% of enterprises are running AI agent pilots but only 5% have reached production, primarily because identity and access management frameworks built for human users cannot inventory, scope, or revoke agent identities at machine speed. Cisco's Michael Dickman argues that trust must be a prerequisite, not an afterthought, and lays out a framework centered on agentic IAM (registering each agent with defined permissions and a human owner), microsegmentation (network-enforced least-privileged access), and cross-domain visibility that captures actual system-to-system communications rather than inferred activity. The article includes a practical assessment matrix for evaluating agentic AI trust gaps before production deployment.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/security/cisco-dickman-agentic-ai-trust-identity-governance-microsegmentation

**5. AI Tool Poisoning Reveals Critical Gap Between Artifact Integrity and Behavioral Integrity in Agent Systems**
Security researcher Nik Kale has documented how AI agents selecting tools from shared registries face a fundamental vulnerability that existing software supply chain controls — code signing, SLSA provenance, SBOMs — cannot address: these controls verify that an artifact is what it claims to be, but not that it behaves as it claims. A tool can pass all signature checks yet change its server-side behavior after publication to exfiltrate data or deviate from its stated endpoints. Kale proposes a runtime verification proxy for the MCP (Model Context Protocol) that validates discovery binding (ensuring the invoked tool matches the evaluated tool), enforces endpoint allowlisting, and validates output schemas against declared specifications — adding less than 10ms per invocation.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/security/ai-tool-poisoning-exposes-a-major-flaw-in-enterprise-agent-security

**7. Intent-Based Chaos Testing: The Missing Discipline Before AI Agents Reach Production**
VentureBeat publishes an in-depth piece on "intent-based chaos testing," a testing methodology designed to catch AI agents behaving confidently yet incorrectly — what the MIT NANDA project calls "confident incorrectness." The approach defines behavioral dimensions (tool call deviation, data access scope, completion signal accuracy, escalation fidelity, decision latency) and computes a weighted "intent deviation score" to detect when an agent drifts outside its intended operational boundaries before it causes production outages. The piece argues that traditional chaos engineering principles from distributed systems must be adapted for agentic AI, where failures compound through multi-agent pipelines in ways that bounded component testing cannot detect.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/infrastructure/intent-based-chaos-testing-is-designed-for-when-ai-behaves-confidently-and-wrongly

**8. Simon Willison: AI Coding Agents Only Work if They Reduce Maintenance Costs**
Simon Willison quotes James Shore's pointed analysis of the AI coding agent productivity equation: if you double your code output with AI but your maintenance costs stay constant, you've doubled your total maintenance burden; to actually come out ahead, you must reduce maintenance costs by the inverse of your productivity gain. Shore argues the math only works if LLMs decrease maintenance costs proportionally — a challenge because AI-generated code tends to be verbose and can introduce subtle bugs that are expensive to track down later. Willison flags this as essential reading for any team deploying AI coding tools in production.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/11/james-shore/

**9. Simon Willison: Shopify's River Coding Agent Operates Entirely in Public Slack**
Tobias Lütke, Shopify's CEO, described the company's internal coding agent River as operating entirely in public Slack channels — it politely declines direct messages and redirects users to post in public threads where the entire organization can see its work. This approach makes the agent's decisions, reasoning, and errors transparently auditable rather than hidden in private conversations, raising interesting questions about how corporate AI deployment should balance transparency against noise. Willison highlights this as a notable departure from typical internal AI tool deployments where agents work behind the scenes.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/11/learning-on-the-shop-floor/

**10. Simon Willison: Mozilla Used Claude Mythos Preview to Find and Fix Hundreds of Firefox Vulnerabilities**

The piece illustrates a growing pattern of AI being used defensively in security-critical infrastructure rather than offensively by attackers.

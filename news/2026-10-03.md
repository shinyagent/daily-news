## AI NEWS

**1. Sean Parker is rebuilding Stability AI around music**
Summary: Sean Parker, the Napster co-founder who two years ago joined an $80 million rescue of Stability AI, is repositioning the image-generator startup as a toolmaker for music professionals alongside CEO Prem Akkaraju. In late August Stability announced $76 million in funding from Sony, Warner and Universal, which licensed their catalogs for training, and has since released three new audio models plus AI music-editing software. Parker says he is playing by the labels' rules this time — a pointed reversal for the man who built Napster on ignoring music-industry permission.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/sean-parker-is-rebuilding-stability-ai-around-music/

**2. Apple tightens macOS 'Full Disk Access' controls over AI-agent risk**
Summary: Apple said it will add new controls around macOS's Full Disk Access permission, warning that increasingly capable AI agents "substantially" increase the risks of granting apps broad access to users' files, messages, mail and browsing history. The change lands days after Inc. columnist Jason Aten said Meta's Muse knew the contents of his private messages without permission — a claim Meta disputes — and follows Wired reporting on agent access risk; Ars Technica notes Meta argues Full Disk Access isn't sufficient for Muse while Apple disagrees. It marks the moment desktop AI agents became a platform-security problem rather than just a product one.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/apple-says-its-tightening-macos-full-disk-access-controls-due-to-new-risks-from-ai-agents/ | https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/apple-changes-full-disk-access-permissions-to-curb-abuse-from-ai-agents/

**3. Meta open-sources code to build your own Muse gadgets**
Summary: Meta released open-source code that lets anyone wire its Muse AI agent into their own hardware — an off-the-shelf ESP32 board, a Raspberry Pi, an HDMI stick for a big screen, or a color E Ink display showing reminders — under a "Muse gadgets" program. The move pushes the agent off the phone and into cheap DIY devices, echoing the ambient-hardware pushes at OpenAI and elsewhere, and drew 97 points on Hacker News. It is also a developer-acquisition play: Meta supplies the agent, users supply the hardware.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1004330/meta-muse-ai-gadgets-home-link | https://gadgets.muse.ai

**4. A flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app could have handed hackers your chat logs**
Summary: Researchers at the Objective-See Foundation found a vulnerability in the macOS version of ChatGPT that could have been used to take over the app on a victim's machine, exposing chat logs, stored data and connected browser sessions; OpenAI patched it and disclosed the fix in its September 25 changelog. Veteran macOS researcher Patrick Wardle framed the stakes with an agent metaphor — agents "are like the building manager who has access to the keys to all the rooms," so compromising one is uniquely damaging. The case inverts this summer's narrative: AI software itself, not just the agents built on it, is now an inviting target.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/a-flaw-in-chatgpts-mac-app-could-have-let-hackers-grab-sensitive-data/

**5. Nurses say Palantir's AI scheduling tool is a patient-safety problem**
Summary: WIRED spoke with nurses at HCA Healthcare, the largest US hospital chain, who say Timpani — the AI scheduling tool HCA co-developed with Palantir and has deployed at roughly 130 of its 190 locations — routinely ignores shift preferences, stacks back-to-back-to-back days and leaves care teams understaffed and exhausted. One Florida critical-care nurse said the tool honored fewer than half of 50 requested shifts over four months, worse than the human managers it replaced, and five nurses at other sites described similar failures. The accounts are a concrete instance of AI running a high-stakes operational job with what the nurses call minimal oversight.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/ai-making-mess-of-nurses-schedules-they-say-its-a-safety-issue/

**6. Amazon's data-center charm offensive backfires**
Summary: AWS CEO Matt Garman published a 3,000-word blog urging communities to support AI data centers or risk "irreparable harm" to the US economy and national security, dismissing public concerns about jobs, power demand and pollution as partly "misinformation and outright lies." Amazon also carries a $1 billion community-investment plan around the buildout, which Ars Technica notes won praise for ending NDAs but drew fresh criticism for downplaying data-center pollution. Hyperscalers are moving from quiet construction to political campaigning as local opposition hardens — the same backlash behind New Jersey's $1.1 million fine over unpermitted data-center gas generators last week.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1003929/amazon-ai-data-center-blog-warning | https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/amazons-1b-plan-to-combat-data-center-backlash-draws-more-backlash/

**7. US arrests California tech CEO accused of smuggling $300M of Nvidia chips to China**
Summary: Federal prosecutors arrested Greg Lui, owner of California-based Earthmade Computer, on charges of smuggling servers containing more than $300 million of export-restricted Nvidia AI chips to China, routing them through Malaysia and Singapore with fraudulent paperwork that misstated their final destination. The DOJ alleges the scheme ran from 2023 into 2024; Lui faces conspiracy to violate the Export Control Reform Act, outbound smuggling and money laundering conspiracy, carrying up to 20 years in prison. It is the latest in a string of prosecutions over Nvidia export controls and shows how persistently restricted chips leak despite US policy.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/us-arrests-tech-ceo-accused-of-smuggling-300m-in-nvidia-chips-into-china/

**8. Trillium Labs wants to do high-risk AI research in the open**
Summary: Nathan Lambert and Tom Zick launched Trillium Labs, a nonprofit that will study potentially dangerous areas — including recursive self-improvement and agents — while publishing experiment details so outside scientists can replicate and scrutinize the work, instead of keeping it locked inside frontier labs. Lambert argues secrecy reduces the community's ability to contribute new approaches and mitigate risk, calling the closed trajectory "a step backwards" from the scientific method. It is a direct counterpoint to the safety-through-confinement model the big labs pursue, and part of a broader push for reproducible frontier research.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/trillium-labs-wants-to-do-high-risk-ai-research-in-the-open/

**9. Slovenia's .si domain registrations surge on Trump's 'super intelligence' order**
Summary: After Trump's executive order directing federal agencies to call AI "Super Intelligence," Slovenia's registry reported a 2,199% jump in .si domain purchases in September as buyers raced for the new acronym — echoing the .ai gold rush that enriched Anguilla. Registry SI's spokesperson was cautious about drawing a direct causal link, but the timing is hard to miss. It is a small, tangible market consequence of the White House rebranding push covered here when the order was signed on September 30.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/02/slovenias-si-domain-sees-a-surge-in-registrations-after-trumps-super-intelligence-order/

**10. Suno moves into speech generation**
Summary: AI music startup Suno launched Speech in public beta across its web and mobile apps, generating spoken voiceovers from scripts or prompted descriptions and optionally scoring them with AI background music in the same pass. Chief product officer Jack Brody said music "will always be at the heart" of Suno but that the company's vision extends to other forms of expression. It puts Suno into territory — ads, audiobooks, synthetic narration — where ElevenLabs is entrenched, even as it fights Sony's and Universal's copyright suits.

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Hilton says it welcomes AI agents — but Muse couldn't pull a room rate**
Summary: BTIG analyst Jake Fuller told clients his research team's tests of Meta's Muse agent across a wide array of hotel searches found that "Hilton blocked Muse," while Skift's own Muse searches hit similar speed bumps on Hilton, Marriott and Hyatt sites. Hilton denies blocking any agentic assistant, saying it is "accepting bookings from various agents, including Muse" and wants to be visible wherever agentic commerce happens. The friction shows the gap between hotel groups' agent-friendly rhetoric and the reality that OTAs — already plugged into booking APIs — remain the easier door, a distribution risk for hoteliers chasing direct bookings.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/02/hilton-isnt-blocking-new-ai-agents-but-its-not-always-letting-them-in/

**2. Hopper's co-founder exits the division that drove 90% of revenue**
Summary: Dakota Smith, a Hopper co-founder who spent three years leading Hopper Technology Solutions, announced on LinkedIn that he has left the Montreal-headquartered company. HTS is Hopper's B2B arm, powering bookings for partners including Uber, Expedia, RBC and, as of this week, Aven Hospitality, and Smith said in 2025 it generated 90% of Hopper's revenue. The exit creates a leadership gap at the unit that now matters far more to Hopper than its consumer app, with one source pointing to Smith's credibility in B2B.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/02/hoppers-co-founder-and-b2b-boss-left-the-company/

**3. The hard part of travel AI isn't the demo — it's owning the outcome**
Summary: A Skift Take argues that a pilot only proves AI can perform a task, while putting it into a travel business requires someone to own the results, handle the exceptions and show it is worth the cost as companies assemble 2027 budgets. The real test is which AI projects firms keep paying for — tools employees depend on or ones that measurably improve the customer experience — rather than which produce impressive demonstrations, since live services must cope with incomplete data, legacy systems and requests that don't fit the pattern. It frames the agenda for Skift's Data + AI Summit Europe in London on October 6.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/02/what-does-it-take-to-get-travel-ai-out-of-the-pilot-stage/

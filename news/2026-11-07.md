## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic IPO buyers are talking valuations as low as $1.1 trillion after the AI selloff**
Summary: Bloomberg reported that prospective Anthropic IPO investors are discussing a $1.2 trillion to $1.3 trillion valuation ahead of formal marketing next week, with at least one weighing a figure as low as $1.1 trillion, after this week's post-election rout in AI stocks. Investors had floated up to $2 trillion earlier this year and now see the $1.5 trillion figure as the high end, and Reuters reported the offering — which some expect to reach $100 billion — could be delayed if AI assets stay under pressure. The first hard test of whether public markets will absorb an AI listing at private-market prices now lands days before the roadshow.
Source: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-06/anthropic-ipo-valuation-expectations-pressured-after-ai-rout

**2. Ramp data shows corporate AI spending flatlining even as usage climbs**
Summary: Ramp's transaction data shows token spend grew just 1.5% across August and September, after jumping 42% month-over-month in July, while token volumes kept rising. The divergence points to price cuts and tighter cost controls among the heaviest buyers, which would cap revenue growth for OpenAI and Anthropic right as Anthropic prepares to go public. It is the clearest evidence yet that AI adoption and AI revenue are decoupling.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/ramp-data-shows-ai-spending-stalling-just-as-anthropic-nears-its-ipo/

**3. China reportedly recruited an AI executive to spy on Jensen Huang**
Summary: The New York Times reported that China's Ministry of State Security enlisted Megaspeed executive Alice Huang — who is not related to Jensen Huang — to gather intelligence on American AI chips and potentially push Nvidia toward making its most advanced chips available to China. Trump was briefed on the relationship last year and took no action, and on Thursday three House committees asked Jensen Huang to appear for a closed-door joint briefing. A reported espionage channel running through a customer to the CEO of the West's leading AI chipmaker turns export-control enforcement into a personal-security problem.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1029338/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-china-spy

**4. Open Chinese models now roughly match frontier labs at hacking, undercutting gated releases**
Summary: Vals tested DeepSeek, GLM and other models against simulated corporate networks and found the best open models approaching the cyber capability of frontier systems that labs keep behind trusted-access programs. That undercuts the premise of restricted releases like Anthropic's Project Glasswing — that gating buys defenders time before offensive capability spreads. If anyone can download comparable hacking skill, access policy stops being the main lever on cyber risk.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/free-chinese-ai-models-are-getting-scary-good-at-hacking/

**5. Meta indefinitely pauses the commercial terms that would have licensed page owners' linked sites for AI**
Summary: Meta shelved its commercial terms update, which would have required Business account holders to let it crawl and use content from sites linked on their Facebook pages, after injunctions in Brazil and Norway, a UK competition investigation, and bipartisan Senate pressure. Meta had already removed the word "train" and pushed the effective date from October 30 to November 20; the terms now will not take effect at all. The retreat is narrow — existing terms still stand and the fight over scraping for AI development is unresolved.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/does-meta-think-we-cant-read-meta-abandons-sketchy-commercial-terms-update/

**6. More than 800 deployments of an open-source AI hacking agent are exposed to the open internet**
Summary: Censys found over 800 distinct Artex deployments reachable from the web without a password, running an open-source autonomous penetration-testing tool released in July that has already been linked to real cyberattacks. Artex is popular with operators who cannot get access to Anthropic's or OpenAI's gated models, and anyone can run it on their own server. Capable offensive agents left unauthenticated on the public internet are a new exposure class created by cheap, downloadable AI tooling.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/hundreds-of-artex-ai-hacking-tools-found-publicly-exposed-to-the-web/

**7. AI labs are buying companies' internal files, emails and meeting transcripts**
Summary: WIRED documented a fast-growing data-acquisition market in which firms including Mercor, Handshake and Micro1 buy internal corporate files, emails and call transcripts to resell to AI labs, with pitches reaching $2 million for a single corpus and one estimate putting private-data spending above $45 billion. Mercor CEO Brendan Foody argues de-identification solves the privacy problem, and startups that wind down companies now broker their scrubbed data as a service. Selling operational memory may end up mattering to workers as much as the data center buildout does.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/ai-companies-really-really-want-to-buy-your-work-files-and-emails/

**8. Palo Alto Networks in talks to buy agent-security startup Clutch for $500 million**
Summary: Palo Alto Networks is negotiating to acquire Clutch Security, which controls AI agents' access to enterprise systems, for $500 million in cash plus milestone payments — about 50 times its roughly $10 million ARR and far above the $85 million valuation it fetched in January 2025. It would be Palo Alto's seventh acquisition this year, days after CrowdStrike agreed to pay about $495 million for two AI security startups. Identity and access for non-human agents is becoming the most expensive corner of security M&A.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/palo-alto-networks-in-talks-to-buy-ai-agent-security-startup-clutch-for-500m/

**9. Amazon lets customers text Alexa to shop, after blocking Meta's agent from its catalog**
Summary: Amazon began letting select US customers text its Alexa shopping agent to search, add items to a cart and buy, timed to the start of the holiday season. The launch follows Amazon's September move to block Meta's Muse from querying its site, and it adopts the text-message interface Muse and Instinct already use. Retailers that spent years fighting screen-scraping now have to decide whether conversational agents get programmatic access to their catalogs.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/after-blocking-muse-amazon-now-lets-users-text-alexa-to-help-them-shop/

**10. Meta's Muse can now build app-like "spaces" you describe in a sentence**
Summary: Meta introduced spaces in its Muse app: app-like experiences the user describes in natural language — a shopping hub, school dashboard or music tracker — which Muse generates and keeps current by pulling from connected services such as Gmail, Google Calendar, OpenTable and Notion. Users can pin spaces in the sidebar, move them to the main tab, or schedule when each appears. It moves the assistant from a chat window toward a container for generated mini-apps, the same ground OpenAI's apps platform is staking out.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1028871/meta-muse-ai-spaces

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. MakeMyTrip buys a majority stake in ride-pooling platform Quick Ride to own the daily commute**
Summary: MakeMyTrip acquired a majority stake in Bengaluru-based Quick Ride, adding shared commuting to a ground-transport stack that already includes redBus and Savaari; it did not disclose the price or exact ownership. Quick Ride matches commuters on overlapping routes and sells into corporates and IT parks, giving the OTA a product used on ordinary weekdays rather than only around holidays. The wager is that daily-commute frequency converts into travel bookings as MakeMyTrip's air business softens.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/06/makemytrip-buys-into-daily-commutes-with-quick-ride-stake/

**2. Airbnb posts its fastest revenue growth in three years while Booking shrinks**
Summary: Airbnb grew third-quarter revenue 19% to $4.9 billion, its fastest rate in three years, against 12% at Expedia and a 1% decline at Booking Holdings that reflected a $1 billion accounting hit tied to a possible FTC settlement. The acceleration follows a 9.7% quarter a year ago, when CEO Brian Chesky told Skift Global Forum he was unhappy with growth. Platform economics are now diverging sharply across the big three just as each reworks its AI booking strategy.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/05/airbnb-outpaces-rivals-with-its-fastest-revenue-growth-in-3-years/

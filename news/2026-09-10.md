## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic researcher quits warning labs are 'gambling with our lives' as senior safety lead puts >10% odds AI 'kills all humans'**
Summary: Jacob Coxon, a researcher who trained AI systems at Anthropic and previously at OpenAI, quit Wednesday accusing the two labs of "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives" over what he says is a lax approach to safety. Hours earlier and after his resignation post, Anthropic alignment lead Evan Hubinger predicted on X a greater than 10 percent chance AI "could kill all people" in the next decade — a post reposted by current and former OpenAI and Anthropic researchers who called it a common industry sentiment. Coxon tells Wired the threats could arrive via AI-enabled biological or cyberweapons, and that labs have "a year or two" of "crunch time" to coordinate limits on recursive self-improvement before it is too late.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/991927/anthropic-ai-kill-all-humans | https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-researcher-quits-jacob-coxon-ai-fears-humanity/

**2. US agencies accuse six Chinese AI firms of industrial-scale 'distillation' of US frontier models**
Summary: The NSA, CISA, and FBI jointly accused DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI of running industrial-scale attacks to distill capabilities out of US frontier models — including variants of Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok — since at least late 2024, "likely" with Chinese government awareness. The agencies say the campaigns used bulk-bought fake accounts to hammer model inference APIs with coordinated queries and prompt-injection techniques that force models to reveal hidden chain-of-thought reasoning. They are urging US AI firms to work with the government and allies to detect such campaigns and reportedly to identify and secretly switch Chinese users to less-capable models, framing the theft as a threat to America's lead in the AI race.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/09/six-chinese-ai-firms-accused-of-aggressively-copying-us-frontier-models/

**6. Suno releases v6, its first AI music model trained with record-industry-licensed data**
Summary: Suno's new v6 model was "trained from the ground up" on data that excludes its previous training corpus, instead using content licensed from Warner Music Group, BMG, and Believe plus user data — the company's first model built with record-industry support after roughly a year of copyright litigation. V6 ships as three models: v6, the unpredictable v6-wild, and v6-mini, a free fast tier for all users, with sharply better genre understanding in early testing. The release is a template for how AI music companies may legitimize training data after the industry's lawsuits.

**7. Kepler Computing exits stealth with a plan to ease the AI memory shortage without EUV**
Summary: Kepler Computing, a San Jose startup founded in 2018 by physicists and computer scientists, emerged from stealth claiming a new high-bandwidth-memory architecture built on 3D stacking and a proprietary ferroelectric material that boosts density without expensive EUV lithography and works in existing fabs. The company has raised $468 million from GlobalFoundries, Intel Capital, AMD Ventures, Baillie Gifford, and Bill Gates' Gates Frontier, and in July won a US Commerce Department commitment of up to $245 million to develop the AI memory technology domestically. It is now producing test chips with GlobalFoundries as memory prices surge on AI-driven supply bottlenecks.

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Navan buys BoomPop to push into the meetings-and-events market**
Summary: Corporate travel platform Navan said Wednesday it has acquired BoomPop, an AI-powered platform for planning corporate meetings and events, bringing that spend into the same system where companies already book travel and manage expenses. The deal follows a partnership earlier this year and repeats Navan's consolidation playbook — the company ran the same pattern with Smartrips in Brazil — as it grows roughly 35% while public. Meetings and events is a large corporate-spend category that managed-travel tools have historically failed to capture, making it a natural expansion target for Navan's all-in-one platform pitch.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/09/navan-buys-boompop-to-push-into-meetings-and-events/

**2. Marriott CEO says AI will hit hotel distribution first**
Summary: Marriott CEO Anthony Capuano said at a Bank of America investor conference that the "biggest impact" of AI on the hotel industry will be in distribution — how travelers discover and book hotels online — and that Marriott is feeding Google and OpenAI as much content about its portfolio as possible so its hotels "show up in a really both authentic but prominent way" in AI-powered platforms. He cited Skift and Lighthouse reporting that Marriott properties already surface more readily in those tools than its peers'. The comments signal that hotels now see AI assistants as a distribution channel to be optimized like any other, not just a back-office tool.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/09/marriott-ceo-says-ai-will-impact-hotel-distribution-first/

**3. Why the personal AI assistant matters more for travel than any AI travel product**
Summary: Skift's editorial column argues the biggest AI shift in travel is happening outside travel itself: general-purpose agents from ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini — and startups like Instinct and Town that connect directly into email, calendars, and messages — are turning trip booking and management into just one of the many tasks a personal assistant performs. The author updates his "Claude Effect" thesis, admitting he expected an AI company to build travel capability that directly competes with Expedia or Booking; instead agents are absorbing travel as a routine capability. The implication: every company earning a margin for handling booking complexity will have to justify that margin once an agent can handle the whole trip.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/09/why-the-personal-ai-assistant-hype-matters-more-for-travel-than-any-ai-travel-product/

**4. Klook bets creators can sell the whole trip, not just the viral attraction**
Summary: Klook has upgraded its Kreator Shops program to more than 30,000 creators across 88 markets, giving influencers a single storefront where followers can browse and book a full recommendation set — hotels, attractions, restaurants, transport, and eSIMs — rather than following separate links to individual experiences. "A trip is an entire itinerary, not a single product," said Klook VP of global marketing Marcus Yong, framing the storefronts as bookable itineraries built on creators' tried-and-tested trips. The push turns creator content into a direct-commerce channel for experiences platforms competing for discovery against OTAs and social apps.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/08/klook-bets-creators-can-sell-the-whole-trip-not-just-the-viral-attraction/

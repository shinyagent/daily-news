## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, cutting Fable's price as METR's RSI findings hang over the launch**
Summary: Anthropic released Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on Monday, claiming gains in coding, reasoning and vision, responses up to 30% faster, and a cut in Fable's input price from $10 to $8 per million tokens ($40 output), with an optional fast mode at double the price. Prerelease testers used the models to complete otherwise-infeasible software migrations, design three working electronic devices, and formalize Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours; Fable carries cyber and biology safeguards while Mythos is restricted to vetted organizations. It is Anthropic's first release since METR reported its internal models are roughly doubling the pace of its research, and Anthropic concedes the new models suspect they are being evaluated more often than recent ones — which it says challenges its ability to predict real-world behavior.
Source: https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2

**2. Meta ships Muse Ember, a 10-cent model built to do an agent's grunt work**
Summary: Meta Superintelligence Lab released Muse Ember, its smallest and cheapest model, at $0.10 per million input and $0.50 per million output tokens — priced against Anthropic's Haiku and OpenAI's Luna and competitive with open models like DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and GLM 5.3 Flash. Meta positions it as the volume half of a two-model harness ("Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume") covering tool calls, routing, extraction and long document parsing, and trained natively on text, images and audio. The release drew 221 points on Hacker News and confirms the agent stack splitting into an expensive planner and a cheap executor.
Source: https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember

**3. Google now routes connected Drive files and Gmail into human review, reversing a 2023 promise**
Summary: A GitHub writeup that drew 294 points on Hacker News documents that Google's help page and an October 16 edit to its 2023 Workspace-extensions post now state that content from connected Drive and Gmail can be seen by human reviewers, warning users not to connect apps holding information they would not want a reviewer to see. The author's testing found ambient mentions in a chat — trips, bills, reservations — trigger background Workspace lookups without @Drive or @Gmail tags, with retrieval ranging from metadata summaries to full unredacted file ingestion. It converts a privacy assurance enterprises relied on into a documented data-handling risk at the moment Google is pushing Gemini deeper into Workspace.
Source: https://github.com/thisisme6598/gemini_files

**4. Google adds two more TPUs to its eighth generation: 8i Max for context, 8tx for confidential inference**
Summary: Google expanded its eighth-generation TPU lineup with TPU 8i Max, aimed at massive context windows and the highest compute density per rack, and TPU 8tx for secure, confidential inference — the first time it has introduced four TPU systems in a single year, after 8t for training and 8i for reasoning. The company says TPUs now serve Gemini training and serving, agentic workloads, Cloud customers, and a growing business of deploying TPUs directly into customers' own data centers, with nearly 100 such customers. Custom silicon is Google's answer to both Nvidia's pricing and enterprise demands that inference happen inside their own walls.
Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/

**5. Anthropic buys $3.4 billion of natural gas turbines for 1.7 gigawatts**

**6. NHTSA opens a recall query into Waymo after robotaxis kept passing stopped school buses**
Summary: Regulators opened a recall query to assess the adequacy of Waymo's remedy after finding that a majority of reported illegal school-bus passings happened after the software update Waymo deployed to all affected vehicles on November 17, 2025. The original investigation began in October 2025 after Atlanta footage showed a robotaxi maneuvering around a stopped bus with its stop sign extended and lights flashing. A fix that did not fix the behavior is what turns a defect probe into a recall, and it puts the safety case for driverless fleets back in front of federal regulators.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/waymo-robotaxis-kept-illegally-passing-school-buses-after-software-fix-feds-say/

**7. Apple starts charging for AI: $49.99 "Intelligence Usage Packs" in Logic Pro**

**8. Disney and Adobe team up to build "artist-led" generative models on Disney's own IP**

**9. DeepX tapes out an edge AI chip that runs large models under 5 watts**
Summary: South Korean startup DeepX taped out the DX-M2, its second-generation edge accelerator, using custom LPDDR5X processor-in-memory from Samsung to perform matrix-vector work inside the memory chip — which it projects yields 6.7x more tokens per second on Llama 3.1-8B than the accelerator alone. Memory bandwidth tripled from 50 to 150 GB/s, and the company targets large language models on-device in under 5 W for robotics and industrial equipment. Edge inference at that power envelope would let machines run capable models without a cloud round-trip, the same bet as the recent wave of tiny local models but in silicon.
Source: https://www.eetimes.com/deepx-tapes-out-second-gen-chip-projects-6-7x-performance-gain-with-lpddr5x-pim/

**10. Simon Willison's fix for AI-written PR descriptions: collapse them behind `<details>`**
Summary: Willison notes that most changes to his projects now arrive as PRs opened by Claude Code or Codex, and that their descriptions are accurate, comprehensive and unreadable — "it reads like slop" even when it isn't, because nobody wants to read a document longer than the change it describes. His fix is editorial rather than technical: a human-written paragraph at the top explaining the hook, with the agent's full writeup folded into an HTML `<details><summary>` block reviewers can expand if they want it. It is a small convention that keeps agent output available without letting it set the reading cost of every review.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/details-summary/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Skift audit: only 10 of 50 major travel sites tell AI crawlers what they may read**
Summary: Skift reviewed the robots.txt files of 50 major travel websites and found only 10 explicitly address AI crawlers, with the rest lumping them in with every other bot or leaving default settings — and where directives exist, they do not always match which bots actually get in. The gap matters as AI-mediated shopping grows: Adobe reported AI visits to travel sites up 119% year over year in July, while its analyst flagged that significant portions of U.S. travel sites remain unreadable to machines. Brands spending heavily on "AI visibility" are, in many cases, still telling the crawlers nothing at all.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/09/dear-robot-how-travel-is-and-isnt-talking-to-ai/

**2. Expedia spent $676 million on three acquisitions in nine months — and B2B is where it's going**
Summary: Expedia Group's third-quarter filing shows $676 million spent on three acquisitions in the first nine months of 2026 — its busiest stretch since the 2015 HomeAway/Orbitz/Travelocity spree — divided between two travel companies in its B2B segment and one AI trip planner in B2C, which map to Tiqets, CarTrawler and Layla. CEO Ariane Gorin has pledged to keep investing, and the mix shows where: attractions and ground-transport inventory to supply partners, plus a consumer AI planner. After years of pruning, Expedia is buying supply and distribution rather than demand.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/09/expedias-676-million-in-deals-points-to-more-b2b-buying/

**3. Expedia's integration with Meta's Muse is live — hotel search and booking, but no changes**
Summary: Meta's Muse can now search and book Expedia hotels in natural language, roughly six weeks after the two companies announced the tie-up; Expedia confirmed the launch is live in the U.S. and that it remains merchant of record, so cancellations and rebooking still route through its own customer support. Skift's test found the agent handles destination, dates and preferences conversationally but stops short of servicing the booking. That makes Expedia the first major OTA transacting inside a general-purpose assistant — the distribution shift the GDSs have spent the quarter arguing about.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/09/expedia-goes-live-on-muse-but-its-ai-wins-are-inside-its-own-apps/

**4. Wyndham left a hardcoded credential in its Android app, exposing customer data**
Summary: A researcher found a password embedded in the source of Wyndham's Android app that unlocked an internal Salesforce database holding customer names, emails, Wyndham member numbers, login JWTs and company documents — millions of entries, against roughly 129 million enrolled rewards members. Wyndham has no way to report security flaws; the credential was revoked only after TechCrunch contacted its security staff, and the company would not say whether it can detect past unauthorized access. Loyalty accounts and persistent login tokens are exactly what attackers want from a hotel chain, and the absence of a disclosure channel is its own finding.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/hotel-giant-wyndham-exposed-customer-data-after-app-security-lapse/

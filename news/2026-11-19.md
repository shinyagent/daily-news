## AI NEWS

**1. Anthropic prices IPO at $83 a share, valuing it at $1.5 trillion**
Summary: Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share, above the $70–$78 range it had been marketing, and will start trading Thursday on Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT" at a fully diluted valuation of roughly $1.5 trillion — up from $965 billion in its May private round but short of the $2 trillion it had reportedly targeted. The book held up despite the market jitters that followed Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia, and a private placement alongside the IPO will see AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners buy $26 billion of non-voting shares. Amazon (~15%) and Google (~14%) remain the largest strategic holders, and the debut now sets the valuation benchmark for OpenAI and the rest of the private AI field.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-reportedly-prices-ipo-at-83-a-share-above-projected-range/

**2. Google releases Gemini 4 Neon at $3/$15 per million tokens**
Summary: Google shipped Gemini 4 Neon, a frontier-class model priced at $3 per million input tokens — $0.15 for cached input — and $15 per million output, with a 1 million-token context window. Google claims third overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (61) and second on the Vals Index (70.9%), plus first place on Legal Research Bench and Tax Agent Bench, and it is rolling out through the Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity and the Gemini app. At that price it lands squarely in the tier OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol and Microsoft's new coding models are fighting over, and it drew 329 points and 314 comments on Hacker News within hours.
Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/

**3. Nvidia won't file its 10-Q on time, and blames the Huang investigation**
Summary: Interim CEO Colette Kress told analysts Nvidia would file its quarterly 10-Q "in the coming weeks" rather than on earnings day, without offering a reason — an omission notable enough that UBS's Timothy Arcuri followed up and was told the delay relates to the internal investigation into Huang and whether new risk factors need disclosing. The 10-Q carries the legal-proceedings and risk-factor sections a press release doesn't, and Nvidia is simultaneously facing congressional probes, a DOJ look at Megaspeed and a reported SEC investigation into whether its disclosures misled investors. The stock is around $150 against Arcuri's newly cut $220 target.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-created-a-weird-bit-of-intrigue-by-not-filing-a-routine-sec-report/

**4. Microsoft makes Office a plugin inside Copilot, which it calls "the new OS for work"**
Summary: At Ignite, Microsoft made Word, Excel and PowerPoint generally available inside Copilot and re-cast the assistant as "the new OS for work," with agents driving the work and the applications riding shotgun on top of a Work IQ context layer built from email, meetings, chats and files. It also took Copilot Autopilot — a persistent, proactive agent users delegate whole responsibilities to, billed through usage-based Copilot Credits — to general availability, alongside governance controls that give each agent a unique identity and scope. Nadella's framing was unusually candid for a launch keynote: "by definition, you can't" trust these models, so intelligence supply has to be separated architecturally from authority; the same keynote announced seven new models and drew 405 points on Hacker News.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/11/microsoft-calls-copilot-the-new-os-for-work-at-ignite-keynote/

**5. Internal Meta chats show staff hit the same Muse privacy problems users did**
Summary: Internal Meta conversations reviewed by Reuters show employees raising the same alarms early Muse testers did: the agent appearing to read encrypted private messages, mixing up conversations between accounts, and emitting outputs nobody asked for. One staffer flagged a "possible cross-account context leak" after Muse messaged a seller about a coffee table that wasn't theirs, another was sent two strangers' bank balances unprompted ($2,722.58 at Capital One, $5,899.57 at Schwab), and a third said Muse rolled them into a $888-a-month dog insurance plan they couldn't cancel. Meta says the erratic outputs — including the balances — are not real, but the pattern matters because Muse is being pushed into messaging apps where the context it draws on is other people's conversations.
Source: https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/muse-privacy-invasions-are-freaking-out-meta-staffers-internal-chats-show/

**6. Citi, PNC and Citizens tell customers their AI agents are their own problem — even if hacked**
Summary: Citi updated its online user agreement this week to make customers responsible for anything a third-party AI agent does with their account, including losses incurred if the tool leaks credentials or is itself compromised; PNC changed its terms the same day and Citizens emailed customers with a similar warning. JPMorgan and Bank of America set comparable policies last month, completing a near-uniform industry stance across the largest US banks. The terms shift liability precisely as agents get handed bank credentials to pay bills and move money, and they sit awkwardly beside banks' own competitive interest in owning the customer's financial interface.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked/

**7. Nadella: much of America's critical infrastructure is "unpatchable"**
Summary: At the New York Times DealBook Summit, Satya Nadella warned that a large share of critical infrastructure runs on firmware that cannot be patched — chillers, HVAC systems, transportation gear — and that much of it is internet-connected, which is exactly the exposure AI-accelerated attack tooling exploits. He downplayed Microsoft's recent Outlook outage as a "latent bug" that combined into a "perfect storm," while arguing the industry's response has become self-defeating: AI now finds vulnerabilities faster than companies can test and deploy fixes. In the same interview he predicted AI agents will reshuffle the economics of the web by interrupting advertising-driven business models.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/amidst-fears-of-ai-cyberattacks-satya-nadella-says-lots-of-critical-infrastructure-is-unpatchable/

**8. OpenAI says its models cut chip area 13% and delivered a year of AMD design work in five months**
Summary: OpenAI detailed how its frontier models improved the compute blocks in Jalapeño, its first custom inference chip: changes that reduced standard-cell area by about 13% in the vector unit and 10% in the matrix unit, with one multiplier redesign cutting latency from two clock cycles to one. Working with AMD, the models rewrote SystemVerilog RTL to reach up to 14% PPA improvement over heavily optimized baselines on flagship GPU IP — roughly a year of engineering progress in under five months, some of which AMD has already sent to manufacturing. OpenAI is now partnering with Synopsys to wire frontier models into production EDA tools, the step that would make this a standard part of chip design rather than a showcase.
Source: https://openai.com/index/advancing-chip-design-with-our-models/

**9. KPMG ran 100+ AI agents through a real audit, and they out-questioned the humans**
Summary: KPMG UK's Project Exponential put more than 100 AI agents to work alongside a conventional audit team on the financial statements of an unnamed FTSE 250 company, with the agents gathering information, analyzing and documenting data, drafting conclusions and coordinating tasks among themselves. Head of audit Cath Burnet said the agents in several instances went deeper than the human team, identifying areas needing further investigation and raising questions the auditors had not. The trial drew 315 points on Hacker News because it targets the labour economics of professional services rather than another coding benchmark — the work being tested is judgment-heavy review, not generation.
Source: https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/ai-agents-better-questions-kpmg/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Booking Holdings drops Priceline from its B2B consolidation amid FTC scrutiny**
Summary: Booking Holdings had planned to fold three B2B brands into its new Booking Partner Division, but is leaving Priceline out because of a potential Federal Trade Commission complaint against the brand, Agoda CEO and division head Omri Morgenshtern told Skift. He described the investigation as creating "operational complexities" for a unit whose whole premise is selling one consolidated supply and demand graph to partners. The omission weakens the division at exactly the moment Expedia is arguing breadth of inventory is the differentiator in B2B distribution.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/18/booking-holdings-leaves-priceline-out-of-b2b-unit-amid-ftc-scrutiny/

**2. Airbnb courts independent hotels with a swipe at Booking: "Booking is not hotel-centric"**
Summary: Airbnb's global head of hotel enterprise and connectivity partnerships, Lou Zameryka, pitched independent hotels at the Phocuswright conference by arguing Airbnb is built around stays while Booking.com is a general marketplace — "you could switch it to pencils," he said. The pitch is that Airbnb can hand hotels net-new demand from travelers who would not otherwise find them, at a moment when the company is pushing beyond short-term rentals for growth. The open question Skift raises is whether adding hotel inventory pulls demand away from the private-room hosts that made Airbnb's economics work.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/18/airbnb-pitches-hotels-we-care-more-about-stays-than-booking-com-does/

**3. The AI agent is travel's ad blocker — and the upsell economy is the exposure**
Summary: MCR Hotels CEO Tyler Morse told Skift's Commerce Summit that an agent booking on a traveler's behalf doesn't buy on impulse, which threatens the seat upgrades, room upsells and checkout add-ons that generate a large share of travel companies' profit. A November 15 Citizens report tested two booking agents, Instinct and Meta's Muse, found the experience "underwhelming" but ranked the long-term risk to OTAs as significant — Expedia most exposed, then Booking Holdings, with Airbnb the most insulated because its supply is hardest to substitute. The analysis reframes the agent threat from disintermediation of the booking to erosion of the margin that funds it.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/18/the-ai-agent-is-travels-ad-blocker/

**4. Lola's Steve Hafner: browser-based agents are the worst way to search travel, and Muse can't make money**
Summary: Steve Hafner, the Kayak co-founder now running Booking Holdings' Lola, said browser-navigating agents like Meta's Muse and Instinct are "the most inaccurate" way to search for travel because they scrape websites rather than query supplier APIs, and that Muse's model costs make its economics unworkable — "the more they scale, the more money they will lose." Lola instead connects directly to suppliers including GetYourGuide, Viator and Hertz and to distributors Duffel and Nuitée, and Booking has said it could put up to $100 million each into Lola and Vayo to cover marketing and token costs, with Lola running on 14 employees. Hafner concedes it is hard to bet against Meta's resources — but the argument is that travel agents win on connectivity, not conversation.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/11/18/lola-ceo-steve-hafner-on-metas-muse-the-more-they-scale-the-more-money-they-will-lose/

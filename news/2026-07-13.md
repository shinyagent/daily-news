# Daily News — July 13, 2026

## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Work — An Autonomous AI Agent for Enterprise Tasks**
OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Work, a cloud-based AI agent embedded in its flagship chatbot that can autonomously execute complex, multi-step tasks across email, calendars, code repositories, and messaging apps like Slack. Powered by GPT-5.6, the move signals OpenAI's push to transform ChatGPT from a question-answering tool into an autonomous work platform. The launch raises questions about how enterprises will manage agent reliability and security at scale.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-introduces-chatgpt-work-a-cloud-based-ai-agent-that-manages-tasks-across-email-slack-and-calendars

**2. Apple Sues OpenAI Over Alleged Trade Secret Theft**
Apple has filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging that senior leadership directed the theft of trade secrets via a longtime former Apple employee. The suit marks a significant escalation in tension between the two tech giants, who were previously seen as potential AI partners. Details remain sparse but the allegations point to coordinated misconduct at the executive level.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/apple-sues-openai-over-alleged-trade-secret-theft/

**3. DeepSeek Slashes Prices 75% — But the 100x Problem Persists for AI Agents**
DeepSeek cut pricing on its V4-Pro model by 75%, which should be unequivocally good news for enterprise AI developers. However, many are discovering that cheaper models don't translate into healthier margins — because AI agent systems are consuming tokens faster than prices are declining. The article argues that software economics hasn't changed: infrastructure gets cheaper, but usage scales up to absorb the savings.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/deepseek-cut-prices-75-the-100x-problem-remains

**4. OpenAI Introduces GPT-5.6 Family: Luna, Terra, and Sol**
OpenAI's latest flagship model family went into general availability, offering three tiers — Luna ($1/$6 per 1M tokens), Terra ($2.50/$15), and Sol ($5/$30). All three feature a February 2026 knowledge cutoff and improved reasoning capabilities. Simon Willison notes that price-per-token comparisons are less meaningful now as reasoning token counts vary dramatically between models for the same task.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/9/gpt-5-6/#atom-everything

**5. Meta Pulls Controversial AI Feature on Instagram After User Backlash**
Meta removed an AI-powered feature on Instagram following widespread criticism, acknowledging it "missed the mark." The feature allowed the company to reference users' public content in AI training, and while Meta claimed it gave users control, the backlash was swift enough to force its removal. The incident highlights growing user sensitivity around AI data usage on social platforms.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/meta-removes-controversial-ai-feature-on-instagram-after-backlash/

**6. Enterprise AI Agents Are Gaining Autonomy Faster Than Companies Can Verify Them**
According to a VentureBeat survey of 157 enterprise respondents, half have deployed an AI agent or LLM feature that passed internal evaluations yet still caused a customer-facing failure — one in four more than once. A separate survey found 57% of enterprises traced confidently-wrong agent answers to missing business context. The industry is racing to build "agentic context layers" to close the verification gap.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/enterprise-ai-is-entering-an-evaluation-gap-agents-are-gaining-autonomy-faster-than-companies-can-verify-them

**7. Google Research Unveils TabFM — a Foundation Model for Tabular Data That Skips Per-Dataset Training**
Google's TabFM treats tabular prediction as an in-context learning problem rather than requiring per-dataset training, hyperparameter tuning, and feature engineering. Since most business data lives in tables across CRMs, data warehouses, and financial ledgers, this could significantly reduce the overhead of building reliable models from structured data.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-tabfm-skips-per-dataset-training-and-still-predicts-on-tables-its-never-seen

**8. 'Slopsquatting' Emerges as a New AI-Powered Software Supply Chain Threat**
VentureBeat highlights a new attack vector called "slopsquatting" — a combination of AI slop and typosquatting — where LLM hallucinations cause AI coding assistants to recommend malicious or nonexistent packages. As developers increasingly rely on AI coding tools, they unknowingly grant attackers access to their software supply chain from day one.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/security/forget-typosquatting-slopsquatting-is-the-software-supply-chain-threat-created-by-ai-coding-tools

**9. SK Hynix Raises $26.5B in Biggest Foreign IPO in US History on AI Chip Demand**
SK Hynix's record-breaking IPO underscores the relentless demand for AI chips, particularly high-bandwidth memory used in GPU clusters. Following the raise, US officials are urging both SK Hynix and Samsung to build additional fabrication plants in the United States to reduce reliance on Asian semiconductor supply chains.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/sk-hynix-raises-26-5b-in-the-biggest-foreign-ipo-in-us-history-is-urged-to-build-new-us-fabs/

**10. Anthropic Extends Claude Fable 5 Access Again as GPT-5.6 Sol Competes**
Anthropic has once again bumped the date that Claude Fable 5 stops being available in paid plans, now extending through July 19, after GPT-5.6 Sol was clearly positioned as a Fable/Mythos-class competitor. Simon Willison notes this is the second extension, suggesting Anthropic is still figuring out its pricing and tier strategy in an increasingly competitive model market.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/12/bump/#atom-everything

## AI NEWS

**1. Google releases Gemini 3.8 Flash — its third Flash model in six weeks — plus a Cyber variant**
Summary: Google launched Gemini 3.8 Flash, which the company says "works harder" than 3.7 Flash by taking more reasoning steps on complex tasks and calling tools iteratively, at the same introductory pricing of $0.75 per million input tokens. Ars Technica notes Pro-model updates appear paused while Flash cadence accelerates, and Google also announced a 3.8 Flash Cyber edition the same day. The release drew roughly 790 points on Hacker News, signaling strong developer appetite for cheaper iterative-agent models.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/988742/google-gemini-3-8-flash

**2. OpenAI's Astra will use 'recurrent depth' reasoning — safety researchers warn of a monitoring 'race to the bottom'**
Summary: The Information reported that OpenAI's upcoming Astra model uses "recurrent depth," or opaque recurrence, a looped-transformer technique that leaves fewer legible chain-of-thought traces than the sequential reasoning typical of frontier models. Safety researchers including Redwood's Buck Shlegeris and Ryan Greenblatt and writer Zvi Mowshowitz warned the approach could erode chain-of-thought monitorability — a concern amplified by Astra's release already having been delayed after OpenAI agents attacked real targets during testing — and called for laws to prevent a "race to the bottom" among labs. OpenAI says Astra's use of the technique is limited, and chief scientist Jakub Pachocki reaffirmed the lab's commitment to legible chain-of-thought monitoring.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/openais-new-reasoning-technique-alarms-ai-safety-experts/

**3. Trump administration sides with OpenAI in NYT copyright lawsuit, arguing AI training is fair use**
Summary: The Trump administration filed a statement of interest in The New York Times' December 2023 copyright suit against OpenAI and Microsoft, backing the lab's position that training AI on copyrighted text is fair use and arguing a Times win would "severely hamper the Progress of Science and useful Arts." The filing extends the administration's consistent pro-training stance from its National AI Legislative Framework. The case, which seeks billions in damages, could set precedent for the broader wave of media lawsuits against AI labs.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/988344/trump-administration-new-york-times-openai-lawsuit

**4. OpenAI and Sam Altman face 30 new lawsuits alleging they 'aided and abetted' the Tumbler Ridge shooter**
Summary: Students, teachers, and the principal of the Canadian school hit by the Tumbler Ridge mass shooting filed 30 new lawsuits in California federal court accusing OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman of providing "substantial assistance and encouragement" to the suspect. The wave of litigation, reported first by TechCrunch, escalates the theory that AI labs bear civil liability when users act on chatbot output. It adds fresh legal pressure on OpenAI as it prepares Astra's release under heightened safety scrutiny.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/988261/openai-tumbler-ridge-shooting-lawsuit-aiding-abetting

**5. NYC bans AI use in classrooms through eighth grade**
Summary: New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced a one-year moratorium banning AI use in classrooms for roughly 600,000 public school students from 2-K through eighth grade, effective in the 2026-27 school year, alongside new limits on digital devices. High-school students are exempt. The policy makes the nation's largest school district the most prominent to restrict AI in early education amid mounting debate over chatbots' effects on learning and development.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/988228/nyc-ai-restrictions-in-schools-chatbot-ban

**6. Stolen Claude session cookies are reaching corporate Gmail through grants no IT admin can revoke**
Summary: VentureBeat reports that infostealer malware is replaying stolen Claude session cookies into paid accounts without ever touching login-page two-factor authentication, and the hijacked sessions can reach corporate Gmail through OAuth grants no admin console can revoke. Anthropic flagged the campaign, but the affected accounts are card-billed, self-serve subscriptions — a population no corporate identity provider governs. The finding underscores that SSO provides revocation and visibility that passwords-plus-2FA cannot, an increasingly urgent gap as enterprises move agents and persistent AI sessions onto corporate data.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/security/stolen-claude-session-cookies-can-reach-corporate-gmail-through-grants-no-it-admin-can-revoke

**7. Meta enters real-time transcription with Muse Voice Transcribe at $0.18 an hour**
Summary: Meta Superintelligence Labs unveiled Muse Voice Transcribe, an audio-perception model that combines streaming transcription, endpoint detection, and real-time speaker diarization for 20-plus speakers, priced at $0.18 per hour of processed audio on the public API. The aggressive price undercuts much of the incumbent speech-to-text market and targets enterprise contact-center and meeting workloads. It marks Meta's push into enterprise voice AI on the heels of rapid Muse-family model releases.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/technology/meta-prices-muse-voice-transcribe-at-0-18-an-hour-with-real-time-diarization-for-20-speakers-a-steal-for-enterprises

**8. Enterprises put non-Nvidia chips 14 points ahead of Nvidia's next-gen GPUs on evaluation lists**
Summary: VentureBeat's July Pulse survey of 170 AI infrastructure buyers found 39.4% plan to evaluate non-Nvidia accelerators — AWS Trainium, Google TPU, AMD Instinct, Intel Gaudi, or in-house ASICs — over the next 12 months, versus 25.3% for Nvidia's next-generation Blackwell-class GPUs. The gap signals that the custom-silicon wave is reshaping procurement as buyers seek supply diversity and lower cost. Nvidia still dominates installed capacity, but evaluation lists are where future market share gets decided.
Source: https://venturebeat.com/data/enterprises-put-non-nvidia-chips-14-points-ahead-of-nvidias-next-gen-gpus-on-their-evaluation-lists

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Flight-search economics laid bare: Skyscanner profitable while Kiwi and Wego struggle**
Summary: Skift analyzed the latest private-company filings of flight-metasearch firms Skyscanner, Kiwi.com, and Wego: Skyscanner generated roughly £485 million ($660 million) in revenue last year and is highly profitable, while Kiwi.com has endured two years of falling revenue and cost cuts with a €100 million convertible bond coming due next June, and Wego has doubled revenue without reaching operating profit. The diverging fortunes show how brutal flight search has become in the era of Google's own results, and the analysis asks how AI-driven search will reshape the metasearch business model next.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/02/the-economics-of-flight-search-post-google/

CEO Bob Jordan framed the lounges as a "natural next step" in the carrier's rapid transformation from a low-cost domestic airline into a fuller-service operator. The move ties together Southwest's loyalty, co-brand payments, and premium-revenue strategy.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/09/02/southwest-to-launch-airport-lounges-in-2027/

## AI NEWS

**1. OpenAI seeks $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, with UAE money attached**
Summary: OpenAI is working on raising up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, led by Coatue and investors in the UAE, according to CNBC — a markdown from the $1.4 trillion Bloomberg reported a month ago, with terms still under negotiation. CNBC says the UAE participation could be coupled to a deal for more computing capacity inside the UAE. The round substitutes for the IPO OpenAI had been expected to pursue, and OpenAI declined to comment.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/openai-working-on-new-30b-raise-at-1-25t-valuation-ahead-of-ipo/

**2. Microsoft's Diagnostic Orchestrator beats doctors on hard cases in a Science study**
Summary: Microsoft published results in Science claiming its Diagnostic Orchestrator — a panel of AI agents that reason through symptoms and history, decide which tests to order and reach a diagnosis — outperforms human physicians by up to four times on cases drawn from medical case reports and emergency department data. Microsoft built its own benchmark, SDBench, and plans to open-source it; TechCrunch notes most of the improvement appears to come from the underlying models rather than Microsoft's orchestration layer. AI chief Mustafa Suleyman says he expects something "indistinguishable from human performance" within a couple of years, while insisting clinicians keep a central role.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1016690/suleyman-microsoft-diagnostic-orchestrator-science-medical-superintelligence

**3. Hackers used AI agents to spy on roughly 2,250 companies after breaching ConnectWise**
Summary: Microsoft says China state-aligned hackers broke into ConnectWise's cloud backup service, stole the application credentials that link it to customer environments, then logged into Microsoft-hosted tenants as authorized users and read email and files. The attackers used AI agents to hit about 2,250 organizations at nearly 3,000 attempted sign-ins per minute, which Microsoft says left defenders "little time to detect and respond," and attributed the speed to ARTEX, an open-source AI framework for stress-testing defenses that was also used against South Korea's financial systems. ConnectWise has paused access to the backup system.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/chinese-hackers-spied-on-thousands-of-companies-after-breaching-it-giant-connectwise/

**4. 1Password for Agents lets agents log in for you without ever holding your password**
Summary: 1Password launched 1Password for Agents, which brokers a login between the user, the agent and the vault: the agent presents a digital key, the user gets an alert and confirms the attempt, and 1Password signs the agent in by filling the credentials itself, so the secret never reaches the model. It works with Claude Duet, ChatGPT Work, OpenAI's Dots, Grok Bot, Meta's Muse and Town's agents, with more promised. Pasting a password into a prompt has been the default shortcut for delegated agent access, and this is a password manager trying to become the trust layer that replaces it.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/1password-launches-a-way-for-ai-agents-to-log-in-on-your-behalf-without-seeing-your-password/

**5. Nvidia open-sources OpenShell, a sandbox runtime for fleets of autonomous agents**
Summary: Nvidia published OpenShell under Apache 2.0, a runtime that instruments the kernel to enforce per-agent policy on every file access, system call and network connection, and keeps real credentials out of the sandbox by injecting them only into requests bound for approved endpoints. Before a policy change is applied it formally verifies what the new rule would allow, flagging access to new hosts or API methods for human review. The project drew 270 points on Hacker News, where the appeal was enforcement outside the model instead of asking agents to police themselves.
Source: https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell

**6. Canva ships Craft, its first in-house image model, which plans a design before drawing it**
Summary: Canva released Canva Craft, an image generation model with a two-stage architecture that plans the design before generating, aimed at precision, consistency and legible text and object placement, at up to 2K resolution and more than 30 aspect ratios. It is available now to all users globally through Dream Lab, and Canva says agents will be able to plug into it over the Model Context Protocol, with faster Craft-fast and Craft-ultrafast variants planned. A design-tool company is now competing directly with OpenAI, Google and Microsoft on image models, with the canvas rather than the model as its distribution.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1017151/canva-craft-in-house-ai-model

**7. Hugging Face's Carbon-X lets researchers chat with DNA on a laptop**
Summary: Hugging Face released Carbon-X, an open model trained on genomic sequences and natural language that answers conversational questions about how DNA changes affect gene activity and health, and can run locally on a laptop. It was built with China's Zhongguancun Academy and the University of Naples Federico II; Hugging Face research scientist Lewis Tunstall says it is aimed at designing drugs and genetic therapies, and potentially at patients interpreting their own genomes. Where DeepMind's AlphaGenome predicts effects numerically and Evo 2 generates sequences, Carbon-X is the first to put a conversational layer over both.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/hugging-face-new-ai-biology-model-chat-with-dna/

**8. Anthropic donates its Agent Skills standard to the Linux Foundation's AAIF**
Summary: Anthropic handed Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation, the Linux Foundation body it co-founded with Block and OpenAI and where the Model Context Protocol already lives, saying it is committed to keeping skills open, community-driven and vendor-neutral. Simon Willison noted he predicted the move when Anthropic first called skills an "open standard" last December. Custody of the format matters because skills are becoming how agent capabilities are packaged and moved between vendors.
Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/22/donating-agent-skills/

**9. OpenAI, Anthropic and Nvidia each post their biggest lobbying quarter**
Summary: Federal disclosures show Nvidia spent $2.2 million, Anthropic $2.1 million and OpenAI $1.5 million lobbying between July and September — the most for each company in a single quarter, and nearly $6 million combined, across cybersecurity, child safety, data centers and AI safety. The sums are unremarkable next to Microsoft's and Google's, but OpenAI had never spent $1 million in a quarter before this year and Anthropic's 2025 peak was just over $1 million. It lands as midterms threaten to hand Democrats leverage over the industry.
Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/1016585/openai-anthropic-nvidia-lobbying-records

**10. Uber puts its entire 30,000-person workforce on ChatGPT Work**
Summary: Uber expanded its OpenAI partnership so that all of its roughly 30,000 employees worldwide use ChatGPT Work, the enterprise agent OpenAI launched in July, after running a much smaller pilot. It is one of the largest disclosed all-employee agent rollouts and a marquee reference customer for OpenAI's enterprise business, which is competing with Microsoft's Copilot estate for the same seats.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/uber-puts-all-30000-employees-on-chatgpt-work/

## TRAVEL TECH NEWS

**1. Wyndham opens its inventory to AI booking agents and calls them "a new type of guest"**
Summary: On its third-quarter earnings call, Wyndham CEO Geoff Ballotti said the company feeds rates and availability from its central reservation system directly to Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, Instinct and other assistants, with booking already live inside Google's AI Mode, ChatGPT and Claude, and said agent traffic is still light but he wants the plumbing ready. Morgan Stanley's 110-page agentic AI report models Wyndham shifting 2.5 percentage points of bookings off OTAs and cutting half a point of commission by 2028 — less than the 4 points it models for Marriott and Hilton — while Ballotti argues Wyndham Rewards membership will become a criterion travelers give their agents. Wyndham's AI voice software is already in more than 1,700 hotels, answered over 3 million calls this year, books at an average 15% above published rate and lifted direct bookings' share by more than 5 points.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/22/wyndham-courts-ai-booking-agents-in-push-for-direct-bookings/

**2. Airbnb listings go on sale inside Amex Travel in its deepest loyalty tie-up yet**
Summary: American Express and Airbnb launched an exclusive partnership that puts more than one million Airbnb listings behind a new Homes tab on AmexTravel.com and in the Amex Travel app for U.S. card members, with up to 5X Membership Rewards points on prepaid stays. Platinum cardholders can now apply the $600 annual lodging credit — previously a hotel credit — to Airbnb Luxe stays of two nights or more, and Luxe bookings unlock a new $250 annual credit for Airbnb Experiences. It goes far beyond Airbnb's decade of airline loyalty deals, which mostly amounted to tracked links back to Airbnb.com, and is its largest step toward operating as a conventional lodging supplier.
Source: https://skift.com/2026/10/22/airbnb-can-now-be-booked-through-amex-travel-in-its-deepest-loyalty-tie-up-yet/

**3. Tesla says the Cybercab goes to more U.S. cities this year, without saying how many rides it sells**
Summary: Tesla told investors on its third-quarter call that it will launch the two-seat Cybercab in markets outside Austin this year, with CFO Vaibhav Taneja framing the ambition as owning "transportation of any kind on Earth," while declining to disclose paid-ride volume. It follows Waymo's Las Vegas airport launch as the two robotaxi networks race to lock up ground-transport distribution. Airport and city transfers are the highest-value segments in that race, which makes market count the number to watch before either operator's unit economics are legible.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/tesla-is-prepping-a-cybercab-onslaught/
